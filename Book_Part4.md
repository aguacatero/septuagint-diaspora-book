--- Evidence / Durability Chunk: Part 4 ---
This part tests the narrow claim that a small set of Aramaic or Hebrew-looking forms in the Gospels does not by itself establish a Hebrew or Aramaic written source behind the Greek Gospels.

Primary text references: Mark 5:41 (τάληθα κούμ, with the evangelist's Greek gloss); Mark 7:34 (ἐφφαθά, with an immediate Greek explanation); Matthew 27:46 and parallels (ἐλι ἐλι / lema sabachthani); Luke 23:46 (a Greek rendering of the psalmic cry, useful as a control case). The exact wording and punctuation should be checked against the critical edition used for the manuscript study, preferably NA28/UBS5 for the Greek New Testament, and the relevant Hebrew/Aramaic forms should be compared with the Hebrew Bible editions used in the project.

Proof / counter / counter-counter:
- Proof: the Greek evangelists preserve short foreign-language forms and then translate or explain them for a Greek-speaking audience. That pattern is compatible with a literary author shaping material for readers who need the meaning supplied in Greek.
- Counter: the forms could preserve authentic words or memories of Jesus, transmitted orally before being written in Greek. This is a live possibility, especially for a small number of sayings.
- Counter-counter: the existence of a plausible oral tradition does not prove a written Hebrew or Aramaic gospel source. The argument should therefore be modest: these forms are evidence about narrative presentation and audience orientation, not decisive evidence for a lost Semitic document.

Bracketed — mythic-parallel only, not factual: The Aramaic/Hebrew traces should not be folded into the speculative mythic-parallel appendix. They belong to the textual-linguistic argument as a separate, testable question.

Confidence: medium for the limited literary-audience claim; low for any stronger claim that the forms prove or disprove a written Semitic source. Falsifier: a securely dated, early Semitic manuscript preserving a coherent narrative tradition that materially changes the Greek text would require a major revision of this part's claim.

The conclusion should distinguish three levels of claim. Primary text references: revisit the selected citations in Mark, Matthew, Luke, and Paul, and record the LXX and MT/Hebrew readings side by side. Do not convert a plausible alignment into a certainty without checking the textual witnesses.

Proof / counter / counter-counter:

- Counter: an author could know Hebrew, use oral tradition, or draw from multiple scriptural recensions.
