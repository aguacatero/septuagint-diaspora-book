# Shaped by the Septuagint — Project Constitution

## Working Title

**Shaped by the Septuagint: How Greek Scripture Shaped the Gospels—and What History Remains**

## Purpose

This is a serious crossover book: readable by educated non-specialists, but constructed to withstand specialist scrutiny.

The project investigates whether the canonical Gospel authors primarily composed from Greek Jewish Scripture rather than by direct consultation of Hebrew Scripture; how that scriptural environment contributed to the construction of Gospel narratives; how those narratives also participated in the broader Hellenistic and Greco-Roman literary world; and, after those literary layers are identified, what evidence remains for a historical Jesus.

The historical conclusion is not predetermined.

## Core Hypothesis

The principal hypothesis is not that the Gospel authors were incapable of reading Hebrew.

The testable claim is that **direct consultation of Hebrew Scripture is not necessary to explain the surviving Gospel texts and may be positively unsupported in many or most cases, whereas Greek Jewish scriptural traditions function as the demonstrable textual environment from which the authors composed.**

Hebrew competence and Hebrew compositional use are separate questions.

The project evaluates each author separately and places a high evidentiary burden on claims of direct Hebrew consultation.

## Primary Corpus

The primary corpus is:

- Mark
- Matthew
- Luke–Acts
- John
- the undisputed Pauline letters

Markan priority serves as the operational baseline for Synoptic analysis without making the book dependent on Q or on any single complete solution to the Synoptic Problem.

Literary dependence is assessed claim-by-claim. Repetition across dependent texts does not automatically constitute independent attestation.

Acts may illuminate the authorial practices of Luke–Acts but does not automatically provide independent historical attestation for material appearing in Luke.

## Control Corpora

- **Philo** serves as a Hellenistic Jewish Greek-scriptural control.
- **Hebrews** serves as an early Christian Greek-scriptural control.
- **Dead Sea Scrolls, Old Greek witnesses, kaige and other Greek revisions, and other relevant Hebrew and Greek textual witnesses** serve as controls for first-century textual pluriformity.

Controls are never silently counted as evidence from the primary corpus.

## Textual Method

The old binary **“LXX vs. MT”** framework is retired.

The investigation asks which textual tradition best explains each New Testament use, considering where relevant:

- Old Greek;
- other Jewish Greek forms or revisions;
- Hebrew textual witnesses including DSS material;
- MT-type traditions;
- authorial adaptation;
- oral or liturgical transmission;
- unknown or lost textual forms;
- indeterminate cases.

“Masoretic Text” is not treated automatically as identical with the Hebrew text available to a first-century author.

Likewise, **“mistranslation” is a conclusion that must be demonstrated, not a starting category.**

## Evidence Registry

The GitHub repository is the scholarly apparatus of the project.

Every major datum receives a stable evidence ID and enters a master registry connected to four analytical modules:

- **TXT — Textual tradition.** Which scriptural textual tradition best explains the wording?
- **NAR — Narrative generation.** What role did Scripture play in producing the narrative?
- **CUL — Cultural/literary comparison.** How does the material relate to the wider Hellenistic and Greco-Roman narrative environment?
- **HIS — Historical residue.** After literary dependencies are accounted for, what historical proposition remains evidentially supported?

The registry should be available in both human-readable Markdown and machine-readable structured formats such as CSV or JSON.

The argumentative chapters are written from the completed evidence registry rather than the registry being constructed to defend already-written chapters.

## Textual Evidence Categories

Explicit and identifiable scriptural quotations receive a complete census.

Allusions and echoes are maintained in a separate registry and never inflate quotation statistics.

Allusions may enter narrative analysis only when they meet stronger specificity and density requirements.

Textual source classifications use ordered categories rather than artificial probabilities. Confidence is recorded separately.

A finding might therefore read:

- **Classification:** Old Greek strongly preferred
- **Confidence:** High

or:

- **Classification:** Direct Hebrew consultation plausible
- **Confidence:** Low

## Direct Hebrew Consultation

Agreement with Hebrew against the reconstructed Old Greek is not by itself evidence that an evangelist read Hebrew.

A strong case for direct Hebrew consultation requires converging evidence, preferably including:

1. poor explanation from known Greek textual traditions;
2. agreement with an attested Hebrew form;
3. an argument or literary feature depending specifically upon Hebrew wording, morphology, ambiguity, or wordplay;
4. repeated behavior by the same author sufficient to make coincidence, oral transmission, or an unknown Greek intermediary increasingly implausible.

The investigation therefore tests Hebrew use rather than assuming either Hebrew literacy or Hebrew illiteracy.

## Negative Evidence and Controls

The complete defined corpus is analyzed.

Passages that contradict the working hypothesis remain in the registry with equal visibility.

Hebrew-aligned readings, other Greek revisions, authorial paraphrases, mixed cases, and indeterminate cases are findings rather than problems to remove.

A case such as John 19:37 should therefore function as a serious counterexample requiring explanation rather than being relabeled as supporting evidence.

This replaces selective proof-texting with a corpus-level test.

## Narrative Generation

Scriptural correspondence does not automatically imply invention.

Each relevant narrative is tested against three competing causal models:

1. historical event followed by scriptural interpretation;
2. Scripture generating the narrative;
3. an earlier tradition substantially reshaped through scriptural construction.

The NAR module uses the following causal ladder:

- **Scriptural coloring** — biblical wording or imagery overlays an otherwise intelligible narrative.
- **Scriptural shaping** — Scripture materially determines how a tradition is narrated.
- **Co-construction** — some tradition may precede the author, while Scripture generates important narrative particulars.
- **Probable narrative generation** — the narrative or its defining particulars are better explained as arising from the scriptural text than from independently evidenced events.

Literary construction changes a narrative's historical evidentiary value but does not automatically prove that no underlying event occurred.

## Hellenistic and Greco-Roman Comparison

The Hellenistic component remains an integral part of the book.

It does not begin with the assumption that Gospel stories were copied from pagan myths.

Every cultural comparison must survive controls for:

- chronology;
- accessibility;
- specificity;
- density of parallels;
- order and structure;
- Jewish-scriptural alternatives;
- ordinary genre conventions;
- significant differences;
- a plausible model of transmission.

Each comparison receives one of four classifications:

1. **Direct dependence**
2. **Probable cultural influence**
3. **Shared Mediterranean convention**
4. **Weak parallel**

The term **“borrowed”** is reserved for direct dependence or probable cultural influence.

Weak parallels are allowed to fail and should not be retained merely because they support the desired thesis.

## Historical Evidence Audit

The historical-Jesus question is methodologically bracketed during textual and literary analysis and deliberately reopened afterward.

The HIS audit begins from **zero assumed biography**.

Individual propositions must independently earn evidentiary standing, including claims such as existence, Jewish identity, association with James or Cephas, crucifixion, Pilate, Nazareth, disciples, kingdom preaching, and other biographical details.

One accepted proposition does not automatically establish the rest.

Evidence is evaluated according to:

- chronology;
- textual integrity;
- genuine independence;
- proximity to the claimed events;
- dependence on Christian proclamation;
- identifiable source relationships;
- explanatory alternatives.

Paul, Mark, later Gospel traditions, Josephus, Tacitus, patristic witnesses, and other evidence therefore receive different evidentiary treatment rather than being counted as interchangeable attestations.

The book does not rely mechanically on older authenticity criteria such as embarrassment or simple multiple attestation.

## Permitted Historical Conclusions

The final evidence audit is allowed to reach whichever explanatory model best fits the accumulated evidence.

Possible outcomes include:

- a recoverable historical individual with some surviving biography;
- an extremely minimal individual surrounded by extensive literary construction;
- genuinely indeterminate evidence;
- a literary/nonhistorical-origin model.

No one result is protected in advance.

The method determines the conclusion.

## Book Architecture

### Part I — The Test

Establish the hypothesis, textual pluriformity, definitions, evidence registry, confidence language, controls, and falsification rules.

### Part II — Before the Gospels

Reconstruct the Greek-speaking Jewish scriptural environment, introduce control corpora, and examine undisputed Paul as the earliest surviving Christian authorial evidence in the primary corpus.

### Part III — The Evangelists

Audit Mark, Matthew, Luke–Acts, and John independently through the TXT framework, including evidence both for and against Greek-scriptural dominance and direct Hebrew consultation.

### Part IV — Scripture Becomes Story

Apply the NAR causal ladder to the strongest narrative-construction cases.

### Part V — The Hellenistic Narrative World

Apply the CUL controls to Gospel material only after the scriptural analysis has been completed.

### Part VI — What Is Left?

Perform the HIS audit and ask which model of the historical Jesus best explains the remaining evidence.

Appendices and the public repository contain the complete evidence registry, methodological definitions, counterexamples, textual collations, and reproducible research material.

## Research Order

The methodology is frozen before argumentative prose is rewritten.

1. Define the corpus and registry schema.
2. Classify the complete quotation corpus and relevant controls.
3. Conduct the narrative-generation analysis.
4. Conduct the Hellenistic comparative analysis.
5. Conduct the independent historical-residue audit.
6. Only afterward produce the final argumentative manuscript from those results.

## Governing Principle

The project must be capable of producing conclusions contrary to its initiating hypothesis.

If Hebrew consultation proves necessary for an author, record it.

If a proposed Greek-scriptural dependence disappears under textual criticism, discard it.

If a Hellenistic parallel fails its controls, remove the claim.

If substantial historical evidence survives the literary audit, acknowledge it.

If almost none survives, acknowledge that instead.

The aim of the project is not to prove that the Gospels are Greek literary inventions.

The aim is to determine, as rigorously as the surviving evidence permits, **what textual and cultural processes produced the canonical narratives—and what, if anything, remains historically recoverable after those processes are accounted for.**
