## What this is about

This project builds a peer-review-grade book arguing that the New Testament — especially Mark, Matthew, Luke, and John — was shaped primarily by the **Greek Septuagint (LXX)** rather than direct Hebrew scriptural knowledge, operating in a **Greek-speaking Jewish diaspora** with limited Hebrew literacy, with speculative components (mythic parallels) clearly marked.

1. **Solid core (testable):** Systematic LXX alignment in NT citations; diaspora sociolinguistic evidence (Greek inscriptions, papyri, synagogue records); earliest Christian sources (Pliny 112 CE, Ignatius, Acts) confirming Greek-language, LXX-based communities.
2. **Speculative / to-test component:** Mythic parallels (Osiris, Asclepius, Perseus, Heracles, mystery cult motifs) suggesting the NT story was partly shaped by Hellenistic mythic archetypes. This component is explicitly marked speculative and open to further empirical testing.

The goal: a defensible, source-backed argument that forces mainstream scholarship to confront the LXX-dependence evidence — without inventing unsupported historical claims.

## How the outline strengthens the argument

For each chapter, `Book_Scholar_Outline.md` includes:

- **Proof:** Specific textual or historical evidence (e.g., Isaiah 7:14 LXX vs MT mismatch shaping Matthew's virgin birth narrative)
- **Counter-argument:** Mainstream scholarly objection (e.g., authors could have read both Hebrew and Greek)
- **Counter-counter-argument:** Response showing why LXX alignment dominates (e.g., consistent LXX citation patterns across Mark, Paul, and earliest Christian manuscripts)

The outline also provides:
- A **reviewer critique + response framework** (6 common objections with structured rebuttals)
- A **predictive framework** (5 predictions — LXX-only manuscripts, no Hebrew gospel, deeper mythic parallels, sociolinguistic reinforcement, redactional layers — all testable)
- A **methodological appendix** (computational stylometry, open-access sociolinguistic database, peer-review protocol)
