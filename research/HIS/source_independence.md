# Source Independence Rules

Historical independence is evaluated **claim-by-claim**.

A source is not globally labeled independent or dependent for every proposition it contains.

## Required questions

For each historical claim:

1. Is the source chronologically earlier than other surviving witnesses?
2. Is there evidence of literary dependence on another surviving source for this specific claim?
3. Could the claim derive from shared Christian proclamation rather than independent memory?
4. Is the source preserving an inherited formula or creed whose origin can be dated separately?
5. Does a later author plausibly know an earlier Christian text or tradition containing the same claim?
6. Does the textual integrity of the passage itself remain contested?

## Synoptic rule

Material repeated by Matthew or Luke from Mark does not count as additional independent attestation merely because it appears in another canonical Gospel.

## John rule

John is not assigned blanket independence or dependence. Individual Johannine traditions must be evaluated on their own source relationships.

## External-source rule

Josephus, Tacitus, Pliny, patristic authors, and other external witnesses are evaluated separately for chronology, textual integrity, access to Christian proclamation, and likely information source. Non-Christian authorship alone does not prove informational independence.
