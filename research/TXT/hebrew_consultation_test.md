# Direct Hebrew Consultation Test

The project distinguishes **knowledge of Hebrew** from **demonstrable compositional use of Hebrew Scripture**.

A claim of direct Hebrew consultation should become strong only when several lines of evidence converge.

## Required converging indicators

Preferably all of the following should be addressed:

1. **Greek inadequacy** — known or plausible Greek textual traditions do not satisfactorily explain the NT reading.
2. **Hebrew alignment** — the NT reading agrees meaningfully with an attested Hebrew form.
3. **Hebrew-specific leverage** — the author's literary or theological move depends on something specifically visible in Hebrew, such as wording, morphology, consonantal ambiguity, syntax, or wordplay that is not preserved in the relevant Greek forms.
4. **Pattern within the author** — similar behavior occurs repeatedly enough to make coincidence, isolated oral transmission, or a lost/unknown Greek intermediary progressively less plausible.

## What does not count by itself

- Agreement with MT against reconstructed Old Greek.
- Presence of Aramaic or Hebrew words that are immediately translated into Greek.
- A Semitic-sounding syntax without a defined comparison set.
- A theological concept with Hebrew/Jewish roots.

## Canonical counterexample test

**John 19:37 / Zechariah 12:10** must be analyzed as a serious Hebrew-like counterexample to simple Old Greek dependence. It should not be relabeled as supporting Old Greek merely to preserve the hypothesis.

The correct question is whether John's wording requires direct Hebrew consultation or is better explained by another Greek textual tradition, revision, inherited quotation form, or authorial adaptation.
