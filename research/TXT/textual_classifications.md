# TXT Classification Vocabulary

Use one primary classification per reviewed textual evidence record.

## Classifications

1. **Old Greek strongly preferred** — the NT wording is best explained by an Old Greek form over known Hebrew witnesses and other alternatives.
2. **Other Greek tradition preferred** — the wording is better explained by another Jewish Greek revision or textual form than by Old Greek.
3. **Hebrew-direct plausible** — direct consultation of Hebrew is a live explanation that survives the project's high-bar test, but is not yet established as necessary.
4. **Hebrew-direct strongly indicated** — direct consultation of Hebrew is the best explanation after known/credible Greek intermediaries and other alternatives have been tested and found inadequate.
5. **Authorial adaptation** — the author appears to reshape a scriptural form in a way that prevents confident identification of a specific textual tradition.
6. **Mixed / composite** — more than one textual stream or compositional process plausibly contributes to the wording.
7. **Indeterminate** — surviving evidence does not permit a responsible textual-source classification.

## Confidence

Record confidence separately:

- Very High
- High
- Moderate
- Low
- Very Low

Do not create numeric probabilities from these labels.

## Important prohibition

Do not classify a reading as direct Hebrew consultation merely because it agrees with an MT-type form against reconstructed Old Greek. The possibility of another Greek tradition, a Hebraizing revision, a different Hebrew Vorlage behind a Greek form, oral/liturgical transmission, authorial adaptation, or an unknown Greek intermediary must be evaluated first.
