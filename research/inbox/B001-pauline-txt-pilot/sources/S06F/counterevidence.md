### 2. B001-2: Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 [LXX 2:25]
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 104 [PDF p. 10]; Section 3
- **Authorial Adaptation / Contextual Reinterpretation:** Ryšková notes that Paul takes up Hosea’s words "my people" and "not my people" (οὐ λαός μου), which originally referred to Israel, and adapts them to apply directly to Gentile inclusion (p. 104).
- **Evaluates Other Scholars:** On page 104 (fn. 31, 33), Ryšková evaluates Shiu-Lun Shum's proposals regarding whether Paul quoted by heart or altered wording to avoid confusion between Hosea's and Isaiah's distinct uses of λαός (citing Shum 2002, pp. 207, 211).

### 3. B001-3: Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 [LXX 2:1]
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 104 [PDF p. 10]; Section 3
- **Authorial Adaptation:** Ryšková notes that Paul joins Hosea 1:10 with Hosea 2:23 in Romans 9:25–26 to apply the prophetic promise of divine sonship (υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος) to Gentile believers as introductory context to his Isaiah quotations (p. 104).

### 4. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16 (and Romans 9:33)
- **Source Locators:** Printed pp. 96–97 [PDF pp. 2–3]; p. 105 [PDF p. 11]; p. 106 [PDF p. 12]
- **Discusses Competing Textual Forms:**
  - On printed p. 106, Ryšková notes that Paul quotes directly from LXX Isaiah 28:16, which differs from the Hebrew MT (הַמַּאֲמִין לֹαֹ יָחִישׁ).
  - On printed p. 105, she analyzes the composite text of Romans 9:33 that conflates Isaiah 28:16 and Isaiah 8:14 around the common theme of "stone," comparing it to 1 Peter 2:6–8.
- **Allows Authorial Adaptation / Intentional Insertion:**
  - On printed p. 106, Ryšková points out that Paul adds the pronoun πᾶς ("no one / everyone") to Isaiah 28:16 in Romans 10:11—a word missing both from LXX Isaiah 28:16 and from Paul's earlier citation in Romans 9:33—to emphasize the universal availability of salvation through faith in Christ.
- **Expresses Uncertainty / Ambiguity:**
  - On printed p. 105 (fn. 37), Ryšková notes it is not completely clear whether the original "stone" in Isaiah 28:16 referred to God or to the foundation of the Jerusalem temple (citing Shum 2002, p. 221).
- **Evaluates Other Scholars (Contested Composite Origin):**
  - On printed p. 105 (fn. 35–36), Ryšková presents the immediate origin of the composite text in Rom 9:33 as an open scholarly debate: Dietrich-Alex Koch (oral) and C. H. Dodd (written) argue it stems from pre-Pauline testimonia, whereas Shiu-Lun Shum asserts Paul compiled the passages directly from an Isaiah scroll in Corinth.
- **Allows Quotation from Memory / Differing Vorlage:**
  - On printed pp. 96–97, Ryšková reviews broader scholarship (Stanley, Ellis, Wagner, Whitlock, Tiwald, Wilk) on whether Paul quoted from memory, used excerpt collections, or accessed revised Greek texts.

### 5. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 [LXX 3:5]
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]; Section 6
- **Authorial Adaptation / Unmarked Citation:** Ryšková notes that Paul pairs Isaiah 28:16 in Romans 10:11 with an unmarked quotation of Joel 2:32 in Romans 10:13, using the catchword connection πᾶς to unite faith and salvation while applying Old Testament divine name statements directly to Jesus as the resurrected Lord.

---

### 6. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]; Section 12
- **Allows Authorial Adaptation / Disjunctive Quotation Technique:**
  - Ryšková observes that Paul quotes almost word-for-word from LXX Isaiah 40:13 in Romans 11:34 without an introductory formula, framing Paul's technique disjunctively: quoting either from memory or by drawing automatically on biblical idiom.
  - She highlights Paul's freedom of selective adaptation across epistles: "Elsewhere, in Romans 11:35, he omits the end of Isaiah 40:13; in 1 Corinthians 2:16, he leaves out the middle section" (p. 112).
- **Qualified Job Allusion:**
  - She expressly qualifies the connection to Job 41:11 (LXX 41:3) in Rom 11:35 as a *possible echo* rather than a certain quotation (p. 112).

---

### 7. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]; Section 14
- **Authorial Adaptation / Structural Ideological Shift:**
  - Ryšková highlights a discontinuity and ideological shift from general divine praise in the preceding Psalm and Deuteronomy citations (Rom 15:9–11) to the "root of Jesse" as the specific hope of the Gentiles in Isaiah 11:10 (Rom 15:12).
- **Disagrees with Another Scholar:**
  - On printed p. 114 (fn. 61), Ryšková contrasts her view with Florian Wilk (1998, p. 394): while Wilk plays down the importance of Rom 15:12, Ryšková emphasizes its positive motivational force for Gentile inclusion and community unity.
