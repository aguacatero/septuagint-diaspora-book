# S06F — Synthesis of Isaiah Quotations in Romans

Synthesis of Mireia Ryšková's explicit analysis in *The Reception of the Book of Isaiah in Paul's Letter to the Romans*, *AUC Theologica*, Vol. 9, No. 2 (2019), pp. 95–116, across the core B001 passages.

---

### 1. Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16 (and Romans 9:33)

- **Addition of πᾶς:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]; Section 6 (Rom 10:11)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Ryšková points out that Paul inserts πᾶς ("no one / everyone") to stress that salvation is open to all without distinction: *"The universal aspect of the statement is emphasised by the pronoun ‘no one’ (πᾶς), which is missing from the quotation in Romans 9:33."* (p. 106).
- **Comparison with MT and LXX:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]; Section 6 (Rom 10:11)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Ryšková notes that Paul relies on the Septuagint version rather than the Hebrew text: *"Quoting directly from the LXX (which differs from the MT), Paul returns to Isaiah 28:16 to drive home his point that salvation comes through faith in God’s redeeming work in Christ."* (p. 106).
- **Christological Application:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]; Section 6 (Rom 10:11)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Ryšková stresses that in Romans 10:11, Paul shifts the metaphor from judgment to resurrection and applies Old Testament monotheistic statements directly to Christ: *"The stone is no longer a judge but the resurrection of Jesus Christ and faith in that resurrection."* (p. 106). Relying on Shiu-Lun Shum (2002, p. 223, cited in fn. 40), she adds: *"Paul is applying Isaiah’s monotheistic statements both to God and to Christ, the Lord, who is co-sovereign and co-unique with God the Father."* (p. 106).
- **Origin of the Composite Stone Citation in Romans 9:33:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 105 [PDF p. 11]; Section 5 (Rom 9:33)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Ryšková observes that Romans 9:33 conflates Isaiah 28:16 and Isaiah 8:14 around the common theme of "stone," comparing it to 1 Peter 2:6–8: *"This verse is a composite of two passages from Isaiah with the common theme of ‘stone’; a similar passage can be found in 1 Peter 2:6–8."* (p. 105). She explicitly presents its immediate origin as contested: Dietrich-Alex Koch (oral) and C. H. Dodd (written) propose a pre-Pauline tradition, whereas Shiu-Lun Shum argues that Paul compiled the quotation directly from an Isaiah scroll in Corinth (p. 105, fn. 35–36).

### 2. Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13

- **Paul's Citation Form & Technique:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]; Section 12 (Rom 11:34–35)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Ryšková notes that Paul quotes the Septuagint text almost verbatim without a formal citation marker: *"Without pointing it out, Paul quotes here almost word for word from Isaiah 40:13 – with a possible echo of Job 41:11 (LXX 41:3) – in a hymn-like doxology that expresses the greatness of God and the unsearchable nature of his ways."* (p. 112). She frames his quotation method disjunctively: Paul was *"either quoting by heart from his great knowledge of Isaiah or using Isaiah’s language automatically, naturally."* (p. 112).
- **Comparison with Job 41:11 / Romans 11:35 & 1 Corinthians 2:16:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]; Section 12 (Rom 11:34–35)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Ryšková highlights Paul’s freedom to adapt scripture selectively across contexts: *"Elsewhere, in Romans 11:35, he omits the end of Isaiah 40:13; in 1 Corinthians 2:16, he leaves out the middle section."* (p. 112). She qualifies the Job 41:11 reference in Rom 11:35 as a *"possible echo"* rather than a certain quotation (p. 112).
- **Doxological Function:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]; Section 12 (Rom 11:34–35)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Ryšková emphasizes that the quotation completes Paul's reflection on salvation history (Rom 9–11) by affirming God's unsearchable sovereignty: *"Any possible discussion concerning Israel’s fate is then rounded off with the next quotation, which refers to the ungraspable nature of God’s sovereignty."* (p. 112, citing Wilk 1998, p. 392 in fn. 57).

### 3. Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10

- **Messianic Hope of the Gentiles:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]; Section 14 (Rom 15:12)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Ryšková notes that the quotation presents Christ as the universal messianic ruler who grants salvation to the nations: *"Jesus Christ is the promised shoot from the stump of Jesse, the Messiah, the sovereign Lord, the ruler over the nations and the hope of salvation for the Gentiles; he is the Christ who ‘did not please himself’ (verse 3), who unites all people."* (p. 114).
- **Comparison with LXX and MT:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]; Section 14 (Rom 15:12)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Introduced by the explicit formula Ἠσαΐας λέγει, the citation closely follows the Septuagint text (ἔσται ἡ ῥίζα τοῦ Ἰεσσαί...), culminating Paul's scriptural argument.
- **Role in the Climax of Romans 15:7–13:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]; Section 14 (Rom 15:12)
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Ryšková explains that Isaiah 11:10 forms the peak of Paul's four-text citation chain (Ps 18:49, Deut 32:43, Ps 117:1, Isa 11:10): *"There is something of a discontinuity – an ideological shift – between the quotations from Psalms/Deuteronomy and the verse from Isaiah. From explicit praise of the Lord, attention moves to the ‘root of Jesse’, who is the hope of the Gentiles."* (p. 114). Contrasting her reading with Florian Wilk (1998, p. 394), she emphasizes its positive motivational force for community harmony between the weak and strong, while preparing for Paul's mission to Spain (Rom 15:14–33).
