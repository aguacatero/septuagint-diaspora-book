# S06F — Isaiah In Romans Synthesis

Here is Mireia Ryšková’s explicit analysis of the specified passages in her article "The Reception of the Book of Isaiah in Paul’s Letter to the Romans" (2019).
1. Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16
Addition of πᾶς:
Printed Page Number: Page 104
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková points out that Paul inserts πᾶς ("no one / everyone") to stress that salvation is open to all without distinction. She observes: "The universal aspect of the statement is emphasised by the pronoun ‘no one’ (πᾶς), which is missing from the quotation in Romans 9:33"
1
.
Comparison with MT and LXX:
Printed Page Number: Page 104
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková notes that Paul relies on the Septuagint version rather than the Hebrew text: "Quoting directly from the LXX (which differs from the MT), Paul returns to Isaiah 28:16 to drive home his point that salvation comes through faith in God’s redeeming work in Christ"
1
.
Christological Application:
Printed Page Number: Page 104
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková stresses that in Romans 10:11, Paul shifts the metaphor from judgment to resurrection and applies Old Testament monotheistic statements directly to Christ: "The stone is no longer a judge but the resurrection of Jesus Christ and faith in that resurrection"
1
. She adds: "Paul is applying Isaiah’s monotheistic statements both to God and to Christ, the Lord, who is co-sovereign and co-unique with God the Father"
1
.
2. Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13
Paul's Citation Form:
Printed Page Number: Page 108
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková notes that Paul quotes the Septuagint text almost verbatim without a formal citation marker: "Without pointing it out, Paul quotes here almost word for word from Isaiah 40:13..."
1
. She explains that Paul was "either quoting by heart from his great knowledge of Isaiah or using Isaiah’s language automatically, naturally"
1
.
Comparison with Job 41:3 / Romans 11:35 & 1 Corinthians 2:16:
Printed Page Number: Page 108
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková highlights Paul’s freedom to adapt scripture across contexts: "Elsewhere, in Romans 11:35, he omits the end of Isaiah 40:13; in 1 Corinthians 2:16, he leaves out the middle section"
1
. She also identifies a "possible echo of Job 41:11 (LXX 41:3)" paired with Isaiah 40:13 in Romans 11:35
1
.
Doxological Function:
Printed Page Number: Page 108
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková emphasizes that the quotation completes Paul's reflection on salvation history by exalting God's unsearchable ways: "...in a hymn-like doxology that expresses the greatness of God and the unsearchable nature of his ways"
1
.
3. Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10
Messianic Hope of the Gentiles:
Printed Page Number: Page 110
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková notes that the quotation presents Christ as the universal ruler who grants salvation to the nations: "Jesus Christ is the promised shoot from the stump of Jesse, the Messiah, the sovereign Lord, the ruler over the nations and the hope of salvation for the Gentiles..."
1
.
Comparison with LXX and MT:
Printed Page Numbers: Pages 109–110
Analysis & Quote: Unlike the unmarked or composite citations elsewhere, this passage is introduced by an explicit reference (Ἠσαΐας λέγει) and follows the Septuagint text (ἔσται ἡ ῥίζα τοῦ Ἰεσσαί...)
1
.
Role in the Climax of Romans 15:7–13:
Printed Page Numbers: Pages 109–110
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková explains that Isaiah 11:10 forms the peak of Paul's four-text quotation chain (Ps 18:49, Deut 32:43, Ps 117:1, Isa 11:10): "The quotations from Scripture culminate in the quotation from Isaiah 11:10, which is introduced by an explicit reference to the prophet"
1
. She notes that it marks an ideological shift: "From explicit praise of the Lord, attention moves to the ‘root of Jesse’, who is the hope of the Gentiles. From a focus on the problem of the weak and the strong in the community, Paul moves on to praise God’s generosity in Christ..."
1
.
4. Relation to Other Old Testament Citations
Romans 9:25–26 (Hosea 2:23 & 1:10):
Printed Page Number: Page 101
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková explains that the Hosea quotation prepares for the Isaiah citations in Romans 9:27–29: "The quotation from Isaiah follows one from Hosea (Rom 9:25–26)... Hosea’s words ‘my people’ and ‘not my people’, originally intended to mean Israel, are taken up by Paul and applied to the Gentiles"
1
.
Romans 9:33 (Isaiah 28:16 & 8:14):
Printed Page Number: Page 103
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková analyzes Rom 9:33 as a composite "stone" text that sets up the contrast with Rom 10:11: "This verse is a composite of two passages from Isaiah with the common theme of ‘stone’... This stone is a judge, but for believers it also provides firm ground and certainty"
1
.
Romans 10:13 (Joel 2:32 / LXX 3:5):
Printed Page Number: Page 104
Analysis & Quote: Ryšková connects Rom 10:11 (Isa 28:16) with Rom 10:13 (Joel 2:32): "The verse from Isaiah corresponds to the unmarked quotation from Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5) in Romans 10:13. By repeating the reference to Isaiah 28:16... Paul is able to anchor in Scripture his fundamental criterion, which is faith (πίστις)"
1
