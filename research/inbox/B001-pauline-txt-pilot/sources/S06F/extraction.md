# S06F — Source-Bounded Scholarly Extraction

Itemized extraction of Mireia Ryšková's positions and claims across the substantive B001 items (and their immediate contextual citation units) in *The Reception of the Book of Isaiah in Paul's Letter to the Romans*, *AUC Theologica*, Vol. 9, No. 2 (2019), pp. 95–116.

---

### B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16 (and Romans 9:33)

#### S06F-E001
- **Proposed Evidence ID:** `S06F-E001`
- **B001 Passage ID:** `B001-4`
- **Claim Type:** `TEXTUAL_CRITICAL`
- **Author's Claim:** In Romans 10:11, Paul quotes directly from LXX Isaiah 28:16 and prefixes the pronoun πᾶς to emphasize the universal availability of salvation through faith in Christ.
- **Short Exact Quotation:** "The universal aspect of the statement is emphasised by the pronoun ‘no one’ (πᾶς), which is missing from the quotation in Romans 9:33."
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]; Section 6 (Rom 10:11)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** LXX rendering of Isaiah 28:16 vs. Hebrew MT; insertion of πᾶς in Rom 10:11 compared to Rom 9:33.
- **Qualifications / Conditions:** The pronoun πᾶς is absent from LXX Isaiah 28:16 and from Paul's earlier citation in Romans 9:33.
- **Extraction Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Harmonizes with surrounding future-tense framework and prepares for Joel quotation in Rom 10:13.

#### S06F-E002
- **Proposed Evidence ID:** `S06F-E002`
- **B001 Passage ID:** `B001-4`
- **Claim Type:** `EXEGETICAL`
- **Author's Claim:** In Romans 10:11, Paul shifts the metaphorical meaning of the "stone" in Isaiah 28:16 from a judge causing legalistic stumbling to the resurrection of Jesus Christ and faith in that resurrection.
- **Short Exact Quotation:** "The stone is no longer a judge but the resurrection of Jesus Christ and faith in that resurrection."
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]; Section 6 (Rom 10:11)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Re-contextualization of Isaiah 28:16 in Rom 10:11 following its composite use in Rom 9:33.
- **Qualifications / Conditions:** Distinguishes the dual stumbling/firmness aspect of Rom 9:33 from the unmixed universal promise of Rom 10:11.
- **Extraction Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Christological re-contextualization grounding justification by faith.

#### S06F-E003
- **Proposed Evidence ID:** `S06F-E003`
- **B001 Passage ID:** `B001-4`
- **Claim Type:** `THEOLOGICAL`
- **Author's Claim:** Paul applies Isaiah's monotheistic statements in Isaiah 28:16 to both God the Father and Christ the Lord, presenting Christ as co-sovereign and co-unique with the Father.
- **Short Exact Quotation:** "Paul is applying Isaiah’s monotheistic statements both to God and to Christ, the Lord, who is co-sovereign and co-unique with God the Father."
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]; Section 6 (Rom 10:11)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Parallel citations of Isaiah 28:16 in Rom 9:33 and 10:11.
- **Qualifications / Conditions:** Both citations in Rom 9:33 and 10:11 are introduced by general references to Scripture rather than naming Isaiah directly; formulation of Christ as co-sovereign and co-unique relies directly on Shiu-Lun Shum (2002, p. 223, cited in fn. 40).
- **Extraction Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Cites Shum (2002, 223) in fn. 40 on high Christology in Pauline Old Testament quotations.

#### S06F-E004
- **Proposed Evidence ID:** `S06F-E004`
- **B001 Passage ID:** `B001-4`
- **Claim Type:** `TEXTUAL_CRITICAL`
- **Author's Claim:** Romans 9:33 contains a composite quotation conflating Isaiah 28:16 and Isaiah 8:14; its immediate origin is contested in scholarship between a pre-Pauline oral/written composite tradition (Koch, Dodd) and Paul's direct compilation from an Isaiah scroll (Shum), functioning to show that Israel stumbled over Christ while faith yields certainty.
- **Short Exact Quotation:** "This verse is a composite of two passages from Isaiah with the common theme of ‘stone’; a similar passage can be found in 1 Peter 2:6–8."
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 105 [PDF p. 11]; Section 5 (Rom 9:33)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Conflation of Isa 28:16 and Isa 8:14 in Rom 9:33; comparison with 1 Pet 2:6–8.
- **Qualifications / Conditions:** Scholarly debate explicitly noted: Koch (oral) and Dodd (written) propose pre-Pauline tradition; Shum proposes direct Pauline compilation from an Isaiah scroll in Corinth. Ryšková presents the immediate origin as an open question.
- **Extraction Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Parallel conflation visible in 1 Peter 2:6–8; introduced with the general formula καθώς γέγραπται.

### B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 [LXX 3:5] (Contextual Unit to B001-4)

#### S06F-E005
- **Proposed Evidence ID:** `S06F-E005`
- **B001 Passage ID:** `B001-5`
- **Claim Type:** `EXEGETICAL`
- **Author's Claim:** The citation of Isaiah 28:16 in Romans 10:11 corresponds directly with the unmarked quotation of Joel 2:32 in Romans 10:13, united by πᾶς to establish faith as the universal criterion for salvation.
- **Short Exact Quotation:** "The verse from Isaiah corresponds to the unmarked quotation from Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5) in Romans 10:13."
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]; Section 6 (Rom 10:11)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Pairing of Rom 10:11 (Isa 28:16) and Rom 10:13 (Joel 3:5 LXX).
- **Qualifications / Conditions:** Joel 2:32 is cited without an introductory quotation formula, linked by catchword connection to Rom 10:11.
- **Extraction Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Quotes Wilk (1998, 390) on Paul's commitment to the validity of the gospel for both Jew and Gentile.

### B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13

#### S06F-E006
- **Proposed Evidence ID:** `S06F-E006`
- **B001 Passage ID:** `B001-6`
- **Claim Type:** `LITERARY`
- **Author's Claim:** In Romans 11:34, Paul quotes almost word-for-word from LXX Isaiah 40:13 without an introductory formula—accompanied by a possible echo of Job 41:11 (LXX 41:3) in Rom 11:35—either quoting from memory or drawing automatically on biblical idiom to express God's unsearchable sovereignty.
- **Short Exact Quotation:** "Without pointing it out, Paul quotes here almost word for word from Isaiah 40:13 – with a possible echo of Job 41:11 (LXX 41:3) – in a hymn-like doxology that expresses the greatness of God and the unsearchable nature of his ways."
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]; Section 12 (Rom 11:34–35)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Near-verbatim Greek agreement between Rom 11:34 and LXX Isa 40:13; pairing with Job 41:11 (LXX 41:3) in Rom 11:35.
- **Qualifications / Conditions:** Paul quotes either from memory or by drawing automatically on familiar biblical idiom without an explicit citation marker; the Job 41:11 (LXX 41:3) connection in Rom 11:35 is explicitly qualified as a possible echo rather than a certain quotation.
- **Extraction Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Rounds off the theological discussion of Israel's election in Romans 9–11.

#### S06F-E007
- **Proposed Evidence ID:** `S06F-E007`
- **B001 Passage ID:** `B001-6`
- **Claim Type:** `TEXTUAL_CRITICAL`
- **Author's Claim:** Paul adapts Isaiah 40:13 selectively according to his contextual purpose, omitting the end of the verse in Romans 11:35 while omitting its middle section in 1 Corinthians 2:16.
- **Short Exact Quotation:** "Elsewhere, in Romans 11:35, he omits the end of Isaiah 40:13; in 1 Corinthians 2:16, he leaves out the middle section."
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]; Section 12 (Rom 11:34–35)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Comparison of Rom 11:34–35 and 1 Cor 2:16 against LXX Isa 40:13.
- **Qualifications / Conditions:** Illustrates Paul's freedom of scriptural adaptation across differing epistolary contexts.
- **Extraction Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Direct cross-epistle witness to Pauline quotation technique.

### B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10

#### S06F-E008
- **Proposed Evidence ID:** `S06F-E008`
- **B001 Passage ID:** `B001-7`
- **Claim Type:** `EXEGETICAL`
- **Author's Claim:** In Romans 15:12, Paul places Isaiah 11:10 as the culmination of the citation chain in Romans 15:9–12, marking an ideological shift from general praise to the 'root of Jesse' as the hope of the Gentiles.
- **Short Exact Quotation:** "There is something of a discontinuity – an ideological shift – between the quotations from Psalms/Deuteronomy and the verse from Isaiah. From explicit praise of the Lord, attention moves to the ‘root of Jesse’, who is the hope of the Gentiles."
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]; Section 14 (Rom 15:12)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Citation chain of Ps 18:49, Deut 32:43, Ps 117:1, and Isa 11:10 in Rom 15:9–12; introductory formula Ἠσαΐας λέγει.
- **Qualifications / Conditions:** Shifts focus from the internal community tension of the weak and strong to God's universal generosity in Christ.
- **Extraction Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Introduced with the explicit formula Ἠσαΐας λέγει.

#### S06F-E009
- **Proposed Evidence ID:** `S06F-E009`
- **B001 Passage ID:** `B001-7`
- **Claim Type:** `CHRISTOLOGICAL`
- **Author's Claim:** Paul uses Isaiah 11:10 in Romans 15:12 to present Jesus Christ as the promised messianic ruler over the nations who unites Jewish and Gentile believers in shared praise.
- **Short Exact Quotation:** "Jesus Christ is the promised shoot from the stump of Jesse, the Messiah, the sovereign Lord, the ruler over the nations and the hope of salvation for the Gentiles; he is the Christ who ‘did not please himself’ (verse 3), who unites all people."
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]; Section 14 (Rom 15:12)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** LXX Isa 11:10 text cited in Rom 15:12 (ἔσται ἡ ῥίζα τοῦ Ἰεσσαί...).
- **Qualifications / Conditions:** Provides positive motivation for mutual acceptance while preparing for Paul's concluding travel plans (Rom 15:14–33).
- **Extraction Confidence:** `HIGH`
- **Notes:** Cites Wilk (1998, 394) in fn. 61.
