# S06F — Argument Map

Structural reasoning maps (Question $\rightarrow$ Evidence $\rightarrow$ Inference $\rightarrow$ Conclusion) for Mireia Ryšková, *The Reception of the Book of Isaiah in Paul's Letter to the Romans* (2019), across substantive B001 items.

---

### 1. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16 (and Romans 9:33)

#### Reasoning Map

- **QUESTION:** How does Paul adapt and re-contextualize Isaiah 28:16 in Romans 10:11 following its initial composite citation in Romans 9:33, and what role does it play in establishing justification by faith?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Romans 10:11, Paul quotes directly from LXX Isaiah 28:16 using the introductory formula λέγει γὰρ ἡ γραφή, prefixing the pronoun πᾶς ("no one / everyone"), which is missing both from the Septuagint original and from Paul's earlier quotation in Romans 9:33 (printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Romans 9:33, Paul presents a composite "stone" quotation conflating Isaiah 28:16 and Isaiah 8:14 introduced by καθὼς γέγραπται (printed p. 105 [PDF p. 11]).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Romans 10:13, Paul pairs the citation of Isaiah 28:16 with an unmarked quotation of Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5), linked by the shared term πᾶς (printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* By inserting πᾶς in Romans 10:11, Paul highlights the universal availability of salvation, shifting the metaphorical meaning of the "stone" from a judge causing legalistic Israel to stumble (as in Rom 9:33) to the resurrection of Jesus Christ and faith in that resurrection (printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]).
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul applies monotheistic Old Testament statements from Isaiah to both God the Father and Jesus Christ, presenting Christ as co-sovereign and co-unique with the Father (printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12], citing Shum 2002, p. 223).
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Quoting Isaiah 28:16 enables Paul to anchor in Scripture his fundamental criterion for salvation—faith (πίστις) in the resurrected Lord—which applies without distinction to both Jew and Gentile (printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]).

#### Analytical Breakdown

- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:**
  - Paul assumes that monotheistic Old Testament scriptures describing the Lord God can be applied directly to Jesus Christ as the resurrected Lord (printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]).
  - Paul assumes that faith in Christ's resurrection guarantees that the believer will never be put to shame (printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:**
  - Ryšková explicitly considers whether the composite quotation in Romans 9:33 (Isa 28:16 + 8:14) was derived from a pre-Pauline oral tradition (Koch) or written collection (Dodd), or compiled directly by Paul from an Isaiah scroll in Corinth (Shum) (printed p. 105 [PDF p. 11]).
  - She notes the ambiguity regarding whether the "stone" in original Isaiah 28:16 referred to God or to the foundation of the Jerusalem temple (printed p. 105 [PDF p. 11], citing Shum 2002, p. 221).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:**
  - Ryšková does not address whether the insertion of πᾶς in Romans 10:11 was derived from an unrecorded Hebraizing Greek manuscript variant of Isaiah rather than being Paul's own deliberate stylistic addition.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:**
  - Demonstrating that πᾶς was present in Paul's Septuagint Vorlage would modify the view that Paul deliberately added it for contextual and theological emphasis.

### 2. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13

#### Reasoning Map

- **QUESTION:** How does Paul integrate Isaiah 40:13 into Romans 11:34, and what does it reveal about his scriptural citation technique?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul quotes almost word-for-word from LXX Isaiah 40:13 (τίς γὰρ ἔγνω νοῦν κυρίου...) in Romans 11:34 without an explicit introductory quotation formula (printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The quotation forms part of a hymn-like doxology at the end of Romans 11, accompanied by a possible echo of Job 41:11 (LXX 41:3) in Romans 11:35 (printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In 1 Corinthians 2:16, Paul quotes Isaiah 40:13 while omitting its middle section, whereas in Romans 11:35 he omits the end of the verse (printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul quoted either from memory or by drawing automatically on familiar biblical idiom, demonstrating his freedom to adapt scriptural passages selectively according to his specific contextual purpose (printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]).
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* The rhetorical questions in Romans 11:34 express the vastness, divine transcendence, and unsearchable nature of God's ways in salvation history (printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]).
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Isaiah 40:13 provides the scriptural foundation for concluding Paul's discourse on Israel's salvation with an absolute affirmation of God's unsearchable sovereignty and mercy (printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]).

#### Analytical Breakdown

- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:**
  - Paul assumes his audience will recognize or absorb the weight of biblical idiom expressing God's unsearchable wisdom even when presented without a formal citation marker (printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:**
  - Ryšková explicitly frames Paul's method as disjunctive: quoting by heart from his extensive knowledge of Isaiah versus drawing automatically on biblical language (printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]).
  - She expressly qualifies the Job 41:11 reference in Rom 11:35 as a *possible echo* rather than a definite quotation (printed p. 112 [PDF p. 18]).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:**
  - Ryšková does not address whether Romans 11:33–36 was adapted from a pre-existing Jewish synagogue liturgical hymn.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:**
  - The source does not state explicit falsification criteria for this doxological citation.

### 3. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10

#### Reasoning Map

- **QUESTION:** What is the function of quoting Isaiah 11:10 at the culmination of the citation chain in Romans 15:9–12?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul quotes LXX Isaiah 11:10 (ἔσται ἡ ῥίζα τοῦ Ἰεσσαί...) in Romans 15:12, introducing it explicitly with Ἠσαΐας λέγει (printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* This quotation concludes a chain of four scriptural citations drawn from Psalms (Ps 18:49), Deuteronomy (Deut 32:43), and Psalms (Ps 117:1) in Romans 15:9–12 (printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* The quotation represents an ideological shift from general praise of God to a specific focus on the "root of Jesse" as the sovereign Lord and hope of the Gentiles (printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]).
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* By presenting Christ as the ruler over the nations who did not please Himself (Rom 15:3), Paul offers Christ as the supreme model of self-giving love for both Jewish and Gentile believers (printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]).
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Isaiah 11:10 serves as the culmination of scriptural proof that Gentile inclusion is part of God's messianic promise, motivating mutual acceptance between "the weak and the strong" while setting the stage for Paul's mission to Spain (printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]).

#### Analytical Breakdown

- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:**
  - Paul assumes that Jesus Christ is the messianic "shoot from the stump of Jesse" promised in Isaiah (printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20]).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:**
  - Ryšková explicitly contrasts her view with Florian Wilk (1998, p. 394): while Wilk plays down the theological weight of Romans 15:12, Ryšková emphasizes its positive motivational force for Gentile inclusion and community unity (printed p. 114 [PDF p. 20], fn. 61).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:**
  - Ryšková does not address whether the entire four-text quotation chain in Romans 15:9–12 was adopted intact from an early Christian missionary testimony collection.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:**
  - The source does not state explicit falsification criteria for this passage.
