1. B001-1: Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6 (Galatians 3:6)
Page / Locator: Page 136 (Note on Gal 3:6) & Pages 158–164 (Detached Note: "The Faith of Abraham").
Discusses a Competing Textual Form:
On page 136, Lightfoot notes that the Hebrew MT of Genesis 15:6 reads וַיַּחְשְׁבֶהָ לּוֹ צְדָקָה ("and [He] imputed it to him [for] righteousness"), whereas the Septuagint converts the active Hebrew verb into a passive construction (καὶ ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ εἰς δικαιοσύνην). He observes that Paul quotes the LXX text verbatim in Galatians 3:6 and Romans 4:3, as do James 2:23, 1 Clement 10, and Justin Martyr (Dial. 119) [p. 136]

12. B001-12: Galatians 3:13 / Deuteronomy 21:23
Page / Locator: Pages 139–140 (Note on Gal 3:13) & Pages 152–154 (Detached Note: "The Interpretation of Deut. xxi. 23").
Notes a Hebrew / Greek Ambiguity:
On page 152, Lightfoot analyzes the grammatical ambiguity of קִלְלַת אֱלֹהִים in Deuteronomy 21:23: אֱלֹהִים can function as either a subjective genitive ("accursed of God", as in Judges 9:57) or an objective genitive ("an insult / reproach / contempt to God", as in Gen 27:13) [p. 152]

.
Discusses Competing Textual Forms:
On pages 152–153, Lightfoot evaluates multiple competing versions:
Septuagint: κεκατηραμένος ὑπὸ τοῦ Θεοῦ ("accursed in the sight of God");
Jewish/Ebionite Greek translators: Aquila and Theodotion (κατάρα Θεοῦ κρεμάμενος), Symmachus ("quia propter blasphemiam Dei suspensus est");
Apocryphal / Patristic Jewish-Christian citations: Ebionite Gospel ("ὕβρις Θεοῦ ὁ κρεμάμενος"), Ariston of Pella in the Dispute of Jason and Papiscus (λοιδορία Θεοῦ ὁ κρεμάμενος);
Rabbinic & Aramaic Targums: Siphri ("a reviling of God"), Targum of Pseudo-Jonathan, and Peshito Syriac [pp. 152–153]

Allows Authorial Adaptation & Intentional Omission:
On page 140, Lightfoot points out that Paul instinctively omits ὑπὸ Θεοῦ from LXX Deuteronomy 21:23 in Galatians 3:13 (ἐπικατάρατος πᾶς ὁ κρεμάμενος ἐπὶ ξύλου): "He was in no literal sense κατάρατος ὑπὸ Θεοῦ, and St Paul instinctively omits those words which do not strictly apply, and which, if added, would have required some qualification" [p. 140]

.
On page 154, Lightfoot explains Paul's adaptation in applying a statute originally concerning the post-execution exposure/gibbeting of a corpse to death by crucifixion, noting that Jewish national sentiment attached the ignominy to the hanging itself [p. 154].
Disagrees with Another Scholar:
On page 152, Lightfoot rejects interpretations that abandon the LXX subjective genitive, demonstrating that the LXX rendering suits the context best and is supported by direct biblical parallels (Judges 9:57) [p. 152]
