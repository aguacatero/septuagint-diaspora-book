An audit of the extractions against J. B. Itemized Audit Findings & Corrections
1. Preservation of Qualification on the Relationship Between James and Paul (Check #4 & #6)
Location: Page 164 (Detached Note: "The Faith of Abraham").
Previous Extraction: Characterized Lightfoot’s resolution of Paul (Gal 3:6 / Rom 4:3) and James (Jas 2:23) as a settled certainty that James wrote completely independently without knowledge of Paul's epistles.
Source Observation: On page 164 (and fn. 1), Lightfoot maintains that Paul and James addressed different errors, but explicitly qualifies the extent of James's awareness of Paul's phrasing: "Whether St James was acquainted with St Paul's Epistles or not, the coincidence of language is easily explained... He may have been acquainted with St Paul's teaching in its true form, and written to correct a perversion of it; or he may have lived in a different circle of labor where the same Jewish errors prevailed independently" [p. 164].
Correction: Refine claim S04F-E009 to preserve Lightfoot's qualification: Lightfoot holds that Paul and James are fundamentally harmonious because they target different errors (Pharisaic legalism vs. barren orthodoxy), while leaving open as a possibility whether James was responding to a misconstruction of Paul's actual epistles or addressing an independent Jewish school tradition.
2. Explicit Theological Qualification on the Omission of ὑπὸ Θεοῦ in Galatians 3:13 (Check #6)
Location: Page 140 (Note on Galatians 3:13).
Previous Extraction: Stated that Paul omitted ὑπὸ Θεοῦ because Christ was in no literal sense accursed by God, without detailing Lightfoot's underlying theological rationale.
Source Observation: On page 140, Lightfoot provides the explicit theological qualification: "He was in no literal sense κατάρατος ὑπὸ Θεοῦ... The curse of the law rests upon the sinner. Christ was not a sinner, but He took upon Him the consequences of sin, and thus became a curse for us (γενόμενος ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν κατάρα)" [p. 140].
Correction: Update claim S04F-E002 to include Lightfoot's explicit distinction: Paul instinctively omits ὑπὸ Θεοῦ because Christ was not personally a sinner subject to divine wrath, but rather voluntarily bore the vicarious curse on behalf of humanity (ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν).
