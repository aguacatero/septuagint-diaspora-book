# S09F — Argument Map

**Source ID:** S09F  
**Citation:** Willem Oliver, "Romans 10:5–13 revisited," *HTS Teologiese Studies / Theological Studies* 71, no. 3 (2015), Art. #2929, 12 pp. (`http://dx.doi.org/10.4102/hts.v71i3.2929`)  
**Canonical Scope Distinction:** Direct B001 locus evidence covers **`B001-4` (Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16)** and **`B001-5` (Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 [LXX 3:5])**. Oliver's structural analysis of Romans 10:5–10 (Leviticus 18:5; Deuteronomy 30:12–14) and pericope encasement is preserved separately under **`B001-CONTEXT`** so contextual background is not misclassified as `B001-4` or `B001-5`.

---

## 1. Thesis & Structural Architecture (Contextual Framing — `B001-CONTEXT`)

Oliver argues that Paul did not casually append Romans 10:5–13 as a loosely connected catena, but consciously constructed the sub-pericope using an overarching structural encasement and nested chiastic configurations centered on the dual condition of salvation in Romans 10:9–10 (`Printed p. 1, p. 3, p. 9 [PDF p. 1, p. 3, p. 9]`), building upon the methodological assumption—taken from Lund (`1942:139`) and Fetler (`2005:231`) on `Printed p. 1 [PDF p. 1]`—that Paul was educated in Greek rhetoric and had Hebrew training (treated strictly as an authorial background premise in Oliver, not as text-critical proof of direct Hebrew Vorlage consultation):

1. **Prophetic Encasement (`S09F-E008`, Printed p. 3 [PDF p. 3], NotebookLM `[1]`; Figure 1, Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4]; Table 1, Printed p. 5 [PDF p. 5]):**
   - Except for verse 12 and the chiastic confession in verses 9–10, Paul constructs Romans 10:5–13 from Old Testament citations (Lev 18:5; Hab 2:4; Deut 9:4; Deut 30:12–14; Isa 28:16; Joel 2:32).
   - Oliver hedges this as a rhetorical/structural impression: *"It leaves the impression that, besides the use of the (double) chiasm, Paul has encased these words with Scriptures so as to highlight it more or to give it more prominence"* (`S09F-E008`, Printed p. 3 [PDF p. 3]).
2. **Law vs. Faith Antithesis (`S09F-E001`, Printed p. 5 [PDF p. 5], NotebookLM `[2]`; Table 1, p. 5):**
   - Romans 10:5a and 6a establish an antithetical `a–b–b–a` chiasm contrasting Moses's righteousness from the Law (`τὴν δικαιοσύνην τὴν ἐκ νόμου`, citing Lev 18:5) with righteousness speaking from faith (`ἡ δὲ ἐκ πίστεως δικαιοσύνη`), connected to speech-in-character in Rom 10:6–8 (`Printed p. 1, fn. 1`, citing Kruse 2012:407).
3. **Selective Citation and Implication of Deuteronomy 30:12–14 (`S09F-E002`, `S09F-E003`, Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4], NotebookLM `[5]`; Table 2, Printed p. 5 [PDF p. 5]):**
   - Paul replaces *"beyond the sea"* (Deut 30:13) with *"into the deep (abyss)"* in Rom 10:7 to introduce his Christological gloss on bringing Christ up from the dead (`S09F-E002`, Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4]).
   - Paul passes over Moses's thrice-repeated phrase *"so we/you may obey it"* (Deut 30:12, 13, 14), yet Oliver stresses that *"Paul does not cite these words, but implicates them in his own context"* (`S09F-E003`, Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4]; cf. fn. 9 citing Derrett 1983:143).
4. **Heart–Mouth Interlocking Double Chiasm (`S09F-E004`, Printed p. 9 [PDF p. 9], NotebookLM `[7]`; Figures 2 & 3, Printed pp. 6 & 9 [PDF pp. 6 & 9]):**
   - Extracting *"mouth"* and *"heart"* from Deut 30:14 (Rom 10:8b), Paul reverses their order into the `1-2-3-4` (`mouth/confess -> heart/believe`) and `4-3-2-1` (`heart/believe -> mouth/confess`) pattern of Rom 10:9–10 (`S09F-E004`, Printed p. 9 [PDF p. 9]; note printed `4-3-2-1` wraps across lines as `4-3-2-\n1` on p. 9).
5. **Contextual Hermeneutical Alternatives Evaluated & Rejected (`B001-CONTEXT`, Printed pp. 7–8 [PDF pp. 7–8]):**
   - *Consecutive stages (`pp. 7–8`, citing `Rom 13:11` & future `σωθήσεται` v. 13):* `Hodge (1947:341, 343)`, `Sanday & Headlam (1902:290)`, `Barclay (1975:139)`, `Boice (1993:1209)`, and `Godet (1956:383)` view belief and confession as sequential salvation requirements; Oliver rejects this because Paul states consequences rather than chronological stages (`p. 8`).
   - *Synonymous / two-aspects (`p. 8`):* `Hart (1999)`, `Ironside (1928:131)`, `Nygren (1949:383)`, `Moo (1996:657` — explicitly citing `Rm 9:30; 10:4, 11`), `Aho (1981:308)`, `Kruse (2012:410)`, `Ito (2006:250)`, `Dunn (1988b:609)`, and `Johnson (1974:151)` treat heart-belief and mouth-confession as one act or inseparable aspects; Oliver argues vv. 9–10 show chiasm rather than semantic parallelism.
   - *Faith eventually produces confession (`p. 8`):* `Murray (1968:55–57)`, `Shedd (1978:318–319)`, and `Schreiner (1998:609)` argue genuine faith inevitably reaches the lips; Oliver objects that v. 10c makes confession lead to salvation rather than salvation produce confession (citing `Hodges 1989:197`).

## 2. Argument Mapping to Canonical `B001-4` (Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16)

- **Classificatory Status in Oliver:** `B001-4` is treated with **Substantive** depth across Printed pp. 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, and 11 [PDF pp. 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 11], though Oliver does not perform an MT-vs-LXX text-critical collation of Isaiah 28:16.
- **LXX Identification & Romans 1:16 Linkage (`S09F-E009`, Printed p. 3 [PDF p. 3], NotebookLM `[8]`):**
  - Oliver explicitly identifies Rom 10:11 as a reference to **`Is 28:16 LXX`** (`Printed p. 3 [PDF p. 3]` and `Table 1, Printed p. 5 [PDF p. 5]`) and classifies it among the *prophetic* Scriptures Paul cites (`Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4]`).
  - He argues that Paul's use of `καταισχύνω` in v. 11 links directly back to `ἐπαισχύνομαι` in Romans 1:16—where Jewett (`2007:138`, `Printed p. 3 [PDF p. 3]`) observes that the gospel removes human shame and Matlock (`2010:79, 80`, `Printed p. 1, p. 3 [PDF p. 1, p. 3]`) connects faith in Rom 1:16 with Rom 10:9–10—linking faith-salvation in vv. 9–11 back to the letter's thematic thesis.
- **Methodological Qualification on Aho (`S09F-E010`, Printed p. 3 [PDF p. 3], fn. 5, NotebookLM `Prompt 4 / label=12`):**
  - In fn. 5 on p. 3, Oliver critiques G. Aho's (`1981:308`) claim that the Isaiah 28:16 citation in Rom 10:11 already stipulates *"that ‘the way of righteousness by faith is meant for all’,"* warning: *"This could be a too subjective reading of the verse within this pericope."* (Alongside Aho's body citation on `Printed p. 8 [PDF p. 8]` treating faith and confession as two sides of one coin).
- **Universality & Fourfold `γάρ` Chain (`S09F-E005`, Printed p. 7 [PDF p. 7], NotebookLM `[9]`):**
  - Shared with `B001-5`: Oliver shows that vv. 11–13 introduce four clauses with `γάρ`, asserting the universality of God's salvation through `πᾶς` in vv. 11 and 13 and `κύριος πάντων` in v. 12 (`Printed p. 7 [PDF p. 7]`, citing Bechtler 1994:306). On `Printed p. 10 [PDF p. 10]`, he summarizes the effect of vv. 11–13 as establishing *"No shame for a believer"* and *"Faith is for everyone."*
  - Oliver does **not** argue text-critically that Paul altered LXX Isaiah 28:16 by adding `πᾶς`.
- **Direct Locus Alternative Noted by Oliver (p. 8):**
  - Citing **Moo (`1996:657`)**, Oliver records (and resists within the Rom 10:5–13 sub-pericope) the broader Pauline counter-reading—explicitly anchored by Moo in **`Rm 9:30; 10:4, 11`**—that faith in the heart remains the crucial requirement for salvation rather than elevating oral confession to a second condition (`Printed p. 8 [PDF p. 8]`).

## 3. Argument Mapping to Canonical `B001-5` (Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 [LXX Joel 3:5])

- **Classificatory Status in Oliver:** `B001-5` is treated with **Substantive** depth across Printed pp. 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 11 [PDF pp. 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11].
- **Prophetic Classification, Acts 2:17–21, & 1 Corinthians 1:2 (`Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4], fn. 10`):**
  - Oliver treats Rom 10:13 as a prophetic citation from Joel 2:32 (within Joel 2:28–32; `Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4]`), noting in fn. 10 that in **`Acts 2:17–21` the apostle Peter quotes Joel's words directly** to tell the crowd that in the last days people will proclaim God's word, while **Paul reuses these invocation words elsewhere only in 1 Corinthians 1:2** (`Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4]`, referring to Christians as those who call on the Name of Jesus).
- **Direct Locus Alternatives for Verse 13 (`Printed p. 7 [PDF p. 7]`):**
  - Oliver rejects treating `ἐπικαλέω` (v. 13) merely as a summary combining `πιστεύω` and `ὁμολογέω` (`"There is no evidence in the context for this postulation"`, `p. 7`), arguing instead that it extends the `ῥῆμα τῆς πίστεως` (v. 8) alongside `κηρύσσω` and `ὁμολογέω`.
  - He likewise engages and critiques **Hart (`1999`)** and **Hodges (`1989:198`)**, who argue that `σωτηρία` (v. 10b) / `σωθήσεται` (v. 13) applies to already justified believers calling upon the Lord in public prayer (`Printed p. 7 [PDF p. 7]`), asking why Paul's putative conclusion in v. 13 omits `"believe"` (`pp. 7, 9`).
- **Universality Alongside `B001-4` (`S09F-E005`, Printed p. 7 [PDF p. 7], NotebookLM `[9]`):**
  - Verse 13 forms the climactic `γάρ` statement using `πᾶς` (`Πᾶς γὰρ ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα κυρίου σωθήσεται`), completing the universal scope announced in v. 11 (`Is 28:16`) and v. 12 (`κύριος πάντων`).
- **Scriptural Crescendo of Oral Calling & Salvation (`S09F-E006`, Printed p. 10 [PDF p. 10], NotebookLM `[11]`):**
  - While echoing the chiasm's `σωθήσῃ` (v. 9) via `σωθήσεται` (v. 13), v. 13 introduces *"something ‘new’"* (pp. 7, 10): repeating the verb `ἐπικαλέομαι` from v. 12 to show that *"‘something-being-said’ ... results in σωτηρίαν"* (`S09F-E006`, Printed p. 10 [PDF p. 10]).
- **Rhetorical Hinge to Apostolic Proclamation & Tentative Resolution (`S09F-E007`, Printed p. 5 [PDF p. 5] & pp. 10–11, NotebookLM `[13]`):**
  - Rom 10:13 pivots toward outward apostolic mission in vv. 14–15 (citing Isa 52:7), where Paul reverses the verb sequence (`ἐπικαλέσωνται -> πιστεύσωσιν -> ἀκούσωσιν -> κηρύσσοντος`), replacing v. 9–10's `ὁμολογέω` with `πιστεύσωσιν` (`Printed p. 10 [PDF p. 10]`).
  - Crucially, on `Printed pp. 8–11 [PDF pp. 8–11]`, Oliver candidly labels Paul's elevation of *"something-being-said"* an argument-level **`Hapax Legomenon`** (`pp. 8–9, fn. 21`), *"in total contrast to Paul’s whole ‘gospel’ and theology"* (`p. 10`), and *"the odd exception"* whose words *"do not really form a clear symphony with the rest of his letter"* (`p. 11`), offering his own prophetic-witness reading only as a tentative proposal (*"it looks quite safe to make the conclusion..."*; *"The answer (for me)..."*, `p. 11`).
