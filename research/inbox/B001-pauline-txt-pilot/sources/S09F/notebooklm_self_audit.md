CORRECTIONS REQUIRED
An audit of the extractions against Willem Oliver’s Romans 10:5–13 revisited (2015) reveals that while the structural and literary analyses of the chiasms and Deuteronomy/Joel citations are well grounded, corrections are required regarding an imported textual-critical claim on Isaiah 28:16, the omission of epistemic hedging in quotation framing, and the specification of internal article pagination.
Itemized Audit Findings & Corrections
1. Correction of Imported Model Knowledge in S09F-E005 (Checks #1 & #7)
Location: Page 8 [Passage 43].
Previous Extraction: Claimed in S09F-E005 that Paul “quotes Isaiah 28:16 from the Septuagint in Romans 10:11, adding/incorporating ‘everyone’ (πᾶς) to substantiate that salvation is universally accessible without distinction between Jew and Gentile.”
Source Observation: Oliver himself does not state that Paul added or inserted πᾶς into the Septuagint text of Isaiah 28:16. The observation that πᾶς is an authorial addition to LXX Isaiah 28:16 is a standard finding in Pauline scholarship (e.g., Sanday & Headlam, Moo, Cranfield), but Oliver’s text merely states:
"With the four statements to follow (v. 11–13), each introduced by γάρ and each supporting the previous statement, Paul asserts the universality of God’s salvation, with words or phrases such as πᾶς (vv. 11 & 13) and κύριος πάντων (v. 12) (cf. Bechtler 1994:306)." [p. 8, Passage 43].
Correction: Revise claim S09F-E005 from a textual-critical claim of text alteration to an exegetical claim: Oliver highlights that in Romans 10:11–13, Paul uses four statements introduced by γάρ asserting the universality of salvation through universal terms such as πᾶς (vv. 11 & 13) and κύριος πάντων (v. 12) [p. 8, Passage 43].
2. Restoring Epistemic Hedging in S09F-E008 (Checks #2 & #4)
Location: Page 4 [Passage 20].
Previous Extraction: Quoted S09F-E008 as a definitive statement: "Paul has encased these words with Scriptures so as to highlight it more or to give it more prominence", claiming as fact that Paul encased his words in prophetic authority.
Source Observation: Oliver introduces this observation as an authorial impression/hypothesis rather than a categorical assertion:
"It leaves the impression that, besides the use of the (double) chiasm, Paul has encased these words with Scriptures so as to highlight it more or to give it more prominence (Table 1)." [p. 4, Passage 20].
Correction: Update quotation and claim in S09F-E008 to retain Oliver’s explicit epistemic qualifier: Oliver argues that Paul’s arrangement "leaves the impression that, besides the use of the (double) chiasm, Paul has encased these words with Scriptures so as to highlight it more or to give it more prominence" [p. 4, Passage 20].
3. Calibration of Internal Pagination (Check #3)
Location: Article #2929, pages 1–12.
Source Observation: The article is published in HTS Teologiese Studies / Theological Studies 71(3) as Art. #2929 across 12 pages. The previous turn cited Table 1 / Figure 1 as page 4 or 5 and Figure 3 as page 7 or 10.
Correction: Calibrate all page locators strictly to the internal 12-page PDF run of Art. #2929:
Introduction and Chiasm definition: pp. 1–2 [Passages 1–7].
Faith-Law antithesis and background (Rom 1:16–17; 3:21–31): pp. 2–4 [Passages 8–19].
Text, Figure 1 (Structure), and Table 1: pp. 4–5 [Passages 20–27].
Table 2 (Deuteronomy 30:11–14 vs Rom 10:5–8a): pp. 5–6 [Passages 28–30].
Antithetical chiasm (vv. 5a & 6a) and Figure 2 (Double chiasm): pp. 6–7 [Passages 31–38].
Discussion of consecutive vs simultaneous views and Table/Figure 3: pp. 7–9 [Passages 39–55].
Prophetic context, sequence of verbs, and Figure 3: pp. 9–11 [Passages 56–68].
Conclusion and References: pp. 11–12 [Passages 69–74].
