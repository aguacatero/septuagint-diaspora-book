# S09F — Oliver Romans 10 Chiasm Synthesis

Detailed synthesis of Willem Oliver's structural, textual, and hermeneutical arguments regarding Paul's use of Old Testament citations in Romans 10:5–13, drawn directly from *Romans 10:5–13 revisited*, *HTS Teologiese Studies / Theological Studies*, Vol. 71, No. 3 (2015), Art. #2929, 12 pages.

---

## 1. Overarching Chiastic Structure and Old Testament Framing (Rom 10:5–13)
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed pp. 3–5 [PDF pp. 3–5]

### The Encasing Architecture
Oliver demonstrates that with the exception of verse 12 and the central confession in verses 9–10, the sub-pericope of Romans 10:5–13 consists entirely of Old Testament citations (Lev 18:5; Hab 2:4; Deut 9:4; Deut 30:12–14; Isa 28:16; Joel 2:32). Paul deliberately encases his own apostolic formulation inside a prophetic scriptural framework:
> "It leaves the impression that, besides the use of the (double) chiasm, Paul has encased these words with Scriptures so as to highlight it more or to give it more prominence (Table 1)." (Printed p. 3 [PDF p. 3])

### The Antithetical Chiasm (Verses 5a and 6a)
The unit opens by setting up a stark a-b-b-a chiasm contrasting legal righteousness with faith righteousness:
- Verse 5a: τὴν δικαιοσύνην (a) τὴν ἐκ νόμου (b) [Lev 18:5]
- Verse 6a: ἡ δὲ ἐκ πίστεως (b) δικαιοσύνη (a) [Deut 30:11–12]
> "This contrast is set in the form of a stark a-b-b-a chiasm in verses 5a and 6a: Verse 5a: τὴν δικαιοσύνην (a) τὴν ἐκ νόμο (b) Verse 6a: ἡ δὲ ἐκ πίστεως (b) δικαιοσύνη (a)" (Printed p. 5 [PDF p. 5])

### The Double Chiasm with Centre-Piece (Verses 9–10)
Verses 9–10 form an interlocking double chiasm where the outer A-B-C-B′-A′ structure pivots on personal salvation (C, *sōthēsē*), and the inner sub-unit reverses 1-2-3-4 into 4-3-2-1:
- **A** (1: confess, 2: with mouth) *kyrion Iēsoun*
- **B** (3: believe, 4: in heart) *hoti ho theos auton ēgeiren ek nekrōn*
- **C** *sōthēsē* ("you will be saved" — the central theological climax)
- **B′** (4: with heart, 3: believes) *eis dikaiosynēn*
- **A′** (2: with mouth, 1: confesses) *eis sōtērian*

### Prophetic Universal Climax (Verses 11–13)
Paul concludes the sub-pericope with two prophetic citations—Isaiah 28:16 (LXX) in verse 11 (*pas ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou kataischynthēsetai*) and the core promise of Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5a) in verse 13 (*pas gar hos an epikalesētai to onoma kyriou sōthēsetai*, adapting LXX *kai estai pas hos an ...* with postpositive *gar*)—to assert that salvation is universally accessible without distinction between Jew and Greek (v. 12):
> "Paul asserts the universality of God’s salvation, with words or phrases such as πᾶς (vv. 11 & 13) and κύριος πάντων (v. 12)" (Printed p. 7 [PDF p. 7])

## 2. Textual and Hermeneutical Relationship to Deuteronomy 30:12–14 (MT & LXX)
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed pp. 4–5 [PDF pp. 4–5]

### Personification of Righteousness (Prosopopoeia)
In Deuteronomy 30:11, Moses speaks directly of the commandment/Law (*hammiṣwāh* / *entolē*), whereas in Romans 10:6a Paul personifies "the righteousness that is by faith" and introduces it with the verb *legei* ("says"), contrasting the living voice of faith with Moses writing (*graphei*) in verse 5:
> "Whereas Moses was referring to the Law (Dt 30:11), Paul changes that to ‘the righteousness that is by faith’ (Rm 10:6a). This puts his argument on track, for he is in fact in a polemic with the Law in this letter." (Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4])

### Textual Change: "Beyond the Sea" to "Into the Deep (Abyss)"
In Deuteronomy 30:13, the MT reads *'el-'ēḇer hayyām* and the LXX reads *eis to peran tēs thalassēs* ("beyond the sea"). Paul substitutes *eis tēn abysson* ("into the deep/abyss") in Romans 10:7 to interpret the passage Christologically as referring to Christ's resurrection from the dead:
> "The ‘sea’ of Deuteronomy 30:13 is changed to the ‘deep (abyss)’ (Rm 10:7) that would better refer to bringing Christ from the dead." (Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4])

### Deliberate Omission of Obedience Clauses
Moses repeats the clause "so we/you may obey it" (*kai poiēsomen auto*) three times in Deuteronomy 30:12, 13, and 14. Paul systematically omits this phrase to remove legal works and substitute *to rhēma tēs pisteōs ho kēryssomen* ("the word of faith that we proclaim"):
> "Three times Moses used the words ‘so we/you may obey it’, thereby making it clear that the Law should be obeyed.9 Paul does not cite these words, but implicates them in his own context." (Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4])

### Jewish Hermeneutical Technique
Oliver notes (citing Hart, Dunn, and Kruse) that Paul's method of partial quotation accompanied by interpretive glosses (*tout' estin*) was standard Jewish midrashic exegesis:
> "The partial citation of a passage, joined by an interpretation of the words (as Paul does it here) ‘was a common Jewish approach to the exposition of an OT text’." (Printed pp. 4–5 [PDF pp. 4–5])

## 3. 'Mouth' and 'Heart' in Deuteronomy 30:14 and the Chiastic Inversion in Romans 10:9–10
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed pp. 8–10 [PDF pp. 8–10]

### Derivation from Deuteronomy 30:14
Deuteronomy 30:14 supplies the core anatomical word pair: "The word is near you; it is in your mouth (*en tō stomati sou*) and in your heart (*en tē kardia sou*)." In Romans 10:8b, Paul attaches these terms to proclamation and faith, creating an internal 2-4-3-1 pattern: *stomati* (2), *kardia* (4), *pisteōs* (3), *kēryssomen* (1).

### Chiastic Inversion Between Verses 9 and 10
Paul takes the 1-2-3-4 sequence in verse 9 and reverses it into a 4-3-2-1 sequence in verse 10:
- Verse 9 (1-2-3-4): Confess (1) + mouth (2) → believe (3) + heart (4)
- Verse 10 (4-3-2-1): Heart (4) + believes (3) → mouth (2) + confesses (1)
> "The 1-2-3-4 and 4-3-21 combinations of verses 9 and 10 are therefore already present in verse 8 as a 2-4-3-1 combination." (Printed p. 9 [PDF p. 9])

### Distinction of Results (Righteousness vs. Salvation)
Oliver refutes the claim of Ito, Dunn, and Johnson that verses 9 and 10 are synonymous poetic parallelisms. Paul explicitly separates the bodily faculties and assigns distinct outcomes to each:
- Heart + Believing → results in *dikaiosynēn* ("righteousness")
- Mouth + Confessing ("something-being-said") → results in *sōtērian* ("salvation")
> "If then the heart and the mouth are to be distinguished, the implication is that faith and confession are also distinguished here... Salvation is the result of confession." (Printed p. 8 [PDF p. 8])

## 4. Theological Debate: Contrast (Moses vs. Faith) or Prophetic Testimony to Christ?
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed pp. 4–7 [PDF pp. 4–7]

### Antithesis Between Legal Works and Faith
Oliver demonstrates that Paul establishes a sharp, fundamental contrast between righteousness according to the Law (represented by Moses in v. 5 quoting Lev 18:5) and righteousness according to faith (vv. 6–10):
> "In verses 5–10 Paul describes the means of God’s salvation, contrasting verse 5 (righteousness according to the Law) with verses 6–10 (righteousness according to faith)... Verse 5 depicts the ‘old’ (Jewish) δικαιοσύνη, which is ἐκ νόμου and attached to Moses... Verse 6a brings the new δικαιοσύνη which is ἐκ πίστεως to the fore." (Printed p. 6 [PDF p. 6])

### Deuteronomy Cites a Prophetic Restoration Discourse
While Paul polemicizes against the Law, he does not treat Deuteronomy 30 as legalistic legislation; rather, he reads it as prophetic Scripture anticipating the messianic era:
> "This citation of Deuteronomy 30 is taken from a prophetic part of Deuteronomy where Moses prophesied about the future restoration of Israel... It seems that Paul has deliberately collected the relevant 'prophecies' to explain the situation of Israel concerning the gospel." (Printed p. 4 [PDF p. 4])

### Synthesis: Christ Accomplished What the Law Failed to Do
Paul uses Deuteronomy 30 to show that human striving under the Law (trying to climb to heaven or plunge into the abyss) is excluded because God has already acted in Christ's incarnation and resurrection. The remote command has been replaced by the near oral word (*rhēma*), which calls for inward faith and outward prophetic proclamation:
> "That which the righteousness (works) of the νόμος failed to accomplish, was in fact already done by God himself... This is the reason why Paul professes in verse 8 that the ῥῆμα (the spoken word) is close to his hearers – God has brought the ῥῆμα close to them through Jesus." (Printed pp. 6–7 [PDF pp. 6–7])
