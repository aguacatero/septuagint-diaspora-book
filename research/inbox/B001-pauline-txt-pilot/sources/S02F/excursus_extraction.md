Page Locator: Printed Page 302
1
.
Classification Breakdown: According to Emil Kautzsch (De Veteris Testamenti locis a Paulo Apostolo allegatis), out of eighty-four passages in which Paul cites the Old Testament:
~70 passages: "taken directly from the LXX or do not vary from it appreciably"
1
.
12 passages: "vary considerably, but still show signs of affinity"
1
.
2 passages only: "definitely independent and derived either from the Hebrew text or some quite distinct version"
1
. These two exceptions are both from the Book of Job: Romans 11:35 (= Job 41:3/11) and 1 Corinthians 3:19 (= Job 5:13)

2. Explanations for Resemblance to Hebrew Text Against the LXX
Page Locator: Printed Page 302
1
.
Three Live Hypotheses: Where Pauline quotations derived from the Septuagint show points of agreement with the Hebrew text against the LXX (e.g., Romans 10:15), Sanday and Headlam state that there is not sufficient evidence to decide conclusively between three live possibilities
1
:
"a reminiscence of the Hebrew text (conscious or unconscious)"
1
;
"an Aramaic Targum"
1
; or
"the use of an earlier form of a LXX text"
1
.
Textual Observation: They also observe that Paul’s citations "sometimes agree with late MSS. of the LXX as against the great uncials"
1
.
3. Quotation from Memory vs. Reference and Distinction from Hebrews
Page Locator: Printed Page 302
1
.
Citation Method: Sanday and Headlam conclude that "it may be safely said that the majority of the quotations are from memory"
1
.
Supporting Evidence: They ground this in two textual features: "for many of them are somewhat inexact, and those which are correct are for the most part short and from well-known books"
1
.
Distinction from Hebrews: They contrast Paul's habit with that of another New Testament writer: "There is a very marked distinction between these and the long literary quotations of the Epistle to the Hebrews"
1
.
4. Non-Historical Exegesis and Opposite-Sense Applications
Page Locators: Printed Pages 303–304
2
3
.
Non-Historical Character: Paul's interpretation "is quite clearly not 'historical' in the modern sense of the word. The passages are quoted without regard to their context or to the circumstances under which they were written"
2
.
Opposite-Sense Usage: "The most striking instances of this are those cases in which the words of the O. T. are used in an exactly opposite sense to that which they originally possessed"
2
.
Romans 9:25–26: "words used in the O. T. of the ten tribes are used of the Gentiles"
2
.
Romans 10:6–8: "words used of the Law are applied to the Gospel as against the Law"
2
.
Literal vs. Allegorical: Despite disregarding historical context, Paul's method is not allegorical in the Rabbinic sense: "St. Paul almost invariably takes the literal and direct meaning of the words (although without regard to their context), he does not allegorize or play upon their meaning, or find hidden and mysterious principles" (noting Galatians 4:22 ff. as a rare exception)
2
.
Three Functional Classes of Passages:
Corresponding Sense: Passages used in a sense corresponding to their Old Testament meaning (the largest class, e.g., Rom 9:6–13, 20–21)
2
.
Rhetorical Adaptation: Old Testament language used without formal citation to express Paul's own ideas (e.g., Rom 10:18)
2
3
.
Logical Proof Against Context: Passages used to give logical proof where "the words are used in a sense which the original context will not bear" (e.g., using the Law to condemn the Law, applying non-Messianic passages Messianically, or applying texts to the calling of Gentiles)
3
.

First-Century Logic vs. Universal Religious Insight: The authors distinguish between the timeless spiritual truth of the New Testament and its historical argumentation: "As expounders of religion, they belong to the whole world and to all time; as logicians, they belong to the first century" (quoting C. H. Toy)
3
.
Outer Shell vs. Inner Essence: "The essence of their writing is the Divine spirit of love and righteousness that filled their souls, the outer shell is the intellectual form in which the spirit found expression in words"
3
.
Faulty Exegesis vs. Spiritual Value: "We must trace the superiority of the N. T. writers to its root and take from them that, and not their faulty exegesis"
4
.
Validity of the Messianic Idea: "Any incorrectness of exegesis that there may be lies not in the ideas themselves but in finding them in passages which have probably a different meaning. We are not bound, and it would be wrong to bind ourselves, by the incorrect exegesis of particular passages; but the reality and truth of the Messianic idea... remain one of the most real and impressive facts in religious history"
5
.
Core Value of Paul's Exegesis: "The value of St. Paul's exegesis therefore lies not in his true interpretation of individual passages, but in his insight into the spiritual meaning of the O. T."
5
