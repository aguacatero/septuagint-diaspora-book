Here is the reasoning map and analytical breakdown for each of the seven substantive B001 comparisons in William Sanday and Arthur C. Headlam's A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans (1895).
1. B001-1: Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6
Reasoning Map
QUESTION: How does Paul understand and apply Genesis 15:6 (ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ εἰς δικαιοσύνην), and how does his exegesis compare with James 2:21–23
1
2
?
EVIDENCE:
In Genesis 15:6, faith is reckoned (ἐλογίσθη) as standing in place of righteousness
1
.
In Septuagint usage, λογίζομαι carries a legal accounting sense ("set down on the credit side")
1
.
James 2:21–23 quotes Genesis 15:6 alongside Genesis 22 (the offering of Isaac) to conclude that faith requires works
2
.
Paul quotes Genesis 15:6 in Romans 4:3 and later appeals to predictions of Isaac's birth (Rom 4:18–21)
2
.
INFERENCE:
Structural Paraphrase: Paul adheres more strictly to the literal statement of Genesis 15:6 (which mentions faith alone) than James does, employing a more searching exegesis confirmed by personal spiritual experience
2
.
Explicit Source Statement: The term ἐλογίσθη means that faith is set down, credited, or counted as equivalent to righteousness as a gift of divine grace rather than earned pay
1
3
.
CONCLUSION: Paul uses Genesis 15:6 to establish the foundational principle of justification by faith apart from works (πίστει χωρὶς ἔργων)
2
.
Analytical Breakdown
Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged: The idea of imputation (crediting faith in place of righteousness) was familiar to 1st-century Jewish thought (e.g., Weber), as was the idea of merit/demerit transference
1
.
Alternative Explanations Considered:
James's Exegesis: Supporting justification by works through appealing to a later incident in Abraham's life (the offering of Isaac in Gen 22)
2
.
Rabbinic Interpretation: Treating Abraham's faith itself as a meritorious work that earned divine favor (e.g., Mechilta on Exod 14:31)
1
3
.
Patristic Exegesis: Greek theologians (e.g., Euthymius Zigabenus) taking δικαιοῦν as "to make righteous" rather than "to declare righteous as a judge"
3
.
Alternative Explanations Not Addressed: The possibility that Paul and James were addressing completely different pastoral/polemical situations rather than employing competing exegetical methods on Genesis.
Falsifying / Weakening Evidence: The source notes that if δικαιοῦν were proven to mean "to make righteous" rather than "to declare righteous," the Greek patristic view would hold
3
.
2. B001-2: Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 [LXX 2:25]
Reasoning Map
QUESTION: Why does Paul invert the clauses of Hosea 2:23, substitute καλέσω for ἐρῶ, and adopt the love terminology of Codex Vaticanus
4
?
EVIDENCE:
LXX Hosea 2:23 reads ἐλεήσω τὴν οὐκ ἠλεημένην, καὶ ἐρῶ τῷ οὐ λαῷ μου...
4
.
Codex Vaticanus (B) reads ἀγαπήσω τὴν οὐκ ἠγαπημένην
4
.
Paul writes καλέσω τὸν οὐ λαόν μου... καὶ τὴν οὐκ ἠγαπημένην..., placing "not my people" first and using καλέσω
4
.
INFERENCE:
Structural Paraphrase: Paul utilized a Septuagint manuscript tradition containing the reading of Codex Vaticanus (ἀγαπήσω)
4
.
Explicit Source Statement: Paul inverted the clauses so that the reference to τὸν οὐ λαόν μου (which suited Gentiles best) came first, and substituted καλέσω because it "naturally crept in from the ἐκάλεσεν of the previous verse"
4
.
CONCLUSION: Paul applies the underlying principle of Hosea 2:23—that God can take into His covenant those previously cut off—to justify the divine calling of the Gentiles
4
.
Analytical Breakdown
Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged: The habit had arisen in the early Church of quoting Old Testament passages to prove the calling of the Gentiles, creating commonplaces that were later collected in writing
4
.
Alternative Explanations Considered:
Scribal Conformation: The hypothesis that Codex Vaticanus was altered retrospectively to match Paul's text (rejected by the authors: "can hardly have been altered to harmonize with him")
4
.
Rabbinic Interpretation: Rabbi Eliezer's view (Pesachim viii. f.) that Israel was exiled among the nations specifically to gather proselytes
4
.
1 Peter 2:10: Follows the majority LXX manuscript reading (ἠλεημένοι) rather than Codex Vaticanus
4
.
Alternative Explanations Not Addressed: That Paul cited an existing Hellenistic Jewish-Christian testimonia collection rather than modifying the Septuagint text directly.
Falsifying / Weakening Evidence: Evidence proving that Codex Vaticanus was systematically conformed to the New Testament text by later Christian scribes
4
.
3. B001-3: Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 [LXX 2:1]
Reasoning Map
QUESTION: What is the purpose and location intended by Paul's insertion of ἐκεῖ into Hosea 1:10 in Romans 9:26
4
?
EVIDENCE:
Paul quotes Hosea 1:10 as καὶ ἔσται ἐν τῷ τόπῳ οὗ ἐρρέθη αὐτοῖς Οὐ λαός μου ὑμεῖς, ἐκεῖ κληθήσονται υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος
4
.
The uncial Septuagint text lacks ἐκεῖ (κληθήσονται καὶ αὐτοί...)
4
.
INFERENCE:
Structural Paraphrase: The insertion of ἐκεῖ serves to emphasize the locus of divine reception
4
.
CONCLUSION: Hosea 1:10 confirms that wherever Gentiles suffer the reproach of being outside God's people, in that very place they shall become members of His universal Church
4
.
Analytical Breakdown
Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged: Paul was often strongly influenced by the language and ideas of Jewish eschatology, though in his more spiritual passages he was freed from them
4
.
Alternative Explanations Considered:
Literal / Palestinian Interpretation: ἐκεῖ refers to Palestine/Jerusalem as the gathering site for the nations in a restored Messianic kingdom
4
.
Universal Interpretation: ἐκεῖ refers spiritually to anywhere throughout the whole world where Gentiles reside
4
.
Alternative Explanations Not Addressed: That ἐκεῖ was already present in an established pre-Pauline Greek manuscript variant (such as Codex Alexandrinus).
Falsifying / Weakening Evidence: None explicitly stated in the source text.
4. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16
Reasoning Map
QUESTION: Why does Paul re-quote Isaiah 28:16 in Romans 10:11 with the addition of πᾶς
5
?
EVIDENCE:
In Romans 9:33, Paul quotes Isaiah 28:16 as ὁ πιστεύων ἐπʼ αὐτῷ...
5
.
In Romans 10:11, he quotes λέγει γὰρ ἡ γραφή· Πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων ἐπʼ αὐτῷ οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται
5
.
INFERENCE:
Explicit Source Statement: πᾶς is added "to bring out the point on which emphasis is to be laid" in the ongoing argument
5
.
CONCLUSION: Isaiah 28:16 serves as scriptural proof that faith is the sole condition of salvation and demonstrates the universal character of Gospel righteousness for both Jew and Gentile
5
.
Analytical Breakdown
Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged: The old system of δικαιοσύνη ἐκ νόμου has been done away with in Christ to establish a new system defined by faith and universality
5
.
Alternative Explanations Considered: None mentioned for this specific textual addition in the commentary note
5
.
Alternative Explanations Not Addressed: That πᾶς was inserted due to catchword assimilation from Joel 2:32 (πᾶς ὃς ἂν...) quoted two verses later.
Falsifying / Weakening Evidence: None stated in the source text.
5. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 [LXX 3:5]
Reasoning Map
QUESTION: How does Paul apply Joel 2:32 (πᾶς γὰρ ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου σωθήσεται), and to whom does Κύριος refer
5
6
?
EVIDENCE:
Joel 2:32 LXX/MT promises salvation to "whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord" in the context of the "day of the Lord"
6
.
Paul quotes Joel 2:32 verbatim in Romans 10:13
6
.
In Romans 10:9, 12, Κύριος refers directly to Jesus Christ (Κύριον Ἰησοῦν, ὁ αὐτὸς Κύριος πάντων)
5
6
.
ἐπικαλεῖσθαι τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου was the technical LXX formula for worshipping Jehovah
6
.
INFERENCE:
Structural Paraphrase: Paul takes the Jewish expectation of safety in the end-time Messianic kingdom and applies it spiritually to Christian salvation in Christ
6
.
Explicit Source Statement: The divine title Κύριος is transferred to Christ, making the phrase a technical designation for Christians as worshippers of Christ
6
.
CONCLUSION: Joel 2:32 clenches Paul's argument that salvation is universally guaranteed to anyone who calls upon Christ
6
.
Analytical Breakdown
Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged: Calling on the name of the Lord was the distinguishing mark of Jehovah's worshippers in the OT, which Christians adopted to distinguish themselves as worshippers of Christ
6
.
Alternative Explanations Considered:
Alfred Seeberg's Thesis: (Die Anbetung des Herrn bei Paulus) analyzing early Christian prayer to Christ
6
.
National Eschatological View: Literal Jewish expectation of physical safety in Jerusalem during apocalyptic judgment
6
.
Alternative Explanations Not Addressed: That Paul understood Κύριος in Joel 2:32 as God the Father while presenting Christ as the mediator.
Falsifying / Weakening Evidence: None explicitly stated in the source text.
6. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13
Reasoning Map
QUESTION: How does Paul use Isaiah 40:13 (τίς γὰρ ἔγνω νοῦν Κυρίου; ἢ τίς σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο;) in his doxology
7
?
EVIDENCE: Isaiah 40:13 LXX is quoted in Romans 11:34 "varying only very slightly from the LXX," and is also cited in 1 Corinthians 2:16
7
.
INFERENCE:
Structural Paraphrase: The rhetorical question affirms the transcendent, unsearchable character of God's wisdom and plans
7
.
CONCLUSION: Isaiah 40:13 provides scriptural confirmation for the unsearchable depth of God's judgments and ways at the climax of Romans 9–11
7
.
Analytical Breakdown
Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged: The infinite character of Divine wisdom was a fundamental assumption inherited by Paul from Judaism (also expressed in 4 Ezra and Apoc. Baruch)
7
8
.
Alternative Explanations Considered:
Trinitarian Interpretation (e.g., Liddon): Attempting to assign the prepositions in verse 36 (ἐξ αὐτοῦ, διʼ αὐτοῦ, εἰς αὐτόν) to the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit
7
. The authors reject this, stating the prepositions do not suit it and the passage refers to the Godhead as a whole
7
.
Alternative Explanations Not Addressed: That Paul cited Isaiah 40:13 from a Hellenistic Jewish liturgical hymn.
Falsifying / Weakening Evidence: None stated in the source text.
7. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10
Reasoning Map
QUESTION: What is the textual relationship of Paul's citation of Isaiah 11:10 to the Hebrew text, and how does it support his thesis
9
?
EVIDENCE:
Hebrew MT Isaiah 11:10 reads "the root of Jesse, which standeth for an ensign of the peoples, unto him shall the Gentiles seek"
9
.
LXX Isaiah 11:10 renders this paraphrastically as Ἔσται ἡ ῥίζα τοῦ Ἰεσσαί, καὶ ὁ ἀνιστάμενος ἄρχειν ἐθνῶν, ἐπʼ αὐτῷ ἔθνη ἐλπιοῦσιν
9
.
Paul quotes the Septuagint text in Romans 15:12
9
.
INFERENCE:
Structural Paraphrase: Paul relies on the Septuagint's explicit rendering (ἄρχειν ἐθνῶν and ἐλπιοῦσιν) to emphasize Gentile hope under Christ's Messianic reign
9
.
CONCLUSION: Isaiah 11:10 concludes Paul's scriptural catena, demonstrating that the Messianic kingdom under Christ incorporates the Gentiles
9
.
Analytical Breakdown
Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged: The Messianic kingdom was destined to take the place of the national Jewish kingdom
9
.
Alternative Explanations Considered:
F. C. Baur's Interpolation Theory: Baur claimed Romans 15:1–13 was un-Pauline because verse 8 calls Christ a "minister of circumcision"
9
. The authors reject this, pointing out that Jewish priority is consistently maintained throughout Romans (e.g., Rom 1:16, 2:9, 11:17–25)
9
.
Alternative Explanations Not Addressed: That Paul re-translated the Hebrew text independently.
Falsifying / Weakening Evidence: None explicitly stated in the source text.
