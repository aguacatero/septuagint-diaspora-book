# S02F — Bibliography Leads

Here is the itemized breakdown of the footnoted and bibliographical sources cited by William Sanday and Arthur C. Headlam across each substantive B001 passage in their commentary (A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans, 1895).
1. Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6 (B001-1)
Ferdinand Weber
Title: System der altsynagogalen palästinischen Theologie (cited as Altsyn. Theol. / Altsyn. Paläst. Theologie)
Year: 1880 (implied in series/edition context)
Pages / Locators: Pages 233, 255 ff., 280 ff. (cited on source p. 100)
Why it matters: Relied upon. Demonstrates that 1st-century Jewish thought was thoroughly accustomed to the legal idea of imputation (ἐλογίσθη), the transference of merit, and the belief that Abraham's faith constituted a unique meritorious work.
Joseph Barber Lightfoot
Title: Saint Paul's Epistle to the Galatians (extract/note on Gen. xv. 6)
Year: Not specified
Pages / Locators: Cited on source p. 101
Why it matters: Relied upon. Quotes the Rabbinic tradition from Mechilta on Exodus xiv. 31, proving that Jewish interpreters attached special merit to Abraham's faith based on Genesis 15:6.
Franz Delitzsch
Title: Commentary on Genesis (cited ad loc. on Gen. xv. 6)
Year: Not specified
Pages / Locators: Cited on source p. 101
Why it matters: Relied upon. Used alongside Lightfoot to document Rabbinic citations of Genesis 15:6 regarding Abrahamic merit.
Origen
Title: Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans
Year: 3rd Century AD
Pages / Locators: Cited on source p. 99
Why it matters: Discussed / Text-Critical Note. Origen conjectured that Paul originally wrote the Hebrew form Ἀβράμ (Gen. xv), but less scrupulous Gentile scribes substituted Ἀβραάμ. Sanday and Headlam reject Origen's conjecture, holding that Paul had the established name Ἀβραάμ in mind throughout.
Euthymius Zigabenus
Title: Commentary on Romans (on vv. 3, 5, 25)
Year: 12th Century AD
Pages / Locators: Cited on source p. 101
Why it matters: Disputed. Euthymius interpreted δικαιοῦν as δίκαιον ποιῆσαι ("to make righteous"). Sanday and Headlam refute this patristic view, arguing that the lexical evidence is decisive that δικαιοῦν means "to declare righteous as a judge" (forensic acquittance).
G. B. Winer (ed. W. F. Moulton)
Title: A Treatise on the Grammar of New Testament Greek (Grammar § xxix. 3 a.)
Year: Not specified (Moulton ed., p. 229)
Pages / Locators: Cited on source p. 100
Why it matters: Relied upon. Validates the Septuagint grammatical construction of ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ εἰς δικαιοσύνην ("was reckoned as equivalent to / standing in the place of righteousness").
2. Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 [LXX 2:25] (B001-2)
Johann Jakob Wetstein
Title: Novum Testamentum Graecum (citing Pesachim viii. f., Dixit R. Eliezer)
Year: 1751–1752
Pages / Locators: Cited on source p. 264
Why it matters: Relied upon. Documents the Rabbinic tradition where Rabbi Eliezer interpreted Hosea 2:23 to mean that God exiled Israel among the nations for the express purpose of gathering Gentile proselytes.
Edwin Hatch
Title: Essays in Biblical Greek
Year: 1889
Pages / Locators: Page 103 (cited on source p. 264)
Why it matters: Relied upon. Supports the authors' view that a habit had arisen in the early Church of quoting Old Testament proof-texts for the calling of the Gentiles, forming written testimonia/commonplaces.
3. Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 [LXX 2:1] (B001-3)
Edwin Hatch
Title: Essays in Biblical Greek
Year: 1889
Pages / Locators: Page 103 (cited on source p. 264)
Why it matters: Relied upon. Applied to explain Paul's insertion of ἐκεῖ when combining Hosea 1:10 with Hosea 2:23 as part of an established early Christian testimony chain.
4. Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16 (B001-4)
Note: No distinct external monograph is cited in the immediate verse note on page 290 beyond cross-referencing the authors' earlier note on Romans 9:33 and their general bibliography on Pauline OT citations (e.g., Kautzsch, 1869; Toy, 1884 on pp. 302, 306–307).
5. Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 [LXX 3:5] (B001-5)
Alfred Seeberg
Title: Die Anbetung des Herrn bei Paulus
Year: 1891 (Riga)
Pages / Locators: Pages 38, 43–46 (cited on source p. 291)
Why it matters: Relied upon. Provides the detailed liturgical and theological evidence showing that the Old Testament formula for invoking Jehovah (ἐπικαλεῖσθαι τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου) was transferred christologically by Paul to Jesus Christ to designate Christians as worshippers of Christ (ὁ Κύριος).
6. Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13 (B001-6)
Henry Parry Liddon
Title: Explanatory Analysis of St. Paul's Epistle to the Romans
Year: Not specified
Pages / Locators: Cited on source p. 340
Why it matters: Disputed. Liddon attempted to assign the prepositions in verse 36 (ἐξ αὐτοῦ, διʼ αὐτοῦ, εἰς αὐτόν) to the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Sanday and Headlam reject Liddon's interpretation as grammatically unsuited (εἰς αὐτόν cannot apply to the Spirit) and contextually alien to the passage, which addresses the Godhead as a unity.
Frederick Field
Title: Origenis Hexaplorum quae supersunt (Field, Hexapla)
Year: 1875
Pages / Locators: Volume ii. p. 617 (cited on source p. 340)
Why it matters: Relied upon. Documents Symmachus's Greek translation of ἀνεξερεύνητα ("unsearchable") in Prov. xxv. 3 and Jer. xvii. 9.
7. Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10 (B001-7)
Ferdinand Christian Baur
Title: Paulus, der Apostel Jesu Christi
Year: 1845 / 1866
Pages / Locators: Cited on source p. 399
Why it matters: Disputed. Baur claimed Romans 15:1–13 was an un-Pauline interpolation because calling Christ a "minister of circumcision" (v. 8) was an impossible concession to Jewish Christians. Sanday and Headlam refute Baur, demonstrating that Jewish priority is fundamental to Paul's theology throughout Romans.
Wilhelm Mangold
Title: Der Römerbrief und die Anfänge der römischen Gemeinde
Year: 1866
Pages / Locators: Pages 81–100 (cited on source p. 399)
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited for detailed arguments refuting Baur and establishing the historical authenticity and unity of Romans 15:1–13.
General Bibliographical Works Cited on Pauline OT Quotations (pp. 302, 306–307)
Emil Kautzsch: De Veteris Testamenti locis a Paulo Apostolo allegatis (Lipsiae, 1869) — Relied on for statistical classification showing that ~70 of Paul's 84 OT citations agree directly with the LXX.
Crawford Howell Toy: Quotations in the New Testament (New York, 1884, pp. xx, xxi) — Relied on for analyzing how OT language became part of the New Testament writers' mental furniture.
Benjamin Jowett: Contrasts of Prophecy / The Epistles of St. Paul to the Thessalonians, Galatians, Romans — Relied on for showing that Paul inherited the spirit of the Old Testament prophets in condemning legalism.
August Clemen: Ueber den Gebrauch des Alten Testaments im Neuen Testamente (Leipzig, 1891).
David McCalman Turpie: The Old Testament in the New (London, 1868).
(Note: Items B001-8 through B001-12 [1 Corinthians and Galatians] are NOT DISCUSSED in this commentary, which covers the Epistle to the Romans exclusively).
