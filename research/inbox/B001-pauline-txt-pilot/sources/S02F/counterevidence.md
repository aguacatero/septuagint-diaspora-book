2. B001-2: Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 [LXX 2:25]
Locator / Citation: Page 264
4
.
Discusses a Competing Textual Form: The authors note that the Septuagint reads ἐλεήσω τὴν οὐκ ἠλεημένην, but Codex Vaticanus (B) substitutes ἀγαπήσω τὴν οὐκ ἠγαπημένην
4
. They observe that Paul used a text containing the reading of the Vatican MS, which "can hardly have been altered to harmonize with him"
4
. They contrast this with 1 Peter 2:10, which uses the majority LXX reading οἱ οὐκ ἠλεημένοι, νῦν δὲ ἐλεηθέντες
4
.
Allows Authorial Adaptation & Oral/Liturgical Transmission: The authors state that Paul inverts the order of the clauses so that τὸν οὐ λαόν μου comes first to suit the Gentiles, and substitutes καλέσω for ἐρῶ because καλέσω "naturally crept in from the ἐκάλεσεν of the previous verse"
4
. They also note that a habit had probably arisen in the early Church of quoting passages to prove the calling of the Gentiles, forming commonplaces that were later collected in written testimonia
4
.
3. B001-3: Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 [LXX 2:1]
Locator / Citation: Page 264
4
.
Expresses Uncertainty & Allows Authorial Adaptation: The authors point out that the only difference between Paul's quotation and the Septuagint is his insertion of ἐκεῖ (ἐκεῖ κληθήσονται υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος), noting that "this insertion seems to emphasize the idea of the place, and it is somewhat difficult to understand what place is intended"
4
.
Acknowledges Competing Interpretations:
Palestinian / Jewish Eschatology: If original, ἐκεῖ refers to Palestine/Jerusalem and the gathering of nations there in a Messianic kingdom, showing that Paul "is often strongly influenced by the language and even the ideas of Jewish eschatology"
4
.
Universal Interpretation: If neglecting the original context, ἐκεῖ refers spiritually to "the whole world" wherever Gentiles suffer reproach
4
.

Allows Re-application / Transfer of Old Testament Title: The authors state that the OT formula for calling on Jehovah (ἐπικαλεῖσθαι τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου) is transferred christologically by Paul to Jesus Christ (Κύριος), converting a prophetic description of the "day of the Lord" into a designation for Christian worshippers

. They cite A. Seeberg's treatise Die Anbetung des Herrn bei Paulus regarding prayer to Christ

6. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13
Locator / Citation: Page 340

Disagrees with Other Scholars: The authors explicitly refute "many commentators" (specifically citing Canon Liddon) who attempted to interpret the doxological prepositions in verse 36 (ἐξ αὐτοῦ, διʼ αὐτοῦ, εἰς αὐτόν) as references to the distinct persons of the Trinity
7
. Sanday and Headlam state that "(1) the prepositions do not suit this interpretation... (2) The whole argument refers to a different line of thought," addressing the Godhead as a whole
