# S01F — Counterevidence and Disagreements

**Source:** James W. Aageson, *Paul's Use of Scripture: A Comparative Study of Biblical Interpretation in Early Palestinian Judaism and the New Testament With Special Reference to Romans 9–11* (D.Phil. thesis, Oxford University, 1983/1984).  
**Source Class:** SECONDARY  
**Mode:** SOURCE-BOUND EXTRACTION

This document extracts every instance where the author expresses uncertainty, discusses competing textual forms, notes Hebrew/Greek ambiguities, allows authorial adaptation or pre-existing traditions, disagrees with other scholars, or acknowledges evidence that weakens the preferred interpretation.

---

## 1. B001-1: Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6
- **Uncertainty / Quotation from Memory:** Aageson explicitly notes that between Romans 4:3 and Galatians 3:6, the word order of ἐπίστευσεν and Ἀβραάμ is transposed, allowing that "the possibility cannot be ruled out that Paul in some cases may have quoted from memory" without mechanical precision (p. 83).
- **Competing Textual Forms & Authorial Adaptation:** The particle δέ is included in Romans 4:3 (Ἐπίστευσεν δὲ Ἀβραάμ) but absent in Galatians 3:6 (Ἀβραὰμ ἐπίστευσεν), neither matching LXX Genesis 15:6 which lacks δέ. Aageson classifies this as an authorial syntactical adaptation designed to integrate the quote smoothly into the letter context (p. 83).
- **Scholarly Disagreements:** Aageson contrasts Paul’s sequential reading of Genesis 15:6 (faith reckoned prior to circumcision in Gen 17) with traditional Tannaitic and Second Temple Jewish interpretations (e.g., 1 Maccabees 2:52, Jubilees) which harmonized Genesis 15:6 with Abraham's ongoing obedience and faithfulness under testing (pp. 131, 159–160).
- **Weakening Evidence:** Aageson acknowledges that Paul's selective focus on chronological sequence isolates Genesis 15:6 from its broader Pentateuchal narrative context where circumcision is explicitly commanded as an eternal covenant (p. 131).

## 2. B001-2: Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 (LXX 2:25)
- **Competing Textual Forms:** Aageson discusses the major variant between Codex Vaticanus (LXX-B), which reads forms of ἀγαπάω (ἠγαπημένην), and Codex Alexandrinus (LXX-A), which reads forms of ἐλεέω (ἠλεημένην). Paul’s agreement with LXX-B illustrates that he utilized distinct Greek textual traditions rather than a uniform recension (p. 69 n. 19).
- **Authorial Adaptation vs. Unknown Textual Form:** Paul substitutes καλέσω for ἐρῶ. Aageson acknowledges the competing possibility that Paul used an unknown Greek manuscript, but concludes that Paul intentionally made the substitution to forge a verbal catchword connection (gezerah shawah) with κληθήσονται in Hosea 1:10 (Rom 9:26) and ἐκάλεσεν in Romans 9:24 (p. 79, 178).
- **Hebrew / Greek Ambiguity:** In the MT, Hosea 2:25 reads וְרִחַמְתִּי אֶת־לֹא רֻחָמָה (root *rḥm*, tender mercy/compassion). The Septuagint translated this with ἀγαπάω (LXX-B) or ἐλεέω (LXX-A). Aageson notes this divergence as reflecting differing translational representations of the underlying Hebrew (p. 69).
- **Scholarly Disagreements:** Aageson engages C.H. Dodd’s testimonia hypothesis, arguing that the catchword alignment in Romans 9:25–26 reflects Paul’s active redactional composition in Romans rather than a static pre-formed Christian florilegium (pp. 115–116, 178).

## 3. B001-3: Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 (LXX 2:1)
- **Competing Textual Forms:** Romans 9:26 agrees verbatim with Codex Alexandrinus (LXX-A), which reads καὶ ἔσται ἐν τῷ τόπῳ οὗ ἐρρέθη αὐτοῖς, οὐ λαός μου ὑμεῖς, ἐκεῖ κληθήσονται υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος. Codex Vaticanus (LXX-B) omits ἐκεῖ and adds καὶ αὐτοί. Aageson highlights this as clear evidence that Paul’s text aligned with an LXX-A type against LXX-B (p. 69 n. 19).
- **Uncertainty / Harmonization Possibility:** Aageson acknowledges the methodological difficulty that LXX-A could theoretically have suffered later Christian scribal harmonization toward the New Testament text, though he maintains that the reading represents an authentic pre-Christian Greek text-type (p. 65).
- **Authorial Adaptation in Context:** Paul shifts the original historical reference of Hosea (Israel’s northern tribes restored to covenant) to apply directly to the calling of Gentile believers (p. 178).

## 4. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16
- **Competing Textual Forms:** Paul includes ἐπʼ αὐτῷ in both Romans 9:33 and 10:11, aligning with LXX-A and Codex Sinaiticus, whereas LXX-B omits ἐπʼ αὐτῷ (p. 69 n. 19).
- **Authorial Adaptation / Double Citation Variation:** In Romans 9:33, Paul quotes ὁ πιστεύων without πᾶς; in Romans 10:11, he quotes Πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων. Aageson argues that the insertion of πᾶς was probably motivated by catchword association with πᾶς in Joel 2:32 (Rom 10:13) (p. 83).
- **Scholarly Disagreements:** Aageson disputes C.K. Barrett's view that the "stone of stumbling" refers primarily to the Torah, maintaining with Cranfield that Paul identifies the stone christologically with Jesus (pp. 116–117).
- **Uncertainty Regarding Pre-Pauline Testimonia:** Aageson considers whether the combination of Isaiah 28:16 and Isaiah 8:14 in Romans 9:33 was already a fixed early Christian "stone testimony" (as in 1 Peter 2:6–8), but notes that Paul’s specific verbal tailoring in Romans 10:11 exhibits independent authorial handling (pp. 116–118).

## 5. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5)
- **Hebrew / Greek Ambiguity (Kyrios Title):** In MT Joel 3:5, the divine name is YHWH (בְּשֵׁם יְהוָה). The Septuagint renders this τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου. Paul exploits the Greek Kyrios to refer the passage directly to Jesus as the risen Lord of Romans 10:9 (pp. 125–126).
- **Alternative Explanations (Pentecost Tradition):** Aageson explicitly examines the alternative that Joel 2:32 entered Christian usage via the Pentecost tradition (as reflected in Acts 2:21). He demonstrates that Paul isolates the catchwords πᾶς and ἐπικαλέομαι to establish universal access to salvation, showing distinct Pauline exegetical purpose (pp. 125–126).

## 6. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13
- **Competing Textual Forms:** Paul reads σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ with LXX-A, whereas LXX-B transposes the words to αὐτοῦ σύμβουλος (p. 69 n. 19).
- **Authorial Adaptation across Epistles:** In Romans 11:34, Paul quotes Isaiah 40:13a–b (τίς ἔγνω νοῦν κυρίου; ἢ τίς σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο;); in 1 Corinthians 2:16, he quotes Isaiah 40:13a, c (τίς γὰρ ἔγνω νοῦν κυρίου, ὃς συμβιβάσει αὐτόν;). Aageson notes this as clear proof that Paul freely excerpted different clauses from the same Old Testament passage to fit distinct epistolary arguments (p. 84).
- **Hebrew / Greek Divergence:** MT Isaiah 40:13 reads מִי־תִכֵּן אֶת־רוּחַ יְהוָה ("Who has directed the Spirit of the LORD"). The Septuagint translated רוּחַ as νοῦν ("mind"), allowing Paul to develop his theological discussion around the "mind of the Lord" and "mind of Christ" (pp. 72, 84).

## 7. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10
- **Authorial Stylistic Omission:** Paul omits the introductory temporal phrase ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ from Isaiah 11:10 LXX. Aageson notes this omission as a typical stylistic truncation that does not alter the sense but allows the citation to fit directly into the catena (pp. 74–76, 91 n. 16).
- **Alternative Explanations (Pre-Pauline Catena):** Aageson considers whether the entire four-verse catena in Romans 15:9–12 was adopted intact from pre-Pauline Jewish-Christian liturgy, concluding that Paul’s overarching theme of Gentile mission makes authorial composition via the catchword ἔθνη equally plausible (pp. 107–108).

---

## 8. B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14
- **Competing Textual Forms:** Paul reads ἀθετήσω in place of LXX κρύψω (MT וְתִסְתַּתָּר). Aageson notes that Paul’s reading may reflect a variant Greek translation, quotation from memory, or an intentional substitution to provide a stronger antithesis to the wisdom of the world (pp. 79, 91–92).
- **Scholarly Disagreements:** Aageson discusses E. Earle Ellis’s proposal that 1 Corinthians 1:18–31 represents an adopted pre-Pauline Jewish-Christian homiletic midrash. Aageson emphasizes that regardless of provenance, the quotation functions constructively in generating the vocabulary and structure of the passage (p. 92).

---

## 9. B001-9: 1 Corinthians 6:16 / Genesis 2:24
- **Competing Textual Forms (Double Citation):** In 1 Corinthians 6:16, Paul inserts γάρ, φησίν; in Ephesians 5:31, this formula is absent and αὐτοῦ is omitted. Aageson points out that the quotation in 1 Corinthians conforms strictly to the LXX reading with the sole addition of the parenthetical formula for epistolary syntax (p. 83).
- **Scholarly Disagreements / Hermeneutical Shift:** Aageson notes that Paul applies a narrative creation text (haggadah) as a normative ethical rule (halakah) prohibiting sexual intercourse with a harlot, which represents a hermeneutical transition characteristic of early Jewish exegesis (p. 106 n. 85).

---

## 10. B001-10: 1 Corinthians 9:9 / Deuteronomy 25:4
- **Competing Textual Forms:** Paul writes οὐ κημώσεις, whereas the standard LXX text reads οὐ φιμώσεις. Aageson identifies this as a recognized manuscript variant in Greek Old Testament transmission (p. 68 n. 15).
- **Scholarly Disagreements / Typology vs. Analogy:** Aageson rejects the notion that Paul is allegorizing away the literal sense or employing formal historical typology. Instead, he demonstrates that Paul employs the rabbinic rule of qal wahomer (a fortiori reasoning): if God cares for working oxen, how much more for apostolic workers (p. 148).
- **Uncertainty / Proverbial Origin:** Aageson acknowledges the possibility that the command had already circulated as a proverbial maxim regarding laborers before Paul applied it (p. 148).

---

## 11. B001-11: 1 Corinthians 10:7 / Exodus 32:6
- **Scholarly Disagreements (Goppelt Typology):** Aageson explicitly challenges Leonhard Goppelt’s typological framework. While Goppelt argues that 1 Corinthians 10 is fundamentally redemptive-historical typology with historical escalation, Aageson demonstrates that the terms τύπος and τυπικῶς in 10:6–11 mean "warning" or "instructive moral example" within a midrashic parenesis against idolatry (p. 154).
- **Weakening Evidence:** Aageson acknowledges that 1 Corinthians 10:1–4 contains elements that could be construed as sacramental typology (baptism into Moses, spiritual rock), but insists that the quotation of Exodus 32:6 in 10:7 functions strictly as an ethical warning against repeating Israel's wilderness failures (p. 154).

---

## 12. B001-12: Galatians 3:13 / Deuteronomy 21:23
- **Authorial / Traditional Omission:** Paul omits ὑπὸ θεοῦ from Deuteronomy 21:23 LXX (κεκατηραμένος ὑπὸ θεοῦ). Aageson stresses that it is "likely that they were omitted in order to avoid presenting an unacceptable image of the divine" (implying that Christ was accursed by God the Father) (p. 79).
- **Uncertainty Regarding Agency:** Aageson emphasizes that it cannot be determined with certainty whether Paul himself executed this theological omission or whether he inherited a pre-existing Jewish-Christian apologetic testimony where the words had already been dropped (p. 79).
- **Hebrew / Qumran Context:** Aageson notes that early Jewish literature (such as the Temple Scroll 11QT 64:6–13) shows active theological engagement with the hanging curse of Deuteronomy 21:22–23, confirming that the theological sensitivity surrounding this text was widespread in Palestinian Judaism (p. 34, 79).
