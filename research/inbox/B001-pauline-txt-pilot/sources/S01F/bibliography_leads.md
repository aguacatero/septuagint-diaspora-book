# S01F — Bibliography Leads

**Source:** James W. Aageson, *Paul's Use of Scripture: A Comparative Study of Biblical Interpretation in Early Palestinian Judaism and the New Testament With Special Reference to Romans 9–11* (D.Phil. thesis, Oxford University, 1983/1984).  
**Source Class:** SECONDARY  
**Mode:** SOURCE-BOUND EXTRACTION

This document itemizes the primary scholarly sources cited in Aageson's footnotes and bibliography that directly bear upon the textual and exegetical problems of the twelve B001 target passages.

---

## 1. B001-1: Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6
- **E. Earle Ellis**, *Paul's Use of the Old Testament* (Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 1957), pp. 16–17, 82–84.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for categorizing double citations in Paul. Ellis demonstrates that minor variations like the transposition of ἐπίστευσεν and Ἀβραάμ between Rom 4:3 and Gal 3:6 do not alter the semantic sense but reflect minor quotational inconsistency.
- **C.E.B. Cranfield**, *A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans*, Vol. I, ICC (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1975), pp. 226ff.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for exegetical analysis of Romans 4. Cranfield shows how Paul isolates the chronological sequence of Genesis 15:6 (faith reckoned as righteousness) prior to Genesis 17 (circumcision) to argue that Gentile justification is independent of the law.
- **Ernst Käsemann**, "The Faith of Abraham in Romans 4" in *Perspectives on Paul*, trans. Margaret Kohl (London: SCM Press, 1971), pp. 79–101.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon to demonstrate that Paul’s use of Genesis 15:6 extracts a theological principle of faith-righteousness vs. works-righteousness that runs counter to traditional Tannaitic interpretations of Abraham’s merit.

---

## 2. B001-2: Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 (LXX 2:25)
- **Dietrich-Alex Koch**, *Die Schrift als Zeuge des Evangeliums: Untersuchungen zur Verwendung und zum Verständnis der Schrift bei Paulus* (Tübingen: J.C.B. Mohr [Paul Siebeck], 1980); and "Beobachtungen zum christologischen Schriftgebrauch in den vorpaulinischen Gemeinden," *ZNW* 71 (1980): 174–191.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for text-critical alignment. Koch notes that Paul follows LXX-B (ἀγαπήσω / ἠγαπημένην) rather than LXX-A (ἐλεήσω / ἠλεημένην), illustrating Paul's reliance on specific Greek manuscript branches.
- **E. Earle Ellis**, *Paul's Use of the Old Testament* (1957), pp. 81, 140.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon to explain textual substitution. Ellis shows that Paul replaced ἐρῶ with καλέσω to create a deliberate verbal catchword link (gezerah shawah) with κληθήσονται in Hosea 1:10 and ἐκάλεσεν in Romans 9:24.
- **C.H. Dodd**, *According to the Scriptures: The Sub-Structure of New Testament Theology* (London: Nisbet & Co., 1952), pp. 41–43.
  - *Why it matters:* Cited regarding the early Christian testimonia tradition, where Hosea 2:23 and 1:10 were clustered as prophetic proofs for the calling of the Gentiles.

---

## 3. B001-3: Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 (LXX 2:1)
- **Dietrich-Alex Koch**, *Die Schrift als Zeuge des Evangeliums* (1980), pp. 65–70.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon to establish that Romans 9:26 aligns verbatim with Codex Alexandrinus (LXX-A), which includes ἐκεῖ and omits καὶ αὐτοί.
- **Barnabas Lindars**, *New Testament Apologetic: The Doctrinal Significance of the Old Testament Quotations* (London: SCM Press, 1961), pp. 37–38, 244–245.
  - *Why it matters:* Cited for the early church's apologetic application of Hosea 1:10 to Gentile converts as "sons of the living God."

---

## 4. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16
- **C.H. Dodd**, *According to the Scriptures* (1952), pp. 41–43; and **J. Rendel Harris**, *Testimonies*, 2 vols. (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1916, 1920).
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon regarding the composite "stone" text (combining Isa 28:16 and Isa 8:14 in Rom 9:33 and 10:11). Dodd and Harris argue for a pre-Pauline Christian florilegium/testimony collection.
- **C.K. Barrett**, "Romans 9:30–10:21: Fall and Responsibility of Israel" in *Essays On Paul* (London: SPCK, 1982), pp. 132–153.
  - *Why it matters:* Disputed in part. Barrett argues that the "stone of stumbling" in Rom 9:32–33 / 10:11 refers primarily to the Law; Aageson argues alongside Cranfield that Paul intended the stone to refer christologically to Jesus.
- **C.E.B. Cranfield**, "Some Notes on Romans 9:30–33" in *Jesus und Paulus*, eds. E.E. Ellis and E. Grässer (Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 1975), pp. 35–43.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon to explain why Paul inserted πᾶς in Rom 10:11 (Πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων...) after omitting it in Rom 9:33—namely, due to verbal catchword influence from Joel 2:32 (πᾶς) in Rom 10:13.

---

## 5. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5)
- **Barnabas Lindars**, *New Testament Apologetic* (1961), pp. 37–38.
  - *Why it matters:* Cited for comparative usage. Lindars notes that while Luke in Acts 2:21 uses Joel 2:32 as an eschatological prophecy of the Spirit at Pentecost, Paul isolates πᾶς and ἐπικαλέομαι as exegetical catchwords to prove universal salvation for both Jews and Greeks.
- **C.E.B. Cranfield**, *A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans*, Vol. II (1979), pp. 531ff.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for exegesis of ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου, showing how Paul applies the divine Old Testament title Kyrios directly to the risen Jesus.

---

## 6. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13
- **E. Earle Ellis**, *Paul's Use of the Old Testament* (1957), pp. 13, 69, 84.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon to establish that Paul’s word order σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ in both Rom 11:34 and 1 Cor 2:16 follows LXX-A against LXX-B (αὐτοῦ σύμβουλος), and to show how Paul quotes different clauses of the same verse in different letters.
- **Anthony Tyrrell Hanson**, *Studies in Paul's Technique and Theology* (London: SPCK, 1974), pp. 120–125.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for analyzing the doxological and midrashic context of Romans 11:33–36.

---

## 7. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10
- **Dietrich-Alex Koch**, "Beobachtungen zum christologischen Schriftgebrauch in den vorpaulinischen Gemeinden," *ZNW* 71 (1980): 174–191.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for identifying Isa 11:10 as part of a pre-Pauline christological catena utilized in early Gentile mission contexts.
- **C.E.B. Cranfield**, *A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans*, Vol. II (1979), pp. 750ff.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon to explain the midrashic stringing technique (charaz) in Rom 15:9–12, where four passages are chained together via the catchword ἔθνη.

---

## 8. B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14
- **E. Earle Ellis**, "Exegetical Patterns in I Corinthians and Romans" in *Prophecy and Hermeneutic in Early Christianity* (Tübingen: J.C.B. Mohr [Paul Siebeck], 1978), pp. 213–220.
  - *Why it matters:* Discussed extensively. Ellis proposes that 1 Cor 1:18–31 was an independent Jewish-Christian midrashic unit adopted by Paul; Aageson notes that even if pre-Pauline, Isa 29:14 functions as the active verbal and thematic generator of Paul's discourse on divine wisdom vs. worldly foolishness.
- **Hans Conzelmann**, *1 Corinthians*, Hermeneia (Philadelphia: Fortress Press, 1975), pp. 48ff.
  - *Why it matters:* Cited for textual and structural analysis of Paul’s replacement of LXX κρύψω with ἀθετήσω in 1 Cor 1:19.

---

## 9. B001-9: 1 Corinthians 6:16 / Genesis 2:24
- **E. Earle Ellis**, *Paul's Use of the Old Testament* (1957), pp. 70–71, 83–84.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for double-citation analysis, comparing 1 Cor 6:16 with Eph 5:31 to prove that insertion of γάρ, φησίν in 1 Cor 6:16 was purely for epistolary sentence integration.
- **David Daube**, *The New Testament and Rabbinic Judaism* (London: Athlone Press, 1956), pp. 67ff.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for hermeneutical background on how 1st-century Jewish exegesis frequently transformed narrative creation accounts (haggadah) into normative behavioral rules (halakah).

---

## 10. B001-10: 1 Corinthians 9:9 / Deuteronomy 25:4
- **A.E. Harvey**, "'The Workman is Worthy of His Hire': Fortunes of a Proverb in the Early Church," *Novum Testamentum* 24 (1982): 209–221.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon to contextualize Paul's use of Deut 25:4 ("you shall not muzzle an ox") within early Christian debates over an apostle's right to financial support.
- **G.M. Lee**, "Studies in Texts: I Cor. 9:9-10," *Theology* 71 (1968): 122–123.
  - *Why it matters:* Cited for the lexical variant κημώσεις (printed in 1 Cor 9:9 in place of LXX φιμώσεις).
- **Joseph Bonsirven**, *Exégèse rabbinique et exégèse paulinienne* (Paris: Beauchesne, 1939), pp. 88ff.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for identifying Paul’s use of Hillel’s first rule (qal wahomer / a fortiori reasoning: if God cares for oxen, how much more for human ministers).

---

## 11. B001-11: 1 Corinthians 10:7 / Exodus 32:6
- **Wayne A. Meeks**, "'And Rose Up to Play': Midrash and Paraenesis in I Corinthians 10:1-22," *Journal for the Study of the New Testament* 16 (1982): 64–78.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon to show that 1 Cor 10:1–13 is a midrashic parenesis where Israel's wilderness story (Exod 32:6) functions as an ethical warning against idolatry and pagan meals.
- **Leonhard Goppelt**, *Typos: The Typological Interpretation of the Old Testament in the New*, trans. Donald H. Madvig (Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 1982), pp. 129–136, 223–225.
  - *Why it matters:* Disputed in part. Goppelt argues that 1 Cor 10:1–13 is salvation-historical typology; Aageson argues that τύπος here means a moral "warning" or "instructive example," not redemptive-historical typology.

---

## 12. B001-12: Galatians 3:13 / Deuteronomy 21:23
- **Hans Dieter Betz**, *Galatians: A Commentary on Paul's Letter to the Churches in Galatia*, Hermeneia (Philadelphia: Fortress Press, 1979), pp. 148ff.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon regarding text modification in Gal 3:13. Betz and Aageson emphasize that the omission of ὑπὸ θεοῦ from LXX Deut 21:23 (κεκατηραμένος ὑπὸ θεοῦ) was an intentional theological adjustment to avoid implying that Christ was accursed by God the Father.
- **Ernest De Witt Burton**, *A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Epistle to the Galatians*, ICC (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1921), pp. 168ff.
  - *Why it matters:* Cited for grammatical and text-critical analysis of Paul's citation formulas and omissions in Galatians 3–4.
- **E.P. Sanders**, *Paul, the Law, and the Jewish People* (Philadelphia: Fortress Press, 1983), pp. 21–23.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon to analyze the terminological character and midrashic structure of the curse catena in Galatians 3:6–14.
