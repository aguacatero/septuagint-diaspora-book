## 1. B001-1: Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** Paul transposes the words ἐπίστευσεν and Ἀβραάμ between Romans 4:3 and Galatians 3:6; this word-order variation does not affect the semantic sense of the texts (p. 83). Furthermore, Paul's emphasis on the literary sequence of the Genesis narrative enables him to use scripture to support his distinction between righteousness by faith and circumcision (p. 131).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Paul believed he was uncovering the true, intrinsic meaning of scripture, operating under the assumption that literary narrative sequence in Genesis was intentionally ordered (p. 131).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Aageson explicitly notes that "the possibility cannot be ruled out that Paul in some cases may have quoted from memory" to explain minor variations in word order (p. 83).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** Why does the word order of Genesis 15:6 differ between Romans 4:3 and Galatians 3:6, and how does Paul employ this passage to construct his argument?
- **EVIDENCE:** In Romans 4:3, the Greek reads Ἐπίστευσεν δὲ Ἀβραὰμ τῷ θεῷ, whereas Galatians 3:6 reads Ἀβραὰμ ἐπίστευσεν. In Genesis, chapter 15:6 (reckoning faith as righteousness) precedes chapter 17 (institution of circumcision).
- **INFERENCE:** The word-order transposition between Romans 4:3 and Galatians 3:6 is a minor quotational variation without semantic consequence. Paul relies on the chronological sequence of the Genesis text to establish priority.
- **CONCLUSION:** Paul utilizes the narrative sequence of Genesis 15:6 in Romans 4:3 to establish a fundamental theological distinction between righteousness by faith and physical circumcision (pp. 83, 131).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** The variation could reflect an uncollated pre-Pauline Greek Old Testament manuscript tradition containing a variant word order for Genesis 15:6.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** The argument would be weakened if contemporary 1st-century Jewish exegesis demonstrated that Genesis 15:6 and Genesis 17 were treated as topical or non-chronological rather than sequentially historical.

## 2. B001-2: Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 (LXX 2:25)

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** Paul and Codex Vaticanus (LXX-B) share forms of ἀγαπᾶν (ἠγαπημένην), whereas Codex Alexandrinus (LXX-A) uses forms of ἐλεεῖν (ἠλεημένην) (p. 69 n. 19). The substitution of καλέσω for ἐρῶ in Romans 9:25 resulted in a verbal connection in Paul's text between the two Hosea passages in 9:25–26 not found in the LXX, probably motivated by the influence of κληθήσονται in Hosea 2:1 (1:10) and divine calling in Romans 9:24 (pp. 81, 178).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Paul intentionally modified the scriptural text to conform it to the context and thematic argument of his epistle (pp. 79, 81, 178).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Aageson notes the general possibility of quotation from memory or reliance on variant vorlagen, but concludes intentional catchword harmonization is the probable cause (pp. 81, 83).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul substitute καλέσω for ἐρῶ in Hosea 2:23 and follow the love terminology of LXX-B rather than LXX-A?
- **EVIDENCE:** LXX-B reads forms of ἀγαπάω (matching Rom 9:25), whereas LXX-A reads forms of ἐλεέω. In LXX Hosea 2:25, the verb is ἐρῶ, whereas Paul writes καλέσω.
- **INFERENCE:** Paul followed an LXX-B text type for vocabulary and deliberately substituted καλέσω for ἐρῶ to forge a verbal catchword link with κληθήσονται in Hosea 1:10 (Rom 9:26) and ἐκάλεσεν in Romans 9:24.
- **CONCLUSION:** Paul intentionally modified Hosea 2:23 to link the Hosea citations together and ground the divine calling of the Gentiles in scriptural terminology (pp. 69 n. 19, 81, 178).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** Paul may have adopted an already existing Jewish-Christian testimonia collection in which Hosea 2:23 and Hosea 1:10 had been combined and modified prior to his writing.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Discovery of a pre-Pauline Septuagint manuscript of Hosea 2:23 containing the reading καλέσω would refute the claim of Pauline intentional modification.

## 3. B001-3: Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 (LXX 2:1)

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** Paul's text in Romans 9:26 agrees verbatim with Codex Alexandrinus (LXX-A) against LXX-B (pp. 69–70 n. 19). The phrase υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος in 9:26 functions as a direct verbal link with υἱῶν Ἰσραήλ in the juxtaposed quotation of Isaiah 10:22 in Romans 9:27 (p. 179).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Paul utilized specific text-types or manuscript traditions that aligned with his theological and literary objectives (p. 69).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Aageson distinguishes between variations arising from textual vorlagen and those resulting from Paul's own editorial agency (pp. 68–71).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** Which textual tradition does Paul follow in Hosea 1:10, and how does he link it with neighboring citations?
- **EVIDENCE:** LXX-A prefixes ἐκεῖ to κληθήσονται and omits καὶ αὐτοί, matching Romans 9:26 verbatim against LXX-B. Romans 9:26 concludes with υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος, while Romans 9:27 begins with υἱῶν Ἰσραήλ.
- **INFERENCE:** Paul utilized an LXX-A text-type and forged a verbal catchword connection (υἱοί / υἱῶν) between Hosea 1:10 and Isaiah 10:22–23.
- **CONCLUSION:** Hosea 1:10 functions as a scriptural proof that Gentiles are called sons of the living God, integrated into a prophetic chain via catchword association (pp. 69–70 n. 19, 179).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** Later Christian scribes may have harmonized LXX-A to the text of Romans 9:26 during transmission.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Evidence that LXX-A readings in Hosea were systematically retroverted or conformed to the Pauline epistles would weaken the claim of Paul's reliance on an LXX-A vorlage.

## 4. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** In Romans 9:33 and 10:11, ἐπʼ αὐτῷ is included in Paul and LXX-A, but omitted in LXX-B (p. 69 n. 19). In Romans 10:11, Paul inserts the word πᾶς into Isaiah 28:16, an addition that probably came about as a result of the association of Isaiah 28:16 with Joel 3:5 (2:32) in Romans 10:13 (pp. 83, 183).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Verbal association between scriptural passages regularly prompted Paul to modify the wording of his citations (pp. 83, 183).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Lapse of memory or spontaneous oral quotation (p. 83).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul insert πᾶς into Isaiah 28:16 in Romans 10:11 when he omitted it in Romans 9:33?
- **EVIDENCE:** In Romans 9:33, Isaiah 28:16 is cited without πᾶς. In Romans 10:11, Paul adds πᾶς. In Romans 10:13, Paul cites Joel 2:32, which contains πᾶς. LXX-A includes ἐπʼ αὐτῷ (matching Rom 9:33 and 10:11 against LXX-B).
- **INFERENCE:** Paul followed an LXX-A text-type for ἐπʼ αὐτῷ. Catchword influence from Joel 2:32 prompted the insertion of πᾶς into Isaiah 28:16.
- **CONCLUSION:** Paul modified Isaiah 28:16 via catchword association with Joel 2:32 to emphasize universal salvation for both Jew and Gentile (pp. 69 n. 19, 83, 183).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** Paul may have inherited a liturgical or baptismal confession where πᾶς had already been prefixed to Isaiah 28:16.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Discovery of a pre-Christian Greek manuscript of Isaiah 28:16 containing πᾶς would refute the hypothesis of Pauline redaction.

## 5. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5)

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** The two words πᾶς and ἐπικαλέσασθαι are key words for Paul, and they assist him in developing an argument concerning the salvation of both Jews and Greeks (p. 126). In contrast to Acts 2:21 where Joel 2:32 is tied to the Pentecost event, Paul applies the passage to the contemporary availability of universal salvation (pp. 125–126).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Scriptural citations served both as proof-texts and as generators of thematic development in the epistles (pp. 91–92, 126).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Aageson examines whether Paul simply adopted an existing early Christian Pentecost tradition, concluding that Paul redirected the text away from Pentecost toward Jew-Gentile soteriology (pp. 125–126).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** How does Joel 2:32 function in Romans 10:13 compared to its usage in early church Pentecost traditions?
- **EVIDENCE:** Paul quotes Joel 3:5 LXX (πᾶς ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται...). The catchwords πᾶς and ἐπικαλέω link to Romans 10:11 (πᾶς) and Romans 10:12 (ἐπικαλουμένους). Acts 2:21 links Joel 2:32 to Pentecost.
- **INFERENCE:** Paul utilizes Joel 2:32 not as an eschatological Pentecost prophecy, but as an exegetical proof built on catchwords to establish universal salvation.
- **CONCLUSION:** Joel 2:32 establishes that divine salvation is open to Jew and Gentile alike on the condition of calling upon the Lord (pp. 125–126).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** The catchword combination of Isaiah 28:16 and Joel 2:32 may have originated in a Hellenistic synagogue lectionary tradition.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Evidence establishing that Joel 2:32 functioned exclusively as a Pentecost text in 1st-century Christian circles would challenge Aageson's view of Paul's independent thematic redirection.

## 6. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** Paul and LXX-A read σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ, whereas LXX-B transposes the terms (p. 69 n. 19). The two citations from Isaiah 40:13 in Romans 11:34 and 1 Corinthians 2:16 illustrate the way in which Paul has used different forms of the same passage to serve distinct epistolary contexts (p. 84).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Paul exercised significant freedom in isolating and citing different clauses from a single Old Testament passage (pp. 72, 84).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Careless quotation from memory (p. 84).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul quote different clauses of Isaiah 40:13 in Romans 11:34 and 1 Corinthians 2:16?
- **EVIDENCE:** In Romans 11:34, Paul quotes Isaiah 40:13a–b. In 1 Corinthians 2:16, he quotes Isaiah 40:13a, c. Both epistles read σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ following LXX-A against LXX-B.
- **INFERENCE:** Paul followed an LXX-A text type for word order and adapted distinct clauses of the prophetic text to suit different theological arguments.
- **CONCLUSION:** Isaiah 40:13 in Romans 11:34 provides scriptural confirmation for the unsearchable wisdom and sovereignty of God at the climax of his doxology (pp. 69 n. 19, 84).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** Romans 11:34 and 1 Corinthians 2:16 may have derived from two separate liturgical catenae circulating independently.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Proof that the two citations were fixed independent florilegia entries would undermine the view that Paul personally selected the clauses for each letter.

## 7. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** A verbal connection links the scriptural passages in Romans 15:9–12 and the opening statement centering on the catchword ἔθνη (p. 91 n. 16). The omission of ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ from Isaiah 11:10 is a minor stylistic variation designed to integrate the text smoothly into the catena (pp. 74–76).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Verbal tally (gezerah shawah) was a standard Jewish interpretive method used by Paul to construct scriptural chains (pp. 55, 91).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Aageson examines whether the catena was pre-Pauline, noting that even if pre-formed, its integration reflects deliberate catchword methodology (pp. 107–108).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** How is Isaiah 11:10 integrated into the scriptural catena in Romans 15:9–12?
- **EVIDENCE:** Isaiah 11:10 is quoted in Romans 15:12 without ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ. The catchword ἔθνη appears across Psalm 17:50 LXX, Deuteronomy 32:43 LXX, Psalm 116:1 LXX, and Isaiah 11:10 LXX.
- **INFERENCE:** The catena is linked via the catchword ἔθνη, with the temporal phrase omitted for smooth stylistic integration.
- **CONCLUSION:** Isaiah 11:10 grounds Gentile hope and inclusion in the messianic root of Jesse (pp. 74–76, 91 n. 16).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** The catena may have originated in a pre-Christian Greek synagogue liturgy celebrating proselyte inclusion.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Finding the exact four-verse catena in a pre-Christian Jewish text would demonstrate that Paul adopted the unit without composing it.

## 8. B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** The scriptural citation of Isaiah 29:14 is related not only to the opening statement but also contributes thematically and verbally to the discussion that follows in 1 Corinthians 1:20–25 (p. 92).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Scriptural texts functioned constructively in Paul's letters to generate vocabulary and thematic concepts (p. 92).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** E. Earle Ellis's thesis that 1 Corinthians 1:18–31 represents an adopted Jewish-Christian midrash (p. 92).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** What role does Isaiah 29:14 play in the structure and thematic development of 1 Corinthians 1:18–25?
- **EVIDENCE:** Citation of Isaiah 29:14 in 1 Corinthians 1:19 is followed immediately by elaboration using σοφία and σοφῶν in 1:20–25.
- **INFERENCE:** The citation functions as an active thematic catalyst rather than an incidental proof-text.
- **CONCLUSION:** Isaiah 29:14 provides the scriptural basis for Paul's argument that God destroys human wisdom through the cross (p. 92).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** Paul may have added the citation after drafting the surrounding argument as an illustrative afterthought.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Showing that 1 Corinthians 1:18–25 relies on Hellenistic diatribe conventions independent of Isaiah 29:14 would weaken the catalytic claim.

## 9. B001-9: 1 Corinthians 6:16 / Genesis 2:24

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** Within the shared core quotation of Genesis 2:24, the only difference between 1 Corinthians 6:16 and Ephesians 5:31 is that the introductory formula γάρ, φησίν has been included for syntactical reasons in 1 Corinthians (pp. 82–83). In four instances, narrative passages are used in contexts pertaining to ethics, including 1 Corinthians 6:16 citing Genesis 2:24 (p. 114 n. 120).
- **Corpus Qualification:** Ephesians 5:31 is cited by Aageson as a comparative textual witness; in accordance with `PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md`, Ephesians is categorized strictly as an outside-primary-corpus comparator.
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Narrative creation traditions could be applied halakically by Paul to establish binding moral standards (p. 114).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Non-Pauline authorship of Ephesians as an explanation for stylistic divergences (p. 83).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** How does Paul integrate Genesis 2:24 in 1 Corinthians 6:16 compared to Ephesians 5:31?
- **EVIDENCE:** 1 Corinthians 6:16 quotes the core clause Ἔσονται γάρ, φησίν, οἱ δύο εἰς σάρκα μίαν with parenthetical formula. Ephesians 5:31 quotes the fuller passage without the formula. In 1 Corinthians, it regulates sexual conduct with a harlot.
- **INFERENCE:** The introductory formula is inserted strictly for syntactical integration. Genesis 2:24 is applied halakically to govern sexual ethics.
- **CONCLUSION:** Genesis 2:24 functions as a halakic ethical command showing that sexual intercourse creates a binding physical union (pp. 82–83, 114 n. 120).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** The citation in 1 Corinthians 6:16 may have been an Corinthian slogan that Paul took up and qualified.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Proof that Genesis 2:24 was never used halakically in Second Temple Judaism would challenge the continuity of Paul's ethical method with Jewish practice.

## 10. B001-10: 1 Corinthians 9:9 / Deuteronomy 25:4

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** The context of Deuteronomy 25:4 in 1 Corinthians concerns Paul's apostolic right to receive material support, and it is to this situation that Paul has applied the scriptural citation (p. 148). The variant reading κημώσεις (for LXX φιμώσεις) is a recognized manuscript variant in Greek transmission (p. 68 n. 15).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Paul's interpretation relies on contemporary Jewish hermeneutical principles (a fortiori / qal wahomer inference) rather than arbitrary allegory (p. 148).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Pure allegorization that denies the literal sense of the law (p. 148).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** How does Paul justify applying Deuteronomy 25:4 (muzzling oxen) to apostolic financial support?
- **EVIDENCE:** Citation of Deuteronomy 25:4 with variant κημώσεις. Rhetorical questions in 1 Corinthians 9:9–10 ("Is it for oxen that God cares?").
- **INFERENCE:** Paul uses a fortiori exegetical reasoning to extend a Mosaic agricultural law to human laborers.
- **CONCLUSION:** Deuteronomy 25:4 establishes the principle that Christian ministers have a right to material support (pp. 68 n. 15, 148).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** Paul may have adopted a Cynic-Stoic philosophical diatribe proverb regarding work and sustenance.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Evidence that 1st-century Jewish legal interpreters categorically barred extending agricultural animal laws to human compensation would challenge Aageson's contextualization.

## 11. B001-11: 1 Corinthians 10:7 / Exodus 32:6

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** The use of τύπος and τυπικῶς in the sense of 'warning' or 'example' in 1 Corinthians 10:6–13 provides little support for the view that Paul's use of scripture is fundamentally typological; it is ethical and parenetic (p. 154).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Historical-redemptive typology is rare in Paul and inadequate as an overarching description of his interpretive method (pp. 151–155).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Leonhard Goppelt's thesis that Paul's hermeneutic in 1 Corinthians 10 is fundamentally redemptive-historical typology (pp. 151–154).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** Is Paul's use of Exodus 32:6 in 1 Corinthians 10:7 typological or parenetic?
- **EVIDENCE:** Citation of Exodus 32:6 framed by τυπικῶς and νουθεσία in 1 Corinthians 10:6, 11.
- **INFERENCE:** τύπος here denotes an instructive moral warning rather than a prophetic historical type.
- **CONCLUSION:** Exodus 32:6 functions as a midrashic parenetic warning against idolatry and participation in pagan meals (p. 154).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** The wilderness motif in 1 Corinthians 10:1–13 may reflect an early Christian baptismal catechism.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Proof that Second Temple texts used τύπος exclusively for salvation-historical typological escalation would undermine Aageson's parenetic reading.

## 12. B001-12: Galatians 3:13 / Deuteronomy 21:23

### AAGESON EXPLICIT STATEMENT (Source-Grounded)
- **Author's Direct Claims:** In Galatians 3:13, Paul's text omits the words ὑπὸ θεοῦ from Deuteronomy 21:23; it is likely that they were omitted in order to avoid presenting an unacceptable image of the divine (p. 79).
- **Qualifications Explicitly Stated by Author:** Aageson explicitly notes: "Although we cannot be certain that Paul was first responsible for the omission of these words, it is likely that they were omitted in order to avoid presenting an unacceptable image of the divine" (p. 79).
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged by Author:** Theological and christological concerns actively motivated textual modification and paraphrastic quotation in early Jewish and Pauline interpretation (pp. 34, 79).
- **Alternative Explanations Directly Considered by Author:** Pre-Pauline Jewish-Christian testimony or vorlage with the words already omitted (p. 79).

### REVIEWER STRUCTURAL PARAPHRASE
- **QUESTION:** Why is ὑπὸ θεοῦ omitted from Deuteronomy 21:23 in Galatians 3:13?
- **EVIDENCE:** LXX Deuteronomy 21:23 reads κεκατηραμένος ὑπὸ θεοῦ. Galatians 3:13 reads Ἐπικατάρατος πᾶς ὁ κρεμάμενος ἐπὶ ξύλου (omitting ὑπὸ θεοῦ).
- **INFERENCE:** The omission safeguards divine integrity by avoiding the implication that Christ was directly cursed by God.
- **CONCLUSION:** Paul or his inherited tradition intentionally modified Deuteronomy 21:23 to protect theological sensibilities while maintaining that Christ bore the curse (p. 79).

### REVIEWER-GENERATED ALTERNATIVES & FALSIFICATION TESTS
- **Researcher-Generated Counterfactuals (Unaddressed Alternatives):** Paul was relying on a Hebrew or Aramaic Targumic tradition (such as 11QTemple) that interpreted the curse differently.
- **Researcher-Generated Falsification Tests:** Discovery of a pre-Christian Greek manuscript of Deuteronomy 21:23 lacking ὑπὸ θεοῦ would demonstrate that the omission was not motivated by Christian theological considerations.
