# S01F — Argument Map

**Source:** James W. Aageson, *Paul's Use of Scripture: A Comparative Study of Biblical Interpretation in Early Palestinian Judaism and the New Testament With Special Reference to Romans 9–11* (D.Phil. thesis, Oxford University, 1983/1984).  
**Source Class:** SECONDARY  
**Mode:** SOURCE-BOUND ARGUMENT MAP

This map systematically reconstructs the author's reasoning across all twelve B001 items, explicitly distinguishing **Structural Paraphrase** (the analytical reconstruction of the logical chain) from **Explicit Source Statements** (the claims, assumptions, alternative explanations, and falsification criteria directly stated in the thesis text).

---

## 1. B001-1: Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** Why does the word order of Genesis 15:6 differ between Romans 4:3 and Galatians 3:6, and how does Paul employ this passage to construct his argument?
- **EVIDENCE:** In Romans 4:3, the Greek reads Ἐπίστευσεν δὲ Ἀβραὰμ τῷ θεῷ, whereas Galatians 3:6 reads Ἀβραὰμ ἐπίστευσεν. In the narrative sequence of Genesis, chapter 15:6 (reckoning faith as righteousness) precedes chapter 17 (the institution of circumcision).
- **INFERENCE:** The word-order transposition between Romans 4:3 and Galatians 3:6 is a minor quotational variation that does not alter the underlying sense. Furthermore, the narrative sequence in Genesis demonstrates that Abraham's faith was recognized as righteousness prior to his circumcision.
- **CONCLUSION:** Paul utilizes the narrative sequence of Genesis 15:6 in Romans 4:3 to establish a fundamental theological distinction between righteousness by faith and physical circumcision (pp. 83, 131).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul assumed that the narrative sequence of Genesis was intentionally ordered by God and that he was uncovering the true, intrinsic meaning of scripture (p. 131).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Paul may have quoted from memory without precision regarding word order (p. 83).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The presence of an uncollated pre-Pauline Greek Old Testament manuscript tradition containing a variant word order for Genesis 15:6.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Evidence showing that early 1st-century Jewish exegesis treated Genesis 15:6 and Genesis 17 as non-chronological or topical rather than sequentially historical.

## 2. B001-2: Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 (LXX 2:25)

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul substitute καλέσω for ἐρῶ in Hosea 2:23 and follow the love terminology of LXX-B rather than LXX-A?
- **EVIDENCE:** Codex Vaticanus (LXX-B) reads ἀγαπήσω and ἠγαπημένην (matching Rom 9:25), whereas Codex Alexandrinus (LXX-A) reads ἐλεήσω and ἠλεημένην. In LXX Hosea 2:25, the introductory verb is ἐρῶ, whereas Paul writes καλέσω.
- **INFERENCE:** Paul followed an LXX-B text type for vocabulary. He deliberately substituted καλέσω for ἐρῶ to forge a verbal catchword link (gezerah shawah / verbal tally) with κληθήσονται in Hosea 1:10 (Rom 9:26) and ἐκάλεσεν in Romans 9:24.
- **CONCLUSION:** Paul intentionally modified Hosea 2:23 to link the Hosea citations together and ground the divine election and calling of the Gentiles in scriptural terminology (pp. 69 n. 19, 79, 178).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul intentionally modified scriptural texts to conform them to his epistolary context and thematic arguments (pp. 79, 178).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Quotation from memory; or fidelity to an unknown, non-LXX Greek manuscript tradition.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Reliance on an existing early Christian testimonia collection where Hosea 2:23 and Hosea 1:10 had already been combined and modified prior to Paul.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Discovery of a pre-Pauline Septuagint manuscript of Hosea 2:23 containing καλέσω.

## 3. B001-3: Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 (LXX 2:1)

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** Which textual tradition does Paul follow in Hosea 1:10, and how does he link it with neighboring citations?
- **EVIDENCE:** Codex Alexandrinus (LXX-A) prefixes ἐκεῖ to κληθήσονται and omits καὶ αὐτοί, matching Romans 9:26 verbatim against LXX-B. In Romans 9:26, the citation ends with υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος, and Romans 9:27 opens with υἱῶν Ἰσραήλ (substituting υἱῶν for Isaiah 10:22's λαός).
- **INFERENCE:** Paul utilized an LXX-A text-type and forged a verbal catchword connection (υἱοί / υἱῶν) between Hosea 1:10 and Isaiah 10:22–23.
- **CONCLUSION:** Hosea 1:10 functions as a scriptural proof that Gentiles are called sons of the living God, integrated into a chain of prophetic citations via catchword association (pp. 69 n. 19, 179).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul selected specific text-types or manuscript traditions that best supported his theological arguments (p. 69).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Textual modification executed directly by Paul himself.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Accidental or mechanical harmonization by later Christian copyists conforming LXX-A to the text of Romans.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Proof that LXX-A readings in Hosea were systematically revised toward the New Testament text post-Paul.

## 4. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul insert πᾶς into Isaiah 28:16 in Romans 10:11 when he omitted it in Romans 9:33?
- **EVIDENCE:** In Romans 9:33, Paul quotes Isaiah 28:16 as ὁ πιστεύων.... In Romans 10:11, he quotes it as Πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων.... Two verses later (Rom 10:13), Paul quotes Joel 2:32 (3:5), which contains πᾶς. LXX-A and Codex Sinaiticus include ἐπʼ αὐτῷ (matching both Rom 9:33 and 10:11 against LXX-B).
- **INFERENCE:** Paul followed an LXX-A text-type for ἐπʼ αὐτῷ. The upcoming citation of Joel 2:32 in Romans 10:13 exerted a catchword influence (πᾶς), prompting Paul to insert πᾶς into his second quotation of Isaiah 28:16.
- **CONCLUSION:** Paul modified Isaiah 28:16 via catchword association with Joel 2:32 to emphasize the universal scope of salvation in Christ for both Jew and Gentile (pp. 69 n. 19, 83, 126, 183).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul was not restrained from modifying scriptural texts when guided by verbal association with adjacent passages (p. 83).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Lapse of memory; or quotation from a different manuscript source.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Influence of early Christian liturgical or catechetical formulas where πᾶς had already been prefixed to Isaiah 28:16.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Discovery of a pre-Christian Greek manuscript of Isaiah 28:16 containing πᾶς.

## 5. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5)

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** How does Joel 2:32 function in Romans 10:13 compared to its usage in early church Pentecost traditions?
- **EVIDENCE:** Paul quotes Joel 3:5 LXX (πᾶς ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου σωθήσεται). The catchwords πᾶς and ἐπικαλέω link to Romans 10:11 (πᾶς) and Romans 10:12 (ἐπικαλουμένους). In Acts 2:21, Joel 2:32 is linked to the historical gift of the Spirit at Pentecost.
- **INFERENCE:** Paul uses Joel 2:32 not as an eschatological Pentecost prophecy (as in Acts), but as a scriptural proof built on verbal catchwords to ground universal salvation.
- **CONCLUSION:** Joel 2:32 establishes that divine salvation is open to Jew and Gentile alike on the sole condition of calling upon the Lord (pp. 126, 183).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Scriptural citations served both as proof-texts and as active generators of thematic/verbal development in Paul's thinking (pp. 91–92, 126).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Adoption of a fixed, pre-existing early Christian Pentecost tradition (pp. 125–126).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The influence of a Hellenistic synagogue lectionary cycle.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Proof that Joel 2:32 functioned exclusively as a Pentecost text across all 1st-century Christian communities.

## 6. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul quote different clauses of Isaiah 40:13 in Romans 11:34 and 1 Corinthians 2:16?
- **EVIDENCE:** In Romans 11:34, Paul quotes Isaiah 40:13a–b (τίς ἔγνω νοῦν κυρίου; ἢ τίς σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο;). In 1 Corinthians 2:16, he quotes Isaiah 40:13a, c (τίς γὰρ ἔγνω νοῦν κυρίου, ὃς συμβιβάσει αὐτόν;). The word order σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ in both epistles follows Codex Alexandrinus (LXX-A) against LXX-B.
- **INFERENCE:** Paul followed an LXX-A text type for word order. He intentionally selected and adapted distinct clauses of the same Old Testament passage to serve the specific needs of different epistolary arguments.
- **CONCLUSION:** Isaiah 40:13 in Romans 11:34 provides scriptural confirmation for the unsearchable wisdom and sovereignty of God at the conclusion of his doxology (pp. 69 n. 19, 84, 197).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul exercised freedom in selecting, isolating, and adapting specific clauses from Old Testament passages (pp. 72, 84).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Inaccurate or careless citation from memory.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Use of distinct testimonia sheets or liturgical florilegia in different mission fields.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Demonstrating that 1 Corinthians 2:16 and Romans 11:34 were drawn from two separate, pre-existing collections of quotes rather than composed directly by Paul.

## 7. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** How is Isaiah 11:10 integrated into the scriptural catena in Romans 15:9–12?
- **EVIDENCE:** Isa 11:10 is quoted in Rom 15:12 with the stylistic omission of ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ. The catchword ἔθνη appears in Psalm 17:50 LXX, Deuteronomy 32:43 LXX, Psalm 116:1 LXX, and Isaiah 11:10 LXX across Romans 15:9–12.
- **INFERENCE:** The catena is constructed via the rabbinic method of stringing passages together (charaz) using the catchword ἔθνη. The omission of the temporal phrase is a minor, non-substantial stylistic adjustment.
- **CONCLUSION:** Isaiah 11:10 grounds the hope and election of the Gentiles in the messianic root of Jesse (pp. 74–76, 91 n. 16, 107–108).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Verbal tally (gezerah shawah) was a primary midrashic technique used by Paul to assemble scriptural catenae (pp. 55, 91).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** The catena was adopted intact from pre-Pauline Jewish-Christian liturgy (pp. 107–108).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The catena was composed originally in Hebrew and later translated into Greek.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Discovery of a pre-Christian Jewish catena containing the exact same four verses in identical order.

## 8. B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** What role does Isaiah 29:14 play in the structure and thematic development of 1 Corinthians 1:18–25?
- **EVIDENCE:** Citation of Isaiah 29:14 (Ἀπολῶ τὴν σοφίαν τῶν σοφῶν...) in 1 Corinthians 1:19, followed immediately by 1 Corinthians 1:20–25, where σοφία and σοφῶν are repeatedly elaborated.
- **INFERENCE:** The citation is not merely an appended proof-text, but an active catalyst that generates the vocabulary and thematic trajectory of the surrounding discourse.
- **CONCLUSION:** Isaiah 29:14 provides the scriptural foundation for Paul's argument that God invalidates human wisdom through the cross (pp. 91–92).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Scripture functioned constructively in the epistles to generate themes and vocabulary for Paul's arguments (p. 92).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** E. Earle Ellis's proposal that 1 Corinthians 1:18–31 was an independent pre-Pauline Jewish-Christian homiletic midrash inserted into the letter.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Paul added the quotation after completing his prose argument as a secondary illustration.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Showing that 1 Corinthians 1:18–25 maintains complete thematic coherence without any reference to Isaiah 29:14.

## 9. B001-9: 1 Corinthians 6:16 / Genesis 2:24

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** How does Paul integrate Genesis 2:24 in 1 Corinthians 6:16 compared to Ephesians 5:31?
- **EVIDENCE:** 1 Corinthians 6:16 includes the parenthetical formula γάρ, φησίν (Ἔσονται γάρ, φησίν, οἱ δύο εἰς σάρκα μίαν). Ephesians 5:31 omits this formula and omits αὐτοῦ. In 1 Corinthians 6:16, the passage is applied to sexual union with a harlot.
- **INFERENCE:** γάρ, φησίν is inserted strictly for syntactical integration into the epistolary sentence. Genesis 2:24 is applied halakically to govern sexual ethics.
- **CONCLUSION:** Genesis 2:24 serves as an ethical/halakic command demonstrating that sexual intercourse creates a binding physical union ("one flesh") (pp. 83, 106 n. 85).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul applied narrative creation traditions as normative ethical/halakic guidelines (p. 106).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** The differences between 1 Corinthians 6:16 and Ephesians 5:31 stem from pseudepigraphal authorship of Ephesians (p. 83).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The quotation in 1 Corinthians 6:16 reflects a slogan originally cited by the Corinthian pneumatics.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Proof that 1st-century Jewish exegesis never used Genesis 2:24 halakically or ethically.

## 10. B001-10: 1 Corinthians 9:9 / Deuteronomy 25:4

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** How does Paul justify applying Deuteronomy 25:4 (a law about muzzling oxen) to apostolic financial support?
- **EVIDENCE:** Citation of Deuteronomy 25:4 with the lexical variant κημώσεις (for LXX φιμώσεις). Rhetorical questions in 1 Corinthians 9:9–10 ("Is it for oxen that God cares? Does he not speak completely for our sake?").
- **INFERENCE:** Paul uses a rabbinic-style exegetical inference (qal wahomer / a fortiori reasoning or midrashic application) to extend a Mosaic law about livestock to human workers.
- **CONCLUSION:** Deuteronomy 25:4 establishes the scriptural principle that Christian ministers have a divine right to material support from those they serve (pp. 68 n. 15, 93, 148).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** κημώσεις versus φιμώσεις represents an authentic manuscript/dialectal variant in Greek Old Testament transmission (p. 68 n. 15).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Paul is using pure allegory without concern for the literal sense (p. 148).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Paul borrowed a proverbial legal maxim common in Stoic or Cynic philosophical schools.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Evidence that 1st-century rabbinic exegesis explicitly rejected applying Deuteronomy 25:4 beyond literal livestock.

## 11. B001-11: 1 Corinthians 10:7 / Exodus 32:6

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** Is Paul's use of Exodus 32:6 in 1 Corinthians 10:7 typological or parenetic?
- **EVIDENCE:** Citation of Exodus 32:6 (Ἐκάθισεν ὁ λαὸς φαγεῖν καὶ πεῖν, καὶ ἀνέστησαν παίζειν). Framed by τυπικῶς (1 Cor 10:11), νουθεσία (1 Cor 10:11), and τύποι (1 Cor 10:6).
- **INFERENCE:** τύπος in this context means "moral warning" or "instructive example" rather than redemptive-historical typology with historical escalation.
- **CONCLUSION:** Exodus 32:6 functions as a midrashic parenetic warning against idolatry and pagan sacrificial feasting (pp. 93, 150, 154).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Typology based on historical-redemptive escalation is rare in Paul and inadequate as an overall description of his scriptural method (pp. 151–155).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Leonhard Goppelt's view that 1 Corinthians 10:1–13 is fundamentally historical-redemptive typology (pp. 151–154).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The passage reflects an early Christian baptismal or eucharistic liturgy.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Demonstrating that τύπος in 1 Corinthians 10:6–11 strictly required a salvation-historical antitype.

## 12. B001-12: Galatians 3:13 / Deuteronomy 21:23

### Structural Paraphrase (Reasoning Map)
- **QUESTION:** Why is ὑπὸ θεοῦ omitted from Deuteronomy 21:23 in Galatians 3:13?
- **EVIDENCE:** LXX Deuteronomy 21:23 reads κεκατηραμένος ὑπὸ θεοῦ. Galatians 3:13 reads Ἐπικατάρατος πᾶς ὁ κρεμάμενος ἐπὶ ξύλου (omitting ὑπὸ θεοῦ).
- **INFERENCE:** The phrase ὑπὸ θεοῦ was omitted to avoid presenting an unacceptable image of God (suggesting that Jesus was accursed by God the Father).
- **CONCLUSION:** Paul or his inherited tradition intentionally modified Deuteronomy 21:23 for theological protection of divine integrity while maintaining that Christ bore the law's curse (pp. 79, 92, 160).

### Explicit Source Statements
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Early Jewish interpreters and Paul routinely modified or paraphrased biblical texts to avoid unacceptable theological or christological implications (pp. 34, 79).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Paul inherited a pre-existing Jewish-Christian testimony where ὑπὸ θεοῦ was already omitted (p. 79).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Paul was quoting a non-LXX Greek manuscript or a Hebrew text tradition (such as 11QT) that phrased the curse differently.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Discovery of a pre-Christian LXX or Hebrew manuscript of Deuteronomy 21:23 lacking ὑπὸ θεοῦ.
