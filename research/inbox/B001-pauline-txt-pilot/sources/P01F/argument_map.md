## Methodological Finding Regarding B001 Passages

Because P01F is a critical edition rather than an expository or theological commentary, it contains no exegetical argument regarding Old Testament intertextuality or the 12 B001 passage comparisons (e.g., Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6, Galatians 3:13 / Deuteronomy 21:23).

The argument maps below trace the editor's explicit reasoning in establishing the Greek text and apparatus across the Pauline corpus.

---

## Reasoning Structures: QUESTION -> EVIDENCE -> INFERENCE -> CONCLUSION

### 1. Establishment of the Baseline Greek Text
- **QUESTION:** How should a contemporary critical Greek text of the New Testament be established without uncritically reproducing Nestle-Aland/UBS?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - Four primary editions representing distinct text-critical judgments: Westcott and Hort (WH 1881), Samuel Prideaux Tregelles (Treg 1857–1879), Robinson–Pierpont (RP 2005 Byzantine Textform), and NIV Greek text (Goodrich–Lukaszewski 2002).
  - 6,928 recorded variation units across the New Testament.
- **INFERENCE:** Where all four independent editions agree, their consensus provides a probable baseline; where they disagree, the point of variation must be critically evaluated to determine which reading best represents the earliest circulating form.
- **CONCLUSION:** Adopted WH as a modified starting point, collated against Treg, NIV, and RP, tentatively accepted four-way consensus, and systematically evaluated points of disagreement (and agreements), occasionally adopting readings outside all four (56 times across the NT).

### 2. Delimitation of Doubtful Readings and Bracket Policy (e.g., Ephesians 1:1; Colossians 1:20)
- **QUESTION:** How should editorial doubt and uncertain words in the Pauline corpus be signaled to readers?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - Frequent usage of brackets in contemporary editions (such as Nestle-Aland/UBS).
  - Disputed wording in Paul, such as `[ἐν Ἐφέσῳ]` in Eph 1:1 and `[διʼ αὐτοῦ]` in Col 1:20.
- **INFERENCE:** Overusing brackets allows editors to avoid making difficult choices and introduces an inherent methodological bias toward inclusion (an editor can bracket an included word with doubt, but cannot bracket an omitted word with equal doubt).
- **CONCLUSION:** Single square brackets are restricted to only six instances across the entire NT (including Eph 1:1 and Col 1:20 in Paul), obligating the editor to make definitive textual decisions in virtually all variation units.

### 3. Departures from Universal Consensus (e.g., 1 Corinthians 12:10)
- **QUESTION:** Should a reading be adopted if all four primary reference editions agree against it?
- **EVIDENCE:** In 1 Corinthians 12:10, WH, Treg, NIV, and RP all read `+ δὲ` across multiple clauses, whereas external and internal witnesses preserve an asyndetic construction (`ἄλλῳ`).
- **INFERENCE:** Unanimous agreement among modern printed editions does not preclude scribal smoothing or harmonizing additions in transmission history.
- **CONCLUSION:** Holmes rejected the reading of all four primary editions in 1 Cor 12:10, printing the asyndetic reading `ἄλλῳ` (one of 56 instances across the NT where the editor adopted an alternative variant).

### 4. Orthography, Breathings, and Punctuation (e.g., Philippians 3:21; Colossians 2:16)
- **QUESTION:** How should orthographical and orthotypographical details be regulated across the epistles?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - The standard lexicon BDAG.
  - Ambiguity regarding whether breathings on pronouns are lexical or exegetical (citing Metzger on Phil 3:21).
- **INFERENCE:** Standardizing orthography to BDAG eliminates arbitrary inconsistencies, but breathing marks sometimes reflect interpretive decisions regarding reflexive meaning rather than mere spelling.
- **CONCLUSION:** Standardize text and apparatus to BDAG (except rare instances such as `νουμηνίας` in Col 2:16), follow WH for elision/crasis, and print rough breathings on forms of `αὐτός` in Pauline passages where exegetically warranted.

## Explicit Assumptions Acknowledged by the Editor

1. **Working Text:** Rejects the assumption that Nestle-Aland/UBS represents a "final text to be passively accepted rather than a 'working' text subject to verification and improvement."
2. **Target Textform:** Defined as recovering the variant that "most likely represents the form in which the text first began to be copied and to circulate."
3. **Dual Purpose of Textual Criticism:** Must identify the earliest text while examining variants for the historical light they shed on how early communities altered texts.
4. **Historical Preoccupations:** Major 19th-century editors displayed preoccupation with the latest "big discovery" (e.g., WH with Codex Vaticanus), justifying balancing WH against Tregelles.
5. **Subjectivity:** Decisions regarding proper noun capitalization and punctuation offer "numerous opportunities for differences of opinion."

---

## Alternative Explanations and Readings Considered

1. **Four Primary Editions:** Across 6,928 variation units, alternative readings of WH, Treg, NIV, and RP were continually weighed.
2. **Marginal Variants:** In 38 of the 56 departures from all four editions, Holmes adopted readings from margins of WH, Treg, or NA.
3. **Byzantine Tradition:** Systematically weighed Byzantine readings via Robinson–Pierpont alongside Alexandrian witnesses.
4. **Syntactical Punctuation vs Paragraphing:** Weighed WH syntax against NRSV paragraph breaks (e.g., Phil 1:18 vs Phil 2:14).

---

## Alternative Explanations Not Addressed (Visible from Presentation)

1. **Secondary vs Primary Manuscript Collation:** The text is constructed by collating four existing printed editions rather than undertaking a fresh collation of primary papyri, majuscules, and minuscules across every verse.
2. **Ambiguity of the "Circulating Archetype":** Whether this represents Paul's dictated draft, the letter sent to the destination church, or the compiled corpus archetype is not distinguished.
3. **Intertextual Harmonization to the Septuagint:** The introduction does not detail how scribal tendency to harmonize Old Testament quotations to LXX witnesses was weighed in quotation variation units.

---

## Conditions Weakening or Falsifying Conclusions

1. **Status as Working Text:** The text is presented as provisional, subject to verification and improvement as research advances.
2. **Forced Choices vs Reticence:** Concedes that deciding hard cases without brackets is done "for better or worse," citing David C. Parker's preference for "wise reticence" as a viable alternative.
3. **Editio Critica Maior (ECM) Data:** The appendix notes 39 differences from ECM in the Catholic Letters, acknowledging that exhaustive manuscript databases and genealogical methods provide a standard against which earlier critical judgments can be re-evaluated.
