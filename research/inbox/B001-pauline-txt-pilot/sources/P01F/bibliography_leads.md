# P01F — Bibliography Leads

**Source ID:** P01F  
**Source Title:** *The Greek New Testament: SBL Edition*  
**Editor:** Michael W. Holmes (2010)  

---

## 1. Direct B001 Intertextual Citations

Because P01F is a primary critical edition of the Greek New Testament text with a variant-recording apparatus rather than an exegetical commentary or study of Old Testament citations, it contains no footnotes, commentary discussions, or bibliographies treating Old Testament intertextuality, Septuagint translation technique, or Pauline hermeneutics for the twelve B001 passages (e.g., Romans 4:3 / Gen 15:6; Romans 9:25–26 / Hos 1–2; Galatians 3:13 / Deut 21:23).

---

## 2. Text-Critical and Methodological Sources Cited in Front Matter and Apparatus

The editor explicitly cites and interacts with a specific corpus of scholarly editions, commentaries, and methodology studies in establishing the text and apparatus across the New Testament (including the Pauline epistles):

### 1. Bruce M. Metzger
- **Title:** *A Textual Commentary on The Greek New Testament*
- **Publication Year:** 1971 (1st ed.)
- **Pages / Locator:** Page 616; cited at Page xii, Footnote 10
- **Significance:** Cited regarding breathing marks on pronouns as exegetical decisions rather than purely lexical ones. Holmes follows a minority position of the UBS Editorial Committee discussed on page 616 of Metzger's first edition (discussing Philippians 3:21; omitted from the 2nd ed.) to print rough breathings on forms of αὐτός when reflexive meaning is intended.

### 2. David C. Parker
- **Title:** "The Development of the Critical Text of the Epistle of James: From Lachmann to the Editio Critica Maior" (in *New Testament Textual Criticism and Exegesis: Festschrift J. Delobel*, ed. A. Denaux; BETL 161; Leuven: Leuven University Press and Peeters)
- **Publication Year:** 2002
- **Pages / Locator:** Pages 325 and 329; cited at Page x, Footnote 3; Pages xiv–xv, Footnote 16
- **Significance:** 
  - On page x, Parker is cited (p. 329) for commending Tregelles's "consistency of view and breadth of appreciation" of textual evidence over against the tendency of Westcott–Hort, Tischendorf, and Lachmann to become preoccupied with a single "big discovery" (such as Vaticanus or Sinaiticus).
  - On pages xiv–xv, Parker is cited (p. 325) regarding the concept of "wise reticence" in editorial decisions. Holmes explains why he restricts single square brackets to only six instances across the New Testament (including Eph 1:1 and Col 1:20), arguing that brackets have been overused to evade difficult choices and introduce an inherent bias toward inclusion.

### 3. Brooke Foss Westcott and Fenton John Anthony Hort
- **Title:** *The New Testament in the Original Greek*, vol. 1: *Text*; vol. 2: *Introduction [and] Appendix*
- **Publication Year:** 1881
- **Pages / Locator:** Entire edition; cited at Pages ix, xi, xiii, xiv, xv, xviii
- **Significance:** Served as the initial baseline and starting point for the SBLGNT. After orthographic adjustments, its text was collated against the other three primary editions. It also provides the standard for punctuation, elision, crasis, movable ν, and the apparatus's recording of ancient non-original text preserved in double brackets (`〚WH〛`).

### 4. Samuel Prideaux Tregelles
- **Title:** *The Greek New Testament, Edited from Ancient Authorities, with their Various Readings in Full, and the Latin Version of Jerome*
- **Publication Year:** 1857–1879
- **Pages / Locator:** Entire edition; cited at Pages ix–x, xi, xv, xviii
- **Significance:** One of the four primary reference editions collated for every variation unit. It provides a balanced nineteenth-century critical alternative to Westcott and Hort that does not over-privilege Codex Vaticanus.

### 5. Richard J. Goodrich and Albert L. Lukaszewski
- **Title:** *A Reader’s Greek New Testament*
- **Publication Year:** 2003 (with reference to 2nd ed. 2007 modified by Gordon Fee)
- **Pages / Locator:** Page 10, Footnote 6; cited at Page x, Footnotes 5–6; Pages xi, xv, xvii
- **Significance:** One of the four primary reference editions collated across all 6,928 variation units. It presents the Greek text underlying the New International Version (reconstructed by Edward Goodrick and John Kohlenberger III), embodying modern editorial decisions of the Committee on Bible Translation.

### 6. Maurice A. Robinson and William G. Pierpont
- **Title:** *The New Testament in the Original Greek: Byzantine Textform 2005*
- **Publication Year:** 2005
- **Pages / Locator:** Entire edition; cited at Pages x–xi, xv, xvii
- **Significance:** One of the four primary reference editions. It serves as the primary witness for the Byzantine textual tradition in the collation process, ensuring systematic comparison between Byzantine and critical/Alexandrian readings.

### 7. Kurt Aland, Matthew Black, Bruce M. Metzger, Allen Wikgren, and Eberhard Nestle
- **Titles:**
  - *The Greek New Testament* (UBS, 1966)
  - *Novum Testamentum Graece* (Nestle-Aland / NA26–27, 1898/1936/1993)
  - *The Text of the New Testament* (Kurt Aland and Barbara Aland, 2nd ed., trans. E. F. Rhodes, 1989)
- **Pages / Locator:** Page v (UBS 1966); p. 38* (Nestle 16th ed., 1936); pp. 19–20 (Aland & Aland 1989); cited at Page ix, Footnotes 1–2; Page xvii
- **Significance:** Represents the contemporary "standard text". Holmes establishes SBLGNT to challenge passive acceptance of NA/UBS as a "final" text, departing from it in more than 540 variation units. NA is also cited explicitly in the apparatus whenever it differs from the NIV text.

### 8. Barbara Aland, Kurt Aland, Gerd Mink, Holger Strutwolf, and Klaus Wachtel (INTF)
- **Title:** *Novum Testamentum Graecum: Editio Critica Maior*, vol. 4: *Catholic Letters*
- **Publication Year:** 1997–2005
- **Pages / Locator:** Page 24* (installment 2, *The Letters of Peter*); cited at Pages xvi–xvii; Appendix, Pages 515–516
- **Significance:** Cited in the Appendix to compare the text of the Catholic Letters, documenting 39 variation units where SBLGNT differs from ECM. Footnote 1 (p. 515) cites page 24* regarding the ECM's system of bold dots signaling readings of equal value or high doubt.

### 9. Frederick William Danker and Walter Bauer
- **Title:** *A Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament and Other Early Christian Literature* (BDAG, 3rd ed.)
- **Publication Year:** 2000
- **Pages / Locator:** Cited at Pages xii–xiii, Footnote 11
- **Significance:** Governs orthography, accentuation, and breathings of both the printed text and apparatus, resolving spelling discrepancies across older editions (with rare noted exceptions like Col 2:16).

### 10. Michael W. Holmes
- **Titles:**
  - "Reconstructing the Text of the New Testament" (in *The Blackwell Companion to the New Testament*, ed. David E. Aune; Oxford: Wiley-Blackwell, 2010), pp. 77–89
  - *The Apostolic Fathers: Greek Texts and English Translations* (3rd ed.; Grand Rapids: Baker Academic, 2007)
- **Pages / Locator:** Cited at Page xi, Footnote 7; Page xiii, Footnote 12; Page xv, Footnote 17
- **Significance:** Provides the external statement of the editor's text-critical methodology (*Blackwell*) and supplies the model for capitalization rules and apparatus formatting (*Apostolic Fathers*).
