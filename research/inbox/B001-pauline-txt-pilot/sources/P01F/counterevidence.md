## 2. General Methodological and Text-Critical Disclosures

Across the front matter (Preface, Introduction) and Appendix, the editor explicitly addresses several methodological categories:

### A. Expresses Uncertainty / Editorial Doubt
- **Doubtful Text Indicated by Brackets:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xiv, "Symbols Used in the Text"
  - **Evidence:** Single square brackets `[ ]` indicate that "The enclosed text is doubtful."
- **Uncertainty Over Verse Status (Acts 19:41):**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xiv, Footnote 14
  - **Evidence:** A 41st verse number was placed in brackets at the end of Acts 19 "to indicate uncertainty regarding its status."
- **Severe Limitation on Bracketing to Avoid Evading Hard Choices:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xv, Footnote 16
  - **Evidence:** Holmes notes that brackets were used only six times in the entire New Testament (Luke 22:19–20; 24:40; 24:51; 24:52; Eph 1:1; Col 1:20). He explains that brackets have often been overused "as what could be perceived as a means of avoiding difficult choices", whereas an editor's responsibility is "to make choices, particularly in the 'hard cases'."
- **Subjectivity of Proper Noun Capitalization:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xiii, Footnote 13
  - **Evidence:** Deciding proper noun capitalization is explicitly described as "A category that offers, to be sure, numerous opportunities for differences of opinion."
- **ECM Uncertainty Markers (Appendix):**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page 515, Appendix Footnote 1
  - **Evidence:** Holmes explains that bold dots in the *Editio Critica Maior* designate "alternative readings which were considered of equal value" or instances where reasons for a primary reading were superior "but not sufficiently to rule out with complete confidence the claims of the indicated alternative reading."

### B. Discusses Competing Textual Forms
- **Scope of Documented Variation Units:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Pages xi, xv
  - **Evidence:** The edition systematically records all differences across 6,928 variation units between the SBL text and WH, Treg, NIV, RP, and NA (excluding purely orthographical matters).
- **Westcott and Hort Double Brackets for Ancient Non-Original Material:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Pages xvi, xxi
  - **Evidence:** The apparatus preserves WH's double brackets (`〚WH〛`), marking passages (such as Luke 22:43–44) that WH "did not think belonged to the genuine text but that they did not feel free to remove completely from their printed text due to its antiquity or intrinsic interest."

### C. Notes a Hebrew/Greek Ambiguity
- **Findings:** `NONE FOUND IN THIS SOURCE`. The editor nowhere analyzes Hebrew versus Greek linguistic, translational, or Septuagintal ambiguities in the introductory matter or apparatus notes.

### D. Allows Authorial Adaptation, Oral Transmission, Liturgical Transmission, or Unknown Textual Forms
- **Historical Alteration by Communities:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page viii, Preface
  - **Evidence:** The editor affirms that variant readings illuminate "how particular individuals and faith communities adopted, used, and sometimes altered the texts that they read, studied, and transmitted."
- **Definition of Target Text as Initial Circulating Form:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xi, Footnote 8
  - **Evidence:** Holmes defines the objective of the edition as determining "which variant most likely represents the form in which the text first began to be copied and to circulate."
- **Liturgical / Doxological Additions in Apparatus:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page 10 (Matthew 6:13); Page 337 (Romans 16:24)
  - **Evidence:** The apparatus records traditional liturgical/doxological additions preserved in the Byzantine textform (RP), such as the conclusion to the Lord's Prayer (`+ Ὅτι σοῦ ἐστιν ἡ βασιλεία... RP` in Matt 6:13) and the closing benediction in Rom 16:24 (`Ἡ χάρις … ὑμῶν RP ] – WH Treg NIV`).

### E. Disagrees with Another Scholar or Edition
- **Disagreement with the Standard Modern Text (NA/UBS):**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page viii, Preface
  - **Evidence:** The SBLGNT "differs from the standard text in more than 540 variation units."
- **Departures from All Four Primary Reference Editions:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xi, Footnote 9
  - **Evidence:** Holmes preferred a reading not found in any of the four primary editions (WH, Treg, NIV, RP) in 56 variation units. In 38 of these, the reading was supported by marginal notes in WH, Treg, or NA.
- **Rejection of Universal Agreement in 1 Corinthians 12:10:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xxi
  - **Evidence:** In 1 Cor 12:10, Holmes prints the asyndetic reading `ἄλλῳ` against all four primary editions (`+ δὲ WH Treg NIV RP`).
- **Disagreement with BDAG Orthography (Colossians 2:16):**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xiii, Footnote 11
  - **Evidence:** Holmes departs from BDAG's spelling by printing `νουμηνίας` rather than `νεομηνίας` in Col 2:16.
- **Disagreement with UBS Editorial Committee Majority (Philippians 3:21):**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xii, Footnote 10
  - **Evidence:** In agreement with a minority of the UBS committee against the majority, the SBLGNT prints rough breathings on forms of αὐτός where exegetically reflexive.
- **Disagreements with the Editio Critica Maior (Catholic Letters):**
  - **Page / Locator:** Pages 515–516, Appendix
  - **Evidence:** In the Catholic Letters, Holmes enumerates 39 specific places where the SBLGNT differs from the text of the ECM.

### F. Acknowledges Evidence That Weakens the Preferred Interpretation
- **Bias of Bracketing Toward Inclusion:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Page xv, Footnote 16
  - **Evidence:** Holmes acknowledges that decisions on doubtful text are made "for better or worse", citing David C. Parker's concept of "wise reticence" and admitting that the availability of brackets inherently "biases the decision-making process toward inclusion (one can bracket an included word about which one has some degree of doubt... but one cannot bracket the omission of a word about which one has an equal degree of doubt)."
- **Rejection of Textual Finality:**
  - **Page / Locator:** Pages vii–viii, Preface
  - **Evidence:** Holmes explicitly cautions against viewing any printed critical text as a "final" text, affirming that it must be treated as a "working" text open to continual verification, revision, and improvement.
