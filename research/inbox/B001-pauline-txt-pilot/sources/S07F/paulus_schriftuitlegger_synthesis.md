# S07F — Paulus Schriftuitlegger Synthesis

Synthesis of Pieter K. Baaij's explicit analysis in *Paulus als Schriftuitlegger: Paulus’ vertolking van Genesis 15:5b–6 in Romeinen 4:3*, *In die Skriflig*, Vol. 39, No. 2 (2005), pp. 239–254.

---

### 1. Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:5b–6 (and comparison with Galatians 3:6)

- **Paul's Use and Citation in Romans 4:3:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 239 [PDF p. 1]; Section 1
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Baaij demonstrates that in Romans 4:3 (*τί γὰρ ἡ γραφὴ λέγει; ἐπίστευσεν δὲ Ἀβραὰμ τῷ θεῷ, καὶ ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ εἰς δικαιοσύνην*), Paul renders Genesis 15:5b–6 not as a loose Greek paraphrase, but as an exact, direct re-translation of Hebrew thought patterns: *"Paulus zijn tekst eerst in het Bijbels-Hebreeuws heeft overdacht om die daarna met grote nauwkeurigheid in het Grieks te vertalen."* (p. 239).

- **Comparison with Galatians 3:6 & Selective Citation Refutation:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]; Section 3, fn. 8
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Baaij addresses scholarly claims (specifically E.P. Meijering 2003, pp. 31, 35) that Paul cited Scripture selectively or altered Old Testament texts across Romans, Galatians (Gal 3:6), and 1 Corinthians to suit his theology. Baaij refutes this view, asserting that Paul's gospel in all his epistles remains completely faithful to Hebrew Scripture: *"Paulus’ evangelie wijkt echter in niets af van wat de Schriften leren..."* (p. 248, fn. 8).

### 2. Comparison Between the Masoretic Text (MT) and Septuagint (LXX)

- **Comparison Between the Masoretic Text (MT) and Septuagint (LXX):**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]; Section 3
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Baaij compares the MT of Genesis 15:5b–6 (וְהֶאֱמִן בַּיהוָה וַיַּחְשְׁבֶהָ לּוֹ צְדָקָה) with the LXX (*καὶ ἐπίστευσεν Ἀβρὰμ τῷ θεῷ καὶ ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ εἰς δικαιοσύνην*). He notes that in extant Greek wording, Paul (Rom 4:3) and the LXX (Gen 15:6) agree in sharing the passive clause *καὶ ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ εἰς δικαιοσύνην* (p. 249: *"evenals de LXX"*) and simple dative *τῷ θεῷ*. The actual textual differences are the conjunction (*καί* vs. postpositive *δέ*) and patriarch's name (*Ἀβράμ* vs. *Ἀβραάμ*). Baaij argues that Paul's Greek syntax with postpositive *δέ* reflects active Hebrew trust (*'āman* in hif'il) directly from Hebrew thought patterns.

- **Word Order and Postpositive δé:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]; Section 3
  - **Analysis & Quote:** The LXX places the name "Abram" (*Ἀβράμ*) immediately after the verb, whereas Paul in Romans 4:3 uses postpositive δέ (*ἐπίστευσεν δὲ Ἀβραάμ*) to place primary structural emphasis on the active verb "trusted" (*ἐπίστευσεν*), setting the patriarch's name second: *"om het voorafgaande [werk]woord extra nadruk te verlenen én om aan te geven dat hij meer aspecten aangaande dit gebeuren [‘vertrouwen’] gaat vermelden..."* (p. 248).

- **Covenant Name Change (Abraham vs Abram):**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]; Section 3
  - **Analysis & Quote:** The LXX retains the pre-covenant name "Abram" (*Ἀβράμ*), whereas Paul intentionally uses the covenant name "Abraham" (*Ἀβραάμ*), designating the responsive covenant partner: *"Niet dus “Abram” (LXX), maar Abraham, de God antwoordende mens, over wie Paulus in het vervolg nog uitgebreid zal spreken."* (p. 248).

- **Septuagint Misleading Readers & Polemic:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed pp. 252–253 [PDF pp. 14–15]; Section 5
  - **Analysis & Quote:** In the concluding section following discussion of Romans 10:5ff, Baaij attributes to Paul a broader polemical argument that uncritical reliance on Septuagint translations alienates readers from the God of Scripture and teaches the opposite of what Hebrew Scripture states: *"de mens het tegenovergestelde van wat de Schrift zegt (Baaij, 2003:145-148). Vertalers en uitleggers van de Schriften zouden zich dus beter door Paulus laten onderrichten."* (pp. 252–253). This represents a broader hermeneutical polemic attributed to Paul regarding translation reliance, distinct from direct syntactic observation on Genesis 15:6 variants (where Paul and the LXX share passive *ἐλογίσθη* and simple dative *τῷ θεῷ*).

### 3. Grammatical Analysis: Active Hebrew vs. Passive Greek & Justification

- **Grammatical Analysis: Active Hebrew (wayyaḥšəḇehā) vs. Passive Greek (ἐλογίσθη):**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]; Section 3
  - **Analysis & Quote:** In the MT, *wayyaḥšəḇehā* is active, where God actively credits or attributes Abram's trust as *ṣəḏāqāh* (restoration of covenant relationship). Both the LXX and Paul in Romans 4:3 employ the passive *ἐλογίσθη*, but Paul uses passive *ἐλογίσθη* with *εἰς* to reflect Hebrew *ḥāšaḇ*, stressing that righteousness is an unmerited relational gift from God.

- **Paul's Theology of Justification & Dativus Causae:**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]; Section 3
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Paul constructs *πιστεύω* with a simple dative (*τῷ θεῷ*) without prepositions *ἐπί* or *εἰς*, which Baaij interprets as a *dativus causae* identifying God as the divine Cause and Initiator whose promise evokes Abraham's active response of trust: *"Met pi,steuw cum dativo (zonder prepositie dus) legt Paulus de nadruk op de Veroorzaker... Dit gebeuren wordt door de God van Abraham geïnitieerd en actief door de ontvanger aanvaard."* (p. 248).

### 4. Paul as 'Schriftuitlegger bij uitstek' and Methodological Implications

- **Paul as 'Schriftuitlegger bij uitstek' (Expositor Par Excellence):**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 240 [PDF p. 2]; Section 1
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Baaij designates Paul as *"de schriftuitlegger bij uitstek"* who teaches readers how to interpret Old Testament texts correctly: *"de echte Paulus de schriftuitlegger is bij uitstek. Als wij de instrumenten gebruiken die deze Paulus ons als lezers aanreikt, leidt dit niet alleen tot een duidelijk verstaan van Paulus’ verkondiging, maar ook van de Schriften zelf."* (p. 240).

- **Methodological Implications & Pharisaic Hebrew Background (W.C. van Unnik):**
  - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 241 [PDF p. 3]; Section 1
  - **Analysis & Quote:** Citing W.C. van Unnik (1952), Baaij emphasizes that Paul was raised and educated as a Pharisee in Jerusalem, thinking out his theological concepts in Biblical Hebrew before writing them accurately in Greek: *"Van Unnik, die dit scherp heeft gezien: “[…] in tegenstelling tot de algemeen aanvaarde opvatting, dat Paulus zijn jeugd in Tarsus heeft doorgebracht […] is hij in Jeruzalem opgegroeid.”"* (p. 241).
