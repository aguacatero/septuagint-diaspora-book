# S07F — Bibliography Leads

Disaggregated bibliographic entries from footnotes and bibliography in Pieter K. Baaij, *Paulus als Schriftuitlegger: Genesis 15:5b–6 in Romeinen 4:3*, *In die Skriflig*, Vol. 39, No. 2 (2005), pp. 239–254.

---

## 1. Primary Exegetical Leads on Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6 (B001-1)

### W.C. van Unnik (1952)
- **Author:** Willem Cornelis van Unnik
- **Title:** *Tarsus of Jeruzalem: De stad van Paulus’ jeugd*
- **Publication Year:** 1952 (Amsterdam: Noord-Hollandsche Uitgevers Maatschappij; Mededelingen der Koninklijke Nederlandse Akademie van Wetenschappen, Afd. Letterkunde, Nieuwe Reeks, Deel 15, No. 5, pp. 141–183)
- **Source Locator:** Printed pp. 241–242 [PDF pp. 3–4]; Section 1
- **Why It Matters:** Provides the foundational historical-biographical proof that Paul was raised, educated, and trained as a strict Pharisee in Jerusalem rather than Tarsus, meaning Paul thought primarily in Aramaic and Biblical Hebrew before expressing his thoughts in Greek. Baaij relies on Van Unnik (pp. 173, 176, 178) to argue that Paul retro-translated Genesis 15:5b–6 directly from Biblical Hebrew in Romans 4:3.

### Alfred Jepsen (1973–1995)
- **Author:** Alfred Jepsen
- **Title:** Article *‘āman* in G.J. Botterweck & H. Ringgren (eds.), *Theologisches Wörterbuch zum Alten Testament* (ThWAT), Vol. I, cols. 313–348
- **Publication Year:** 1973–1995 (Stuttgart: Kohlhammer)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 245 [PDF p. 7]; Section 3
- **Why It Matters:** In-depth lexical and syntactic analysis of the hif'il stem of Hebrew *'āman* in Genesis 15:6. Jepsen notes that the hif'il is notoriously difficult to translate into modern European languages, representing an inner-transitive response of active trust in God's covenant initiative.

### Klaus Seybold (1973–1995)
- **Author:** Klaus Seybold
- **Title:** Article *ḥāšaḇ* in G.J. Botterweck & H. Ringgren (eds.), *Theologisches Wörterbuch zum Alten Testament* (ThWAT), Vol. III, cols. 243–257
- **Publication Year:** 1973–1995 (Stuttgart: Kohlhammer)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 246 [PDF p. 8]; Section 3
- **Why It Matters:** Semasiological investigation of the Hebrew root *ḥāšaḇ* (חָשַׁב) in Genesis 15:6. Seybold distinguishes between forensic accounting and creative divine design/choice, demonstrating that God's attributing of righteousness to Abraham in Genesis 15:6 proceeds from prior divine knowledge.

### Bo Johnson (1973–1995)
- **Author:** Bo Johnson
- **Title:** Article *tsedāqāh* in G.J. Botterweck & H. Ringgren (eds.), *Theologisches Wörterbuch zum Alten Testament* (ThWAT), Vol. VI, cols. 898–924
- **Publication Year:** 1973–1995 (Stuttgart: Kohlhammer)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 247 [PDF p. 9]; Section 3
- **Why It Matters:** Authoritative semantic study of Hebrew *ṣəḏāqāh* (צְדָקָה). Johnson establishes that *ṣəḏāqāh* denotes the active restoration of a breached or endangered relationship rather than an abstract forensic verdict, supporting Baaij's translation of Romans 4:3 as "herstel van de relatie tussen God en de door Hem aangesproken mens".

### Jaap van Dorp (2004)
- **Author:** Jaap van Dorp
- **Title:** *Genesis 15:6 in de Nieuwe Bijbelvertaling (NBV)*
- **Publication Year:** 2004 (*In de Waagschaal*, Vol. 33, No. 10, pp. 24–26)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 244 [PDF p. 6], fn. 4; Printed p. 246 [PDF p. 8]; Section 3
- **Why It Matters:** Exegetical and translation analysis evaluating how the Dutch *Nieuwe Bijbelvertaling* (NBV 2004) renders Genesis 15:6. Van Dorp examines the placement of the subject and the rendering of active trust, providing direct contemporary Dutch context for Baaij's thesis.

### Pieter van Walbeek (2004a, 2004b)
- **Author:** Pieter van Walbeek
- **Title:** *Vertaling als provocatie of toch iets anders ... ?* (2004a, *In de Waagschaal*, Vol. 33, No. 10, pp. 22–24); *Is er geen spanning tussen de gerechtigheid van God en de gerechtigheid uit het geloof?* (2004b, *In de Waagschaal*, Vol. 33, No. 16, pp. 11–13)
- **Publication Year:** 2004
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 244 [PDF p. 6], fn. 4; Section 2
- **Why It Matters:** Critical evaluations of the NBV translation choices in Romans and Genesis, focusing on the tension between God's righteousness and justification by faith.

### E.P. Meijering (2003)
- **Author:** Eginhard Peter Meijering
- **Title:** *Als de uitleg maar goed is: Hoe vroege Christenen de Schriften gebruikten*
- **Publication Year:** 2003 (Amsterdam: Balans)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10], fn. 8; Section 3
- **Why It Matters:** Cites Meijering's argument (pp. 31, 35) that Paul cited Old Testament passages selectively and altered texts across Romans, Galatians, and 1 Corinthians. Baaij explicitly disputes Meijering, asserting that Paul's gospel adheres strictly and faithfully to Hebrew Scripture without manipulation.

### Frans Breukelman / Y. Bekker (1989)
- **Author:** Frans Breukelman (ed. Ype Bekker et al.)
- **Title:** *Gesprekken met Frans Breukelman*
- **Publication Year:** 1989 (’s-Gravenhage: Meinema)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 252 [PDF p. 14]; Section 5
- **Why It Matters:** Cites Breukelman's dictum (p. 58) that any translation of Scripture that introduces priorities foreign to the text itself must be rejected because it alienates the reader from the God of Scripture.

### P.K. Baaij (1993, 1998, 2003)
- **Author:** Pieter K. Baaij
- **Title:** *Paulus over Paulus: Exegetische studie van Romeinen 7* (1993); *De God antwoordende mens: Exegetische studie van Romeinen 1-4* (1998, Kampen: Kok); *Israël en de volken: Exegetische studie van Romeinen 9-11* (2003, Kampen: Kok)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 242 [PDF p. 4], fn. 2; Printed p. 247 [PDF p. 9], fn. 7; Printed p. 253 [PDF p. 15]; Section 5
- **Why It Matters:** Baaij's trilogy of exegetical commentaries on Romans establishing his hermeneutical method: Paul thought in Biblical Hebrew, constructed Greek syntax to reflect Hebrew semantics, and intended Romans for Jewish and Gentile believers familiar with the Torah.

## 2. Disputed Scholarly Authorities

### C.E.B. Cranfield (1985) & Joseph A. Fitzmyer (1993)
- **Authors:** Charles E.B. Cranfield; Joseph A. Fitzmyer
- **Titles:** *A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans* (Cranfield 1985, ICC, Edinburgh: T&T Clark); *Romans: A New Translation with Introduction and Commentary* (Fitzmyer 1993, Anchor Bible 33, New York: Doubleday)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 240 [PDF p. 2]; Printed p. 242 [PDF p. 4], fn. 3; Section 1
- **Why It Matters:** Major international commentary authorities who argue that Paul must be interpreted primarily through Hellenistic-Roman culture and thought patterns. Baaij directly disputes their approach, maintaining that understanding Paul through Greek-Hellenistic philosophy distorts his Hebrew-grounded exegesis.

### C.J. den Heijer (1998)
- **Author:** C.J. den Heijer
- **Title:** *Paulus: Man van twee werelden*
- **Publication Year:** 1998 (Zoetermeer: Meinema)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 251 [PDF p. 13]; Section 5
- **Why It Matters:** Den Heijer claims that Romans 3:20b and 3:21 express "very negative statements about the Torah". Baaij refutes this, demonstrating that Den Heijer relied on flawed Dutch translations rather than reading Paul's Greek through Hebrew syntax.

### J. van Bruggen (2001)
- **Author:** Jakob van Bruggen
- **Title:** *Paulus: Pionier voor de Messias van Israël*
- **Publication Year:** 2001 (Commentaar op het Nieuwe Testament, Kampen: Kok)
- **Source Locator:** Printed p. 251 [PDF p. 13], fn. 9; Section 5
- **Why It Matters:** Prominent Dutch Reformed New Testament commentator whose interpretations Baaij critiques for relying on Hellenistic Greek categories and conventional translations rather than Paul's Hebrew Vorlage.
