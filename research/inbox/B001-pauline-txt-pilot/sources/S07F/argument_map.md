# S07F — Argument Map

## B001-1: Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:5b–6

### Reasoning Map

- **QUESTION:** How does Paul translate and interpret Genesis 15:5b–6 in Romans 4:3, and does his rendering reflect an accurate exegesis of Biblical Hebrew syntax or a loose reliance on the Septuagint (LXX)? (Printed pp. 239–241 [PDF pp. 1–3])
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Hebrew Verb Morphology:* In Genesis 15:5b–6 (MT), the Hebrew verb *'āman* (נֶאֱמַן / הֶאֱמִין) appears in the hif'il stem, expressing an inner-transitive, active content ("zich toevertrouwen aan", active trust responding to a divine Initiator). (Printed p. 245 [PDF p. 7])
  - *Causal Conjunction:* The waw introducing Genesis 15:6 functions logically as a causal conjunction ("because he trusted") preceding the main clause ("He counted it to him as righteousness"). (Printed pp. 245–246 [PDF pp. 7–8])
  - *Semantic Range of ḥāšaḇ and ṣəḏāqāh:* The Hebrew root *ḥāšaḇ* (חָשַׁב) signifies counting or attributing based on prior divine knowledge, while *ṣəḏāqāh* (צְדָקָה) denotes God's positive restorative activity re-establishing the covenant relationship with the responsive human partner. (Printed pp. 246–247 [PDF pp. 8–9])
  - *Pauline Greek Citation Form:* In Romans 4:3, Paul writes: *τί γὰρ ἡ γραφὴ λέγει; ἐπίστευσεν δὲ Ἀβραὰμ τῷ θεῷ, καὶ ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ εἰς δικαιοσύνην*. (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10])
  - *Word Order and Postpositive δέ:* Paul places the verb *ἐπίστευσεν* ("trusted") at the very front of the Greek clause and uses postpositive δέ to attach extra grammatical emphasis to the verb rather than placing the patriarch's name first. (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10])
  - *Dativus Causae Construction:* Paul constructs *πιστεύω* with a simple dative *τῷ θεῷ* (without prepositions *ἐπί* or *εἰς*), which functions as a *dativus causae* identifying God as the Initiator and Cause of trust. (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10])
  - *Covenant Name Substitution:* Paul deliberately uses the covenant name *Ἀβραάμ* ("Abraham") rather than the LXX's *Ἀβράμ* ("Abram"). (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10])
  - *Biographical Background (W.C. van Unnik):* Historical and linguistic evidence shows Paul was raised and educated as a Pharisee in Jerusalem, thinking in Aramaic/Hebrew before translating his thoughts into Greek. (Printed pp. 241–242 [PDF pp. 3–4])
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul did not blindly copy the Septuagint (which used passive forms or placed "Abram" first), but independently retro-translated his theological thoughts directly from Biblical Hebrew into Greek when composing Romans 4:3. (Printed pp. 239–240 [PDF pp. 1–2]; Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10])
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* By placing *ἐπίστευσεν* first, utilizing postpositive δέ, and selecting the simple dative *τῷ θεῷ*, Paul intentionally highlights active trust in God's initiative as the central requirement of the Torah, placing human trust and divine attribution at the heart of covenant righteousness rather than human merit or the person of Abram. (Printed pp. 248–249 [PDF pp. 10–11])
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Romans 4:3, Paul provides an exact, precise, and faithful rendering of Genesis 15:5b–6, establishing himself as an expositor of Hebrew Scripture par excellence (*schriftuitlegger bij uitstek*). (Printed p. 240 [PDF p. 2]; Printed p. 254 [PDF p. 16])

### Analytical Breakdown

#### 1. Assumptions the Author Explicitly Acknowledges
- **Masoretic Text Identity:** Baaij explicitly assumes that the Hebrew text known to Paul did not deviate in any meaningful way from the Hebrew Masoretic Text (BHS) available today: *"Hierbij gaan wij ervan uit dat... de Hebreeuwse tekst die Paulus heeft gekend, niet heeft afgeweken van... de Masoretische tekst"* (Printed p. 243 [PDF p. 5]).
- **Pharisaic Jerusalem Upbringing:** He assumes that Paul must be understood from his Pharisaic, Jerusalem-reared background (citing W.C. van Unnik 1952) rather than as a Hellenized Diaspora Jew from Tarsus who thought in Greek (Printed pp. 241–242 [PDF pp. 3–4]).
- **Authorial Worldview Premise:** He assumes that valid exegesis and translation must strive to understand the text from the experiential world (*belevingswereld*) and intended meaning of the original biblical author rather than forcing modern categories onto ancient words (Printed p. 241 [PDF p. 3], citing Noordegraaf 2003).
- **Audience Familiarity with Torah:** He assumes that Paul's addressees in Rome included Greek-speaking Jewish Christians and educated synagogue attendees who knew the Hebrew Scriptures well (Printed p. 242 [PDF p. 4]).

#### 2. Alternative Explanations the Author Considers
- **Hellenistic Paul Hypothesis:** The dominant scholarly view (e.g., Cranfield 1985, Fitzmyer 1993, Den Heijer 1998) that Paul was thoroughly Hellenized and wrote out of Hellenistic-Roman culture and Greek thought-patterns (Printed p. 240 [PDF p. 2], p. 242 [PDF p. 4] fn. 3, p. 251 [PDF p. 13]).
- **Selective Citation / Textual Manipulation View:** The claim by commentators (e.g., E.P. Meijering 2003) that Paul cited Scripture selectively, manipulated texts, or altered Old Testament meanings across Romans, Galatians, and 1 Corinthians to fit Christian ideology (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10] fn. 8).
- **Standard Translation Choices:** Existing Dutch translations (Statenvertaling, NBG 1951, Willibrord 1995, NBV 2004) that place "Abram" or "Abraham" at the beginning of Genesis 15:6 and Romans 4:3, thereby highlighting the patriarch rather than the act of trusting (Printed p. 244 [PDF p. 6], p. 250 [PDF p. 12]).
- **Septuagint Primacy:** Uncritical reliance on the Septuagint (LXX) text of Genesis 15:6, which Baaij argues distorts Hebrew syntax (e.g., teaching the opposite of what Hebrew Scripture states) (Printed p. 244 [PDF p. 6], p. 253 [PDF p. 15]).

#### 3. Alternative Explanations the Author Does Not Address (Visible from Source Presentation)
- **Koine Verb-First Narrative Syntax:** Baaij does not address whether Paul's placement of *ἐπίστευσεν* at the start of Romans 4:3 could be explained as standard Koine Greek verb-initial narrative style (common in biblical citations) rather than a deliberate, primary re-translation of Hebrew syntax.
- **Early Christian Use of Name Abraham:** Baaij does not address whether the use of the name "Abraham" instead of "Abram" in Romans 4:3 was simply universal early Christian usage (since early Christians consistently called the patriarch Abraham post-Genesis 17) rather than a conscious grammatical correction of the Septuagint text of Genesis 15:6.
- **Verbatim Septuagint Agreement in τῷ θεῷ:** Baaij does not address the fact that the simple dative construction *τῷ θεῷ* in Romans 4:3 was already present verbatim in the Septuagint text of Genesis 15:6 (*καὶ ἐπίστευσεν Ἀβρὰμ τῷ θεῷ*), rather than being independently devised by Paul as a novel *dativus causae*.

#### 4. What Evidence Would Weaken or Falsify the Author's Conclusion
- **Falsification of the Hebrew Text Premise:** Baaij explicitly notes that his thesis rests on the condition: *"Hierbij gaan wij ervan uit dat... de Hebreeuwse tekst die Paulus heeft gekend, niet heeft afgeweken van... de Masoretische tekst"* (Printed p. 243 [PDF p. 5]). If text-critical evidence (such as a Hebrew manuscript variant from Qumran) demonstrated that Paul's Hebrew Vorlage for Genesis 15:6 differed significantly from the Masoretic Text, Baaij's grammatical reconstruction would be undermined.
- **Falsification of Hebrew Retro-Translation:** If historical or linguistic evidence proved that Paul possessed no active competence in Biblical Hebrew and worked exclusively from existing Septuagint Greek exemplars, Baaij's core thesis—that Paul thought in Biblical Hebrew before translating into Greek—would be invalidated (Printed pp. 241–242 [PDF pp. 3–4]).
