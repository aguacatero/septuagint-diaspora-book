# S07F — Counterevidence

## 1. B001-1: Romans 4:3 / Genesis 15:6

### A. Expresses Uncertainty / Explicit Assumptions
- **Assumption Regarding Masoretic Text Identity:** Baaij explicitly acknowledges that his entire exegetical reconstruction relies on the assumption that Paul's Hebrew Vorlage did not deviate from today's Masoretic Text:  
  *Quote:* "Hierbij gaan wij ervan uit dat, wat deze passage aangaat, de Hebreeuwse tekst die Paulus heeft gekend, niet heeft afgeweken van de Hebreeuwse tekst die ons ter beschikking staat, namelijk de Masoretische tekst" (Printed p. 243 [PDF p. 5]).
- **Acknowledging Translation Limitations (*Traduttore Traditore*):** Baaij notes that even when applying Paul's own linguistic tools, all translation inherently involves a degree of distortion:  
  *Quote:* "Niettemin blijft, zelfs onder de door mij genoemde voorwaarden, vertalen toch een vorm van verraden (‘traduttore tradittore’)..." (Printed p. 250 [PDF p. 12]).

### B. Discusses Competing Textual Forms
- **Hebrew Masoretic Text vs. Septuagint (LXX):** Baaij analyzes the divergence between MT Genesis 15:6 (waw + hif'il verb *'āman* followed by *ḥāšaḇ*) and LXX Genesis 15:6 (*καὶ ἐπίστευσεν Ἀβρὰμ τῷ θεῷ καὶ ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ εἰς δικαιοσύνην*). He notes that the LXX places the name "Abram" first and uses passive constructions that obscure Hebrew syntax (Printed p. 244 [PDF p. 6], p. 248 [PDF p. 10]).
- **Polemic Against Septuagint Misleading Readers:** Baaij asserts that relying uncritically on LXX renderings leads translators to teach the opposite of Hebrew Scripture:  
  *Quote:* "de mens het tegenovergestelde van wat de Schrift zegt (Baaij, 2003:145-148)" (Printed p. 253 [PDF p. 15]).
- **Comparison with Dutch Translation Traditions:** Baaij systematically compares Romans 4:3 and Genesis 15:6 across the Statenvertaling, NBG-vertaling (1951), Willibrord-vertaling (1995), and Nieuwe Bijbelvertaling (NBV, 2004), demonstrating how all four misplace the grammatical emphasis by putting "Abram / Abraham" at the beginning of the clause rather than highlighting the active verb "trusted" (*ἐπίστευσεν*) (Printed p. 244 [PDF p. 6], p. 250 [PDF p. 12]).
- **Rejection of Rabbinic / Midrashic Exegesis:** Baaij explicitly notes that the rabbinic/midrashic exegesis developed in Persian and Hellenistic times (later codified in Midrash) is rejected by Paul in Romans as taking human possession of Scripture (Printed p. 243 [PDF p. 5]).

### C. Notes Hebrew / Greek Ambiguity & Semantic Nuances
- **Translation Ambiguity of Hebrew *'āman* (Hif'il):** Citing A. Jepsen (ThWAT I, 320), Baaij notes that the hif'il of *'āman* is difficult to render into Dutch ("moeilijk in onze taal weer te geven"). He evaluates proposed German renderings (*Fest sein*, *Festigkeit haben/gewinnen*) and defines it as an inner-transitive active response of trust to a divine Initiator ("zich toevertrouwen aan") (Printed p. 245 [PDF p. 7]).
- **Semantic Nuances of Hebrew *ḥāšaḇ*:** Citing K. Seybold (ThWAT III, 246–248), Baaij notes the dual aspects of *ḥāšaḇ* ("counting/calculating" vs. "planning/designing/choosing"), emphasizing that attribution proceeds solely from prior divine knowledge (Printed p. 246 [PDF p. 8]).
- **Nuance of *ṣəḏāqāh* vs. *ṣædæq*:** Citing B. Johnson (ThWAT VI, 916) and J. van Dorp (2004), Baaij distinguishes *ṣəḏāqāh* (active, restorative activity re-establishing a relationship) from static principle (*ṣædæq*), translating it as "rechtschapenheid" ("herstel van de relatie tussen God en de door Hem aangesproken mens") (Printed p. 247 [PDF p. 9]).
- **Grammatical Ambiguity of *πιστεύω* Constructions:** Baaij contrasts *πιστεύω* constructed with a simple dative (*τῷ θεῷ*) in Romans 4:3 as a *dativus causae* (highlighting God as Initiator/Cause) with *πιστεύω ἐπί* cum dativo (Rom 9:33; 10:11) and *πιστεύω εἰς* cum accusativo (Rom 4:5, 24) (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]).
- **Passive *λογίζομαι* with *εἰς*:** Baaij notes that Paul uses a passive form of *λογίζομαι* with *εἰς* to translate Hebrew *ḥāšaḇ*, where *εἰς* literally means "towards" ("naar... toe") to stress that righteousness is an unmerited gift from God (Printed p. 249 [PDF p. 11]).

### D. Allows Authorial Adaptation & Non-Literal Translation
- **Intentional Dynamic Re-translation:** Baaij explicitly states that Paul does not translate the Hebrew text literally, but adapts his Greek phrasing to capture the original author's exact intent:  
  *Quote:* "Paulus volgt de grondtekst niet letterlijk, maar geeft de inhoud ervan weer... Paulus wil door zijn weergave duidelijker aangeven waar het de bijbelschrijver om gaat" (Printed p. 249 [PDF p. 11]).
- **Specific Authorial Modifications:**
  - Placing *ἐπίστευσεν* first with postpositive δέ to emphasize active trust (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]).
  - Inserting *τῷ θεῷ* as a *dativus causae* (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]).
  - Replacing the Septuagint's "Abram" (*Ἀβράμ*) with the covenant name "Abraham" (*Ἀβραάμ*) (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]).

### E. Disagrees with Other Scholars
- **C. E. B. Cranfield (1985) & Joseph A. Fitzmyer (1993):** Disagrees with their assertion that Paul must be understood through Hellenistic-Roman culture, maintaining (via W. C. van Unnik) that Paul thought in Biblical Hebrew out of a Pharisaic Jerusalem upbringing (Printed p. 240 [PDF p. 2], p. 242 [PDF p. 4] fn. 3).
- **E. P. Meijering (2003):** Disagrees with Meijering’s claim that Paul quoted Scripture selectively and intervened in or altered Old Testament texts to suit his theology (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10] fn. 8).
- **C. J. den Heijer (1998):** Disagrees with Den Heijer’s characterization of Romans 3:20b and 3:21 as "very negative statements about the Torah", proving that Den Heijer relied on flawed Dutch translations rather than Paul's Hebrew-grounded Greek (Printed p. 251 [PDF p. 13]).
- **J. van Bruggen (2001):** Disagrees with Van Bruggen’s exegetical explanations that rely on Hellenistic Greek assumptions or existing translations (Printed p. 251 [PDF p. 13] fn. 9).
- **Translators of Dutch Bible Versions:** Disagrees with the translators of the Statenvertaling, NBG 1951, Willibrord 1995, and NBV 2004 for misinterpreting Genesis 15:6 and Romans 4:3 (Printed p. 244 [PDF p. 6], p. 250 [PDF p. 12]).

### F. Acknowledges Evidence Weakening Preferred Interpretation
- **Falsification Condition on Hebrew Text:** Baaij explicitly states that if Paul's Hebrew text differed from the Masoretic Text (BHS), his entire grammatical argument would be invalidated:  
  *Quote:* "Hierbij gaan wij ervan uit dat, wat deze passage aangaat, de Hebreeuwse tekst die Paulus heeft gekend, niet heeft afgeweken van de Hebreeuwse tekst die ons ter beschikking staat, namelijk de Masoretische tekst" (Printed p. 243 [PDF p. 5]).

## 2. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16

- **Locator:** Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]; Section 3
- **Discusses Competing Prepositional Forms:** Baaij explicitly contrasts Paul's use of *πιστεύω* with a simple dative (*τῷ θεῷ*) in Romans 4:3 with his use of *πιστεύω ἐπί* with the dative in Romans 10:11 and Romans 9:33 (*πιστεύω ἐπ' αὐτῷ* quoting Isa 28:16), noting that in Romans 10:11 the preposition *ἐπί* shifts the grammatical focus to the divine object of faith (Printed p. 248 [PDF p. 10]).
