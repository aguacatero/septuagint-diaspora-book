## 2. B001-2: Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 [LXX 2:25]
- **Locators:** pp. 128–131, 211–214, 393
- **Competing Textual Forms:** Paul quotes Hosea 2:25 LXX (`καλέσω τὸν οὐ λαόν μου λαόν μου καὶ τὴν οὐκ ἠγαπημένην ἠγαπημένην`), following the Greek text-type represented by Codex Vaticanus (`ἠγαπημένην`) against Codex Alexandrinus (`ἠλεημένην`, reflecting Hebrew `רֻחָמָה`).
- **Authorial Adaptation & Catchword Assimilation:** Wilk notes that Paul changes the verb `ἐρῶ` to `καλέσω` in order to forge an authorial verbal connection with `ἐκάλεσεν` in Romans 9:24 and `κληθήσονται` in Hosea 2:1 / Rom 9:26.
- **Hermeneutical Tension:** Hosea originally addressed the restoration of the northern tribes of Israel; Paul applies `οὐ λαός μου` directly as a prophecy of the conversion of the Gentiles. Wilk acknowledges this hermeneutical transposition, emphasizing that Paul presupposes this Gentile application as self-evident to his readers.

## 3. B001-3: Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 [LXX 2:1]
- **Locators:** pp. 128–131, 185–188, 211–214, 393
- **Competing Textual Forms:** Concatenates Hosea 2:1 LXX verbatim (`καὶ ἔσται ἐν τῷ τόπῳ οὗ ἐρρέθη αὐτοῖς, Οὐ λαός μου ὑμεῖς, ἐκεῖ κληθήσονται υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος`) directly with Hosea 2:25.
- **Geographical / Spatial Ambiguity:** In Hosea 1:10, `ἐν τῷ τόπῳ` referred to Palestine or the land of Israel where apostasy took place; Paul applies it to the Gentile territories where Gentiles lived outside the covenant.
- **Scholarly Disagreements:** Wilk disputes interpretations that treat the Hosea quotations as addressing only ethnic Israel or an undifferentiated remnant, showing that the structure of Rom 9:24 (`οὐ μόνον ἐξ Ἰουδαίων ἀλλὰ καὶ ἐξ ἐθνῶν`) demands a direct Gentile reference.

## 4. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16 (and Romans 9:33)
- **Locators:** pp. 31–34, 45, 47, 54–56, 60–62, 73–75, 82–90, 162–166, 224–226, 390
- **Competing Textual Forms:**
  - *Septuagint Form:* LXX Isaiah 28:16bβ reads `ὁ πιστεύων οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ` (no `πᾶς`, subjunctive with double negative).
  - *Pauline Form (Rom 10:11):* `πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ' αὐτῷ οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται` (prefixes `πᾶς`, adds `ἐπ' αὐτῷ`, substitutes indicative future `οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται`).
  - *Pauline Form (Rom 9:33):* `ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον προσκόμματος καὶ πέτραν σκανδάλου, καὶ ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ' αὐτῷ οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται` (composite with Isa 8:14; lacks `πᾶς`).
- **Authorial Redaction vs. Testimonium Tradition:**
  - Wilk documents that the composite "stone" text combining Isaiah 28:16 and 8:14 also appears in 1 Peter 2:6–8. He evaluates whether Paul created the combination or drew from an existing early Christian testimonium.
  - Wilk concludes that Paul utilized a pre-existing revised Greek Jewish or early Christian compilation for Rom 9:33, but that the addition of `πᾶς` in Rom 10:11 and the indicative future `οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται` are Paul's own stylistic and theological adaptations.
- **Scholarly Disagreements:**
  - Wilk explicitly rejects the hypothesis of Vollmer and Koch that `πᾶς` was an accidental memory slip, demonstrating that `πᾶς` is required by Paul's argument in Rom 10:12–13 to link with Joel 3:5.
  - He disputes exegeses that separate the "stone" in Rom 9:33 (God's judgment) from Rom 10:11 (Christ's grace), showing that Paul maintains a single christological reference across both chapters.

## 5. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 [LXX 3:5]
- **Locators:** pp. 24, 47, 61–62, 164–165, 224, 390
- **Verbatim Textual Form:** Quotes Joel 3:5a LXX verbatim: `πᾶς γὰρ ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα κυρίου σωθήσεται`.
- **Theological Shift in Referent:** In Joel 3:5, `κύριος` translates the Tetragrammaton ($יהוה$). In Romans 10:9–13, Paul applies `κύριος` directly to the risen Jesus. Wilk notes this radical theological transposition, highlighting that early Christian liturgical invocation of Jesus authorized this re-reading of prophetic divine-name texts.
- **Intertextual Catchword Function:** The quotation is anchored by `πᾶς`, directly paralleling the authorially added `πᾶς` in Rom 10:11.

## 6. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13
- **Locators:** pp. 307–311, 358–359, 396–397
- **Textual Addition (`ἤ`):**
  - LXX Isaiah 40:13 reads: `τίς ἔγνω νοῦν κυρίου, καὶ τίς σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο...` (reading `καί`).
  - Paul in Romans 11:34 reads: `τίς γὰρ ἔγνω νοῦν κυρίου; ἢ τίς σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο;` (inserting `ἤ`).
  - *Counterevidence & Manuscript Evidence:* Wilk surveys Greek manuscript witnesses of Isaiah 40:13 and demonstrates that `ἤ` has virtually no independent Septuagint support; Wilk therefore concludes it likely represents Paul's own editorial insertion („dürfte das Wort auf Paulus zurückgehen“), offered as a qualified secondary-author hypothesis rather than an established primary-text certainty.
- **Genre Classification:** Wilk classifies Rom 11:34 not as a formal citation (since it lacks an introductory formula like `γέγραπται`), but as an embedded "citation-like allusion" (`zitatähnliche Anspielung`) serving a hymn-doxology function.

## 7. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10
- **Locators:** pp. 18–19, 46, 146–158, 169–171, 214–216, 237–239, 394
- **Textual Form & Deliberate Omission:**
  - LXX Isaiah 11:10a reads: `καὶ ἔσται ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ ἡ ῥίζα τοῦ Ἰεσσαί...`
  - Romans 15:12 reads: `καὶ πάλιν Ἠσαΐας λέγει, Ἔσται ἡ ῥίζα τοῦ Ἰεσσαί...`
  - Paul omits `ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ` ("in that day"). Wilk notes that this omission is an intentional theological appropriation (`Aneignung`): for Paul, the eschatological Davidic ruler has already arrived in Jesus Christ (Rom 15:8).
- **Hebraizing Revision Uncertainty:** Wilk explores whether Rom 15:7–9a reflects a Hebraizing LXX text (`δόξα` for `תִּפְאֶרֶת` in Isa 11:10b), but cautiously notes on p. 238 n. 28 that direct manuscript evidence for a pre-Pauline Hebraizing revision of Isaiah 11 remains uncertain; this possibility cannot be ruled out, and Wilk's hypothesis of Pauline omission of `ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ` remains a secondary-author claim subject to textual reservation.

## 8. B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14
- **Locators:** pp. 18, 44–45, 101–105, 160–162, 246–250, 393
- **Major Textual Divergence (`ἀθετήσω` vs. `κρύψω`):**
  - LXX Isaiah 29:14b reads: `ἀπολῶ τὴν σοφίαν τῶν σοφῶν καὶ τὴν σύνεσιν τῶν συνετῶν κρύψω`.
  - 1 Corinthians 1:19 reads: `ἀπολῶ τὴν σοφίαν τῶν σοφῶν καὶ τὴν σύνεσιν τῶν συνετῶν ἀθετήσω`.
  - MT reads: `וְאָבְדָה חָכְמַת חֲכָמָיו וּבִינַת נְבֹנָיו תִּסְתַּתָּר` (passive: "the discernment of its discerning ones will hide itself").
- **Analysis of Competing Witnesses:**
  - Wilk notes that while `ἀθετήσω` is found in Septuagint minuscules 301 and 564, as well as patristic citations in Justin Martyr (*Dial.* 32.5; 78.11; 123.4) and Eusebius, these occurrences are demonstrably Christian scribal harmonizations to 1 Corinthians 1:19, not independent Old Greek variants.
- **Intertextual Influence of Psalm 32:10 LXX:**
  - Wilk argues that Paul intentionally selected `ἀθετήσω` under the influence of Psalm 32:10 LXX (`κύριος διασκεδάζει βουλὰς ἐθνῶν, ἀθετεῖ δὲ λογισμοὺς λαῶν...`), because in Pauline theology `κρύπτειν` denotes God's hidden wisdom destined for revelation (1 Cor 2:7), whereas `ἀθετεῖν` expresses God's definitive invalidation of human worldly wisdom.
- **Scholarly Disagreements:**
  - Disagrees with Cerfaux and Smits, who argued Justin Martyr accessed an independent Greek version reading `ἀθετήσω`.
  - Disagrees with Fascher and Weder, who claimed Paul merely intensified the text without specific theological motive.
  - Disagrees with Kautzsch and Koch, who claimed Paul adjusted the verb merely to create mechanical parallelism with `ἀπολῶ`.
  - Disagrees with Vollmer and Munck, who attributed `ἀθετήσω` to an unconscious slip of memory.
