**Ingested File:** `wilk_1998_jesaja_paulus.pdf`  
**Purpose:** Scholarly sources relied upon or disputed by Wilk that bear directly on the textual and exegetical problems across the B001 loci.

## 1. B001-2 & B001-3: Romans 9:25–26 (Hosea 2:23 & 1:10)
- **Dietrich-Alex Koch**, *Die Schrift als Zeuge des Evangeliums: Untersuchungen zur Verwendung und zum Verständnis der Schrift bei Paulus* (Tübingen: Mohr Siebeck, 1986), pp. 165–170.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for structural analysis of quotation chains in Romans 9–11 and the formal characteristics of Pauline introductory formulas.
- **Lucien Cerfaux**, *Vestiges d'un florilège dans Romains 9–11* (1951), in: *Recueil Lucien Cerfaux*, Vol. II (BETL 7; Gembloux: Duculot, 1954), pp. 525–530.
  - *Why it matters:* Discusses the possibility of pre-Pauline Hellenistic Jewish-Christian testimonia collections behind the scriptural chain of Romans 9:25–29.
- **G. Riese**, *Die alttestamentlichen Zitate im Römerbrief* (Diss. theol. Basel, 1978), pp. 434–452.
  - *Why it matters:* Analyzes the hermeneutical transition from Hosea's historical message to Paul's eschatological Gentile-mission justification.

---

## 2. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16 (and Romans 9:33)
- **C. H. Dodd**, *According to the Scriptures: The Sub-Structure of New Testament Theology* (London: Nisbet, 1952), pp. 41–43.
  - *Why it matters:* Seminal treatment of the primitive Christian "Stone Testimonium" combining Isaiah 28:16, Isaiah 8:14, and Psalm 118:22, shared between Romans 9:33 / 10:11 and 1 Peter 2:6–8.
- **Barnabas Lindars**, *New Testament Apologetic: The Doctrinal Significance of the Old Testament Quotations* (London: SCM Press, 1961), pp. 169–186.
  - *Why it matters:* Explores the developmental history of the composite stone citation in early Christian apologetics.
- **Dietrich-Alex Koch**, *Die Schrift als Zeuge des Evangeliums* (1986), pp. 114–119, 160.
  - *Why it matters:* Discusses the text-critical classification of Isaiah 28:16 as a partially revised Greek text ($\mathfrak{G}^R$) and argues that `πᾶς` in Rom 10:11 is an authorial addition preparing for v. 12–13.
- **Hans Vollmer**, *Die alttestamentlichen Citate bei Paulus* (Freiburg: J.C.B. Mohr, 1895), p. 28.
  - *Why it matters:* Disputed. Vollmer viewed the addition of `πᾶς` as an unintentional memory lapse; Wilk refutes this, demonstrating its deliberate theological necessity.

## 3. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 [LXX 3:5]
- **Otto Michel**, *Der Brief an die Römer* (KEK 4; Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 14th ed. 1978), pp. 325–330.
  - *Why it matters:* Relied upon for the exegetical link between the baptismal confession of Jesus as Lord in Rom 10:9 and the invocation of the divine name in Rom 10:13.
- **Wilhelm Bousset**, *Kyrios Christos: Geschichte des Christusglaubens von den Anfängen des Christentums bis Irenaeus* (Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 1913; 5th ed. 1965), pp. 91–98.
  - *Why it matters:* Historical-critical baseline for the transfer of the Old Testament Tetragrammaton / Kyrios title to the exalted Jesus in Hellenistic Christianity.

---

## 4. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13
- **Ernst Käsemann**, *An die Römer* (HNT 8a; Tübingen: Mohr Siebeck, 4th ed. 1980), pp. 308–315.
  - *Why it matters:* Structural analysis of the three-part doxology in Romans 11:33–36 and its citation of Isaiah 40:13 and Job 41:3.
- **Ulrich Wilckens**, *Der Brief an die Römer* (EKK VI/2; Zürich/Neukirchen: Benziger/Neukirchener, 1980), pp. 268–275.
  - *Why it matters:* Detailed commentary on Paul's editorial insertion of `ἤ` to divide Isaiah 40:13 into two parallel rhetorical questions.
- **Robert Hanhart**, *Septuaginta: Vetus Testamentum Graecum, Vol. XIV: Isaias* (Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 1960; 2nd ed. 1984).
  - *Why it matters:* Critical apparatus demonstrating the absence of `ἤ` in primary Septuagint witnesses, establishing `ἤ` as Paul's redactional creation.

---

## 5. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10
- **G. Riese**, *Die alttestamentlichen Zitate im Römerbrief* (1978), pp. 413–415.
  - *Why it matters:* Analyzes the climax of the fourfold citation chain (Ps 17:50; Deut 32:43; Ps 116:1; Isa 11:10) in establishing Jewish-Gentile solidarity.
- **Heinrich Schlier**, *Der Römerbrief* (HTKNT VI; Freiburg: Herder, 1977), p. 425.
  - *Why it matters:* Exegetical treatment of Christ as the Davidic "Root of Jesse" ruling the Gentile nations in eschatological fulfillment.
- **Robert Hanhart**, *Septuaginta: Isaias* (Göttingen, 1984), ad loc.
  - *Why it matters:* Confirms that `ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ` is firmly rooted in Old Greek Isaiah 11:10, establishing that its omission in Romans 15:12 is Paul's deliberate choice.

---

## 6. B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14
- **Lucien Cerfaux**, *Vestiges d'un florilège dans I Cor., I,18–III,23*, in: *Revue d'histoire ecclésiastique* 27 (1931), pp. 521–534.
  - *Why it matters:* Disputed. Cerfaux argued that Justin Martyr (*Dial.* 78.11) quoted Isaiah 29:14 with `ἀθετήσω` independently of Paul. Wilk refutes this, demonstrating that Justin's reading depends directly on 1 Corinthians 1:19.
- **E. Smits**, *Oud-Testamentische Citaten in het Nieuwe Testament* (1952), p. 425.
  - *Why it matters:* Disputed on the same grounds regarding the textual independence of patristic citations.
- **Emil Kautzsch**, *De Veteris Testamenti locis a Paulo Apostolo allegatis* (Leipzig: Metzger & Wittig, 1869), p. 83.
  - *Why it matters:* Disputed. Suggested that `ἀθετήσω` was introduced merely for stylistic parallelism with `ἀπολῶ`; Wilk shows it was chosen for specific theological precision.
- **Dietrich-Alex Koch**, *Die Schrift als Zeuge des Evangeliums* (1986), pp. 44–45.
  - *Why it matters:* Disputed regarding the motivation for `ἀθετήσω`; Koch favored formal parallelism, while Wilk demonstrates cross-scriptural influence from Psalm 32:10 LXX (`κύριος... ἀθετεῖ βουλὰς ἀρχόντων`).
- **Hans Vollmer**, *Die alttestamentlichen Citate bei Paulus* (1895), and **Johannes Munck**, *Christus und Israel: Eine Auslegung von Röm 9–11* (1956).
  - *Why it matters:* Disputed. Both attributed the verb exchange to an unconscious memory lapse; Wilk disproves this through structural contextual analysis.
