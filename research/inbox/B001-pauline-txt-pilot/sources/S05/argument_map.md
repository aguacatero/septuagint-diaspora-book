# S05 — Argument Map

**Source:** Florian Wilk, *Die Bedeutung des Jesajabuches für Paulus* (FRLANT 179; Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 1998)  
**Ingested File:** `wilk_1998_jesaja_paulus.pdf`  
**Method:** Source-bounded mapping following the schema `QUESTION -> EVIDENCE -> INFERENCE -> CONCLUSION`.

---

## 1. B001-2: Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23 [LXX 2:25]

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** How does Paul justify applying the Hosea 2:25 promise to the calling of Gentiles in Romans 9:25–26 alongside the remnant of Israel?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul cites Hosea 2:25aβ.bα.1b LXX (`καλέσω τὸν οὐ λαόν μου λαόν μου καὶ τὴν οὐκ ἠγαπημένην ἠγαπημένην`), using the future verb `καλέσω` picking up the preceding aorist `ἐκάλεσεν` in Rom 9:24 (p. 130).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The citation introduces a chain of scripture quotations in Rom 9:25–29 leading directly into Isaiah 10:22f. and Isaiah 1:9 (pp. 120–131, 211–214).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul reads the biblical phrase `οὐ λαός μου` not historically as the northern kingdom of Israel, but eschatologically as a self-evident prophetic description of the Gentile nations.
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* The pairing of the two Hosea verses establishes that Gentile inclusion into covenant blessings is grounded in sovereign divine calling rather than ethnic descent.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Hosea 2:25 LXX scripturally authorizes Paul's thesis that God's mercy extends beyond ethnic Israel to Gentiles, fulfilling prophetic promises of adoption (pp. 128–131, 393).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul presupposes that his audience reads `οὐ λαός μου` as an unambiguous reference to Gentiles (p. 129).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Wilk addresses whether Paul borrowed a pre-existing Christian testimonium, concluding that while traditional combinations may have existed, Paul's specific syntactic integration into Rom 9:24 reflects authorial design (pp. 128–130).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Wilk does not explore whether Paul consulted the Hebrew text of Hosea 2:25 directly, focusing on his use of the Greek Septuagint form (`ἠγαπημένην`).
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Demonstrating that Paul intended `οὐ λαός μου` exclusively as a typology of apostate Jews rather than Gentiles would undermine the argument for direct Gentile mission justification.

## 2. B001-3: Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10 [LXX 2:1]

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** What function does the addition of Hosea 2:1 LXX serve following Hosea 2:25 in Romans 9:26?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul concatenates Hosea 2:1 LXX (`καὶ ἔσται ἐν τῷ τόπῳ οὗ ἐρρέθη αὐτοῖς, Οὐ λαός μου ὑμεῖς, ἐκεῖ κληθήσονται υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος`) directly to Hosea 2:25 (p. 129).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The verb `κληθήσονται` creates a direct verbal echo with `ἐκάλεσεν` (Rom 9:24) and `καλέσω` (Rom 9:25) (pp. 128–130).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* The locative phrase `ἐν τῷ τόπῳ... ἐκεῖ` emphasizes that in the very sphere where Gentiles were alienated from God (the pagan world), they are now proclaimed sons of the living God.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The combined Hosea citations prove that Gentiles receive full status as `υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος`, preparing the way for the Isaianic remnant theology in Rom 9:27–29 (pp. 129–131, 185–188).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul understands divine sonship (`υἱοθεσία`) as an eschatological blessing now granted in Christ (p. 130).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Wilk discusses whether `ἐν τῷ τόπῳ` refers to Palestine/Jerusalem or the Diaspora, arguing that for Paul's Gentile readers it designates their geographical homeland (p. 129).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The possibility that the combination of Hosea 2:25 and 1:10 was already fixed in Hellenistic synagogue liturgical practice prior to Paul.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Textual evidence showing that Paul's citation Vorlage already possessed `καλέσω` in place of `ἐρῶ` independently of Paul's context.

## 3. B001-4: Romans 10:11 / Isaiah 28:16 (and Romans 9:33)

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul prefix `πᾶς` to Isaiah 28:16bβ in Romans 10:11, and how does he interpret the "stone in Zion" christologically across Romans 9:33 and 10:11?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Rom 10:11, Paul cites Isa 28:16bβ: `πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ' αὐτῷ οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται` (pp. 31–34, 45, 60–62).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Rom 9:33, Paul weaves Isa 28:16 with Isa 8:14 without `πᾶς`: `ἰδοὺ τίθημι ἐν Σιὼν λίθον προσκόμματος... καὶ ὁ πιστεύων ἐπ' αὐτῷ οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται` (pp. 31–34, 54–56, 82–90).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Septuagint Isaiah 28:16 reads `ὁ πιστεύων οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ` (lacking `πᾶς` and reading subjunctive with double negative) (pp. 31, 45).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* The addition of `πᾶς` in Rom 10:11 is Paul's deliberate universalizing expansion, designed to anchor the premise of Rom 10:12 ("there is no distinction between Jew and Greek") and link with `πᾶς` in Joel 3:5 (Rom 10:13).
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* The change from `οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ` to `οὐ καταισχυνθήσεται` is an authorial stylistic harmonization with the surrounding future-tense verbs (Rom 10:9, 13).
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul understands the stone in Zion christologically as Jesus Christ, who grants eschatological vindication to all who believe, Jew and Gentile alike (pp. 60–62, 162–166).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** In Isaiah 28:16, God is the speaker; Paul assumes the foundation stone laid by God is Christ Himself as preached in the gospel (pp. 162–164).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Wilk evaluates whether the composite stone quotation in Rom 9:33 was created by Paul or adopted from early Christian testimonia (parallel to 1 Pet 2:6–8), concluding that Paul utilized an existing Greek revision/composite tradition but adapted it stylistically (pp. 31–34, 54–56).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Wilk does not explore whether `πᾶς` might have existed in an unpreserved Hebrew Vorlage or Qumran variant of Isaiah 28:16.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** If a pre-Christian Septuagint manuscript of Isaiah 28:16 were found containing `πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων`, the addition could no longer be attributed to Paul's universalizing theological redaction.

## 4. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 [LXX 3:5]

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** How does the quotation of Joel 3:5a LXX function in connection with Isaiah 28:16 in Romans 10:11–13?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul quotes Joel 3:5a LXX verbatim: `πᾶς γὰρ ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα κυρίου σωθήσεται` (pp. 24, 61–62).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The conjunction `γάρ` and the initial word `πᾶς` tie the Joel citation directly to the universal scope established in Rom 10:11–12 (pp. 61–62).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul executes a deliberate christological transfer: the Old Testament divine name `κύριος` (YHWH) in Joel 3:5 is applied directly to the confessed Lord Jesus of Rom 10:9 (`κύριον Ἰησοῦν`).
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Joel 3:5 proves scripturally that calling upon the name of the Lord Jesus guarantees eschatological salvation to everyone without ethnic distinction (pp. 61–62, 164–165).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul assumes that early Christian baptismal and liturgical invocation of Jesus as Lord legitimates applying YHWH-texts to Christ (p. 61).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Wilk considers whether `κύριος` in Rom 10:13 remains God the Father, but demonstrates from the immediate context of Rom 10:9–14 that Christ is the exclusive referent (pp. 61–62).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Whether the catchword connection between Isaiah 28:16 and Joel 3:5 via `πᾶς` had already been developed in pre-Pauline Hellenistic Jewish-Christian preaching.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** If Paul's discourse in Rom 10:9–14 separated the confession of Jesus from the invocation of `κύριος` in v. 13, the christological identification would fall.

## 5. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul cite Isaiah 40:13 in Romans 11:34, and why does he insert `ἤ` into the text?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Rom 11:34, Paul cites Isa 40:13 LXX: `τίς γὰρ ἔγνω νοῦν κυρίου; ἢ τίς σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο;` (pp. 307–311).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In LXX Isaiah 40:13, the majority witness reads `τίς ἔγνω νοῦν κυρίου, καὶ τίς σύμβουλος αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο...` (reading `καί` rather than `ἤ`) (p. 308).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Romans 11:33 opens with paired attributes: `ὦ βάθος πλούτου καὶ σοφίας καὶ γνώσεως θεοῦ` (p. 308).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul intentionally substitutes `ἤ` for `καί` to convert the passage into two sharp, balanced rhetorical questions matching the bipartite praise of God's wisdom (`σοφία` -> `σύμβουλος`) and knowledge (`γνῶσις` -> `νοῦς`) in v. 33.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Isaiah 40:13 serves as a citation-like scriptural allusion celebrating the unsearchable depths of God's salvation plan for both Gentiles and all Israel (pp. 307–311, 394).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** The concluding doxology of Romans 11 is Paul's own authorial composition reflecting upon the entire mystery of Rom 9–11 (p. 307).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Wilk considers whether `ἤ` stood in Paul's Septuagint exemplar, but notes its negligible manuscript support in Greek Isaiah witnesses, concluding it is Paul's own stylistic modification (p. 308).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Wilk does not discuss whether Paul's Hebrew knowledge of Isaiah 40:13 (`מִי־תִכֵּן אֶת־רוּחַ יְהוָה וְאִישׁ עֲצָתוֹ יוֹדִיעֶנּוּ`) influenced his understanding of `νοῦς` vs. `רוּחַ`.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Finding an early Septuagint papyrus of Isaiah 40:13 containing `ἤ` would falsify the claim that `ἤ` represents Paul's individual redactional creation.

## 6. B001-7: Romans 15:12 / Isaiah 11:10

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** What is the textual form and function of the Isaiah 11:10 citation in Romans 15:12?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul cites Isaiah 11:10a LXX: `ἔσται ἡ ῥίζα τοῦ Ἰεσσαί, καὶ ὁ ἀνιστάμενος ἄρχειν ἐθνῶν, ἐπ' αὐτῷ ἔθνη ἐλπιοῦσιν` (pp. 18–19, 46, 146–158).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The citation reproduces the Old Greek ($\mathfrak{G}^*$) verbatim, except for the deliberate omission of the temporal marker `ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ` (p. 46).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* It forms the fourth and climactic quotation in the catena of Rom 15:9–12 (Ps 17:50; Deut 32:43; Ps 116:1; Isa 11:10) (pp. 146–158).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* The omission of `ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ` removes the purely future-eschatological horizon of the prophecy: for Paul, the Root of Jesse has already arisen in Jesus Christ.
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* While the three preceding citations commanded the Gentiles to praise God alongside Israel, Isaiah 11:10 provides the christological foundation for that praise: the Messiah actively rules over the nations.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Isaiah 11:10 establishes Christ as the eschatological ruler and common ground uniting Jewish and Gentile believers in mutual acceptance and praise (pp. 158, 394).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul presupposes Jesus' Davidic lineage ("Root of Jesse") as an established historical and theological fact (p. 146).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Wilk discusses whether the catena was pre-Pauline, but shows that its theological progression matches Paul's pastoral argument in Rom 14:1–15:7 (pp. 146–158).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Whether the omission of `ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ` was already present in an abbreviated liturgical quotation circulating in early Christian meetings.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** If pre-Pauline Septuagint manuscripts omitted `ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἐκείνῃ`, the omission could not be claimed as Paul's deliberate theological appropriation.

## 7. B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul substitute `ἀθετήσω` for LXX `κρύψω` in 1 Corinthians 1:19, and how does Isaiah 29:14 anchor his theology of the Cross?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul cites Isaiah 29:14b: `ἀπολῶ τὴν σοφίαν τῶν σοφῶν, καὶ τὴν σύνεσιν τῶν συνετῶν ἀθετήσω` (p. 45, 101–105).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Septuagint Isaiah 29:14b reads `κρύψω` ("I will hide"); MT reads `וּבִינַת נְבֹנָיו תִּסְתַּתָּר` (passive: "shall hide itself") (p. 45).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Psalm 32:10 LXX reads `κύριος... ἀθετεῖ βουλὰς ἀρχόντων` (p. 45).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul deliberately substitutes `ἀθετήσω` ("I will set aside / reject / nullify") for `κρύψω` (likely borrowing the vocabulary from Ps 32:10 LXX) to sharpen the prophetic force: God does not merely conceal human wisdom, He actively invalidates and nullifies it.
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Introduced with `γέγραπται γάρ`, the citation provides immediate scriptural warrant for the programmatic thesis of 1 Cor 1:18 that the word of the Cross is folly to those who perish.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Isaiah 29:14 scripturally authenticates God's eschatological destruction of worldly intellectual pride through the crucified Christ (pp. 101–105, 160–162, 393).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul understands "wisdom" (`σοφία`) polemically to include both Hellenistic philosophical rhetoric and Jewish scribal boasting (pp. 101–103).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Wilk explicitly evaluates and rejects the hypothesis that `ἀθετήσω` was an accidental slip of memory, arguing that its theological precision demonstrates deliberate choice (p. 45).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Wilk does not explore whether an independent Jewish Greek translation tradition of Isaiah had translated `תִּסְתַּתָּר` with `ἀθετέω`.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Discovery of an ancient Greek Old Testament manuscript of Isaiah reading `ἀθετήσω` prior to Paul would demonstrate that Paul inherited the reading rather than creating it.

## 8. B001-12: Galatians 3:13 / Deuteronomy 21:23

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul substitute `ἐπικατάρατος` for Old Greek `κεκατηραμένος` in Galatians 3:13?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Galatians 3:13, Paul cites Deuteronomy 21:23a with `ἐπικατάρατος πᾶς ὁ κρεμάμενος ἐπὶ ξύλου` (p. 47 n. 1).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Old Greek Deuteronomy 21:23a reads `κεκατηραμένος ὑπὸ θεοῦ πᾶς κρεμάμενος ἐπὶ ξύλου` (p. 47 n. 1).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Galatians 3:10, Paul cites Deuteronomy 27:26 opening with `ἐπικατάρατος πᾶς ὃς οὐκ ἐμμένει...` (p. 47 n. 1).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul intentionally exchanges `κεκατηραμένος` for `ἐπικατάρατος` to assimilate the wording of the curse in Gal 3:13 directly to the curse formula established in Gal 3:10.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Galatians 3:13 provides an authorial analogy for deliberate lexical adjustment and assimilation of Old Testament quotations across Paul's letters (p. 47 n. 1).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Wilk follows Dietrich-Alex Koch (*Schrift*, 165f.) in treating the substitution as Paul's own stylistic/theological harmonization (p. 47 n. 1).
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Wilk rejects viewing the lexical variation as indicative of a non-Septuagint Greek translation tradition.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The theological nuance of omitting `ὑπὸ θεοῦ` in Gal 3:13 (which Wilk does not treat in detail, focusing strictly on the lexical substitution as an analogy).
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Proof of a pre-Pauline Greek Pentateuch witness reading `ἐπικατάρατος` in Deuteronomy 21:23a.
