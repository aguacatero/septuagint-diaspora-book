## Methodological Finding Regarding B001 Passages

Because P02F is a manual critical edition of the Greek Old Testament rather than an exegetical monograph or commentary on the New Testament, it contains no explicit authorial argument or commentary on Paul's use of Scripture or the 12 B001 passage comparisons.

The argument maps below trace the editor's explicit methodological rationale in establishing the Greek text and critical apparatus for the Pentateuchal books containing the B001 comparanda (Genesis, Exodus, Deuteronomy).

---

## Reasoning Structures: QUESTION -> EVIDENCE -> INFERENCE -> CONCLUSION

### 1. Selection of Base Text (Diplomatic Foundation)
- **QUESTION:** On what foundation should a manual critical edition of the Septuagint be constructed?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - The existence of great 4th- and 5th-century uncial Bibles: Codex Vaticanus (B), Codex Sinaiticus ($\aleph$), Codex Alexandrinus (A).
  - Codex Vaticanus (B) represents on the whole the oldest, least corrupt, and most homogeneous Greek biblical text surviving from antiquity.
  - Previous manual editions (such as Tischendorf) frequently printed composite or eclectic texts that obscured the testimony of the greatest single manuscript witness.
- **INFERENCE:** A diplomatic edition printing the actual text of the best surviving continuous manuscript, supplemented by a negative apparatus recording the variants of the other primary uncials, offers scholars a far more dependable and verifiable tool than an arbitrary subjective reconstruction.
- **CONCLUSION:** Swete adopts Codex Vaticanus (B) as the running text of the edition wherever extant; where B fails or exhibits lacunae, Codex Alexandrinus (A) is substituted.

---

### 2. Treatment of the Genesis Lacuna (B001-1 and B001-9)
- **QUESTION:** How should the Greek text of Genesis be established given the physical state of the manuscripts?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - Codex Vaticanus (B) contains a major lacuna extending from Genesis 1:1 through Genesis 46:28.
  - Codex Alexandrinus (A, 5th cent.) is well preserved throughout Genesis.
  - Codex Cottonianus (D, 5th/6th cent., damaged by fire) and Codex Bodleianus (E, 8th cent.) preserve important early uncial readings for Genesis.
- **INFERENCE:** Because the volume's standard base text (B) is entirely absent for Genesis 1:1–46:28, another premier uncial must supply the continuous text, while the remaining fragmentary uncials supply variants in the apparatus.
- **CONCLUSION:** For Genesis 1:1–46:28 (including Genesis 2:24 and Genesis 15:6), Swete prints Codex Alexandrinus (A) as the base text, collating Cottonianus (D) and Bodleianus (E) at the foot of each page.

---

### 3. Delimitation of the Apparatus (Manual vs. Comprehensive Edition)
- **QUESTION:** What scope of variants should be included in the critical apparatus of a manual edition?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - The vast mass of cursive (minuscule) manuscripts, daughter versions (Old Latin, Coptic, Ethiopic, Syriac, Armenian), and patristic citations across the Septuagint.
  - The plan of the Cambridge Syndics to produce two distinct works: (1) a smaller portable manual edition for ordinary academic use, and (2) a larger comprehensive critical edition (subsequently edited by Brooke and McLean).
- **INFERENCE:** Attempting to incorporate minuscules or secondary versions into the manual edition would make the volumes unwieldy and duplicate the mandate of the larger edition.
- **CONCLUSION:** Swete strictly restricts the apparatus of the manual edition to the primary extant uncial manuscripts (for the Pentateuch: B, A, D, E, F), providing a clear, manageable record of the earliest uncial variations.

---

### 4. Orthographical and Typographical Conservatism
- **QUESTION:** Should the text standardize historical spellings, itacisms, and grammatical peculiarities to classical Attic standards?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - 4th- and 5th-century Greek scribes regularly employed Hellenistic phonology, itacisms (e.g., `ει` for `ι` and vice versa, as in `πειν` for `πιειν` in Exod 32:6), and Hellenistic accidence.
- **INFERENCE:** Normalizing Hellenistic spelling to classical standards obscures the actual linguistic reality of the Hellenistic Jewish translators and early Christian transmission.
- **CONCLUSION:** Swete faithfully reproduces the orthography of the base manuscript (recording corrections by ancient hands as $B^*$ vs. $B^{ab}$), while editorially supplying breathings, accents, and modern punctuation for reader usability.

---

## Explicit Assumptions Acknowledged by the Editor

1. **Working Manual Text:** Acknowledges that the edition presents the text of a single leading manuscript (Codex Vaticanus) rather than a definitive critical reconstruction of the original Old Greek translation (which remains the task of larger critical projects such as the Cambridge Larger LXX and Göttingen Septuaginta).
2. **Superiority of Vaticanus:** Assumes that Codex Vaticanus, despite occasional scribal errors, contains a text generally superior to the Hexaplaric or Lucianic recensions represented in later witnesses.
3. **Apparatus Neutrality:** The apparatus records what the uncials visibly read; the editor refrains from conjectural emendation or subjective harmonization with the Hebrew Masoretic text.
