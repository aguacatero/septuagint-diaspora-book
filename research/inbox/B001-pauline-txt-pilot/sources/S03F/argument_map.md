# S03F — Argument Map

Reasoning map and analytical breakdown for the substantive and comparative B001 items discussed in Archibald Robertson and Alfred Plummer’s *A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the First Epistle of St Paul to the Corinthians* (ICC, 1911/1914).

Each section explicitly distinguishes **Explicit Source Statements** (direct claims, quotes, and citations from the text) from **Structural Paraphrase** (the formal logical framework).

---

## 1. B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** Why does Paul modify Isaiah 29:14 LXX by substituting ἀθετήσω for κρύψω, and how does this quotation function in his argument regarding worldly wisdom?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul quotes Isaiah 29:14 LXX, replacing κρύψω with ἀθετήσω (p. 19; Intro p. liii).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Isaiah 29:14, the prophet refers to the failure of worldly statesmanship in Judah when faced with the judgment of the Assyrian invasion (p. 19).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The verb ἀθετέω is post-classical, occurring in Polybius and the Septuagint, with an etymological sense of "set aside" or "set at nought" rather than "destroy" (p. 19).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* A similar phrase (ἀθετήσω) appears in Psalm 33:10 LXX (32:10 LXX) (p. 19).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul substitutes ἀθετήσω (where the authors infer he "seems to make changes", possibly influenced by Ps 33:10 LXX) in accordance with his habitual freedom of citation to express that God "sets aside" human intellectual cleverness.
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul seizes the prophetic principle behind the Assyrian crisis—that human political and intellectual schemes fail under divine judgment—and applies it to the "word of the Cross".
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The citation serves as scriptural proof (γέγραπται γάρ) for verse 18, demonstrating the failure of worldly cleverness when dealing with the things of God (p. 19).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Paul assumes freedom of citation when applying Old Testament principles to new epistolary situations.
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** The authors consider whether ἀθετήσω was drawn directly from Psalm 33:10 LXX rather than being an original modification by Paul.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The authors do not address whether Paul cited from an uncollated pre-Pauline Greek Old Testament text that already read ἀθετήσω in Isaiah 29:14.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Finding evidence that ἀθετήσω meant "destroy" rather than "set aside" would invalidate the authors' semantic analysis of the passage.

## 2. B001-9: 1 Corinthians 6:16 / Genesis 2:24

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** How does Paul introduce and apply Genesis 2:24 LXX (ἔσονται... οἱ δύο εἰς σάρκα μίαν) to refute sexual immorality in 1 Corinthians 6:16?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The quotation matches LXX Genesis 2:24 directly, which includes οἱ δύο (noted by the authors as "not in the original" Hebrew text) (p. 126; Intro p. liii).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul introduces the quotation with φησίν, where the understood subject is either "God" or "the Scripture" (ἡ γραφή) (p. 126).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The verb κολλᾶσθαι (with dative) is used in LXX (e.g., 2 Kings 18:6, Deut 10:20, Sirach 2:3) to express loyal, permanent adherence resulting in spiritual union (p. 126).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The word κολλώμενος might come directly from Genesis 2:24 (προσκολλᾶσθαι) or possibly from Sirach 19:2 (ὁ κολλώμενος πόρναις) (p. 126).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* By using φησίν, Paul attaches divine authority to the Septuagint wording (including οἱ δύο).
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul contrasts the permanent physical/spiritual "one flesh" union ordained in creation with the temporary physical union with a harlot.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Joining oneself to a harlot creates a physical union that violates the creation design and robs Christ of the believer's members (pp. 126–127).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** The subject of φησίν carries inherent divine authority, whether understood as God speaking directly or Scripture.
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** The authors consider whether φησίν introduces the objection of an opponent (as in 2 Cor 10:10), but conclude it asserts divine authority here. They also consider Sirach 19:2 as an alternate verbal source alongside Gen 2:24 for κολλᾶσθαι.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The authors do not address whether the Corinthian believers were using a distorted creation slogan to justify sexual freedom. Furthermore, in noting that οἱ δύο is "not in the original", they do not explore whether Paul utilized a variant Hebrew Vorlage sharing Samaritan/LXX readings.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Showing that κολλᾶσθαι carried no connotation of permanent or covenantal adherence would weaken the contrast Paul draws between lawful union and harlotry.

## 3. B001-10: 1 Corinthians 9:9 / Deuteronomy 25:4

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** How does Paul interpret Deuteronomy 25:4 ("you shall not muzzle an ox"), and does he reject its literal meaning?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The manuscript reading κημώσεις is well-attested in 1 Cor 9:9 (B* D* F G, Chrysostom, Theodoret) against φιμώσεις (p. 183; Intro p. lxii).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The authors explicitly note: "It is not easy to decide between φιμώσεις and κημώσεις" (p. 183).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Philo (De Somniis i. 16; De Sacrificiis 1) rejected the literal meaning of Mosaic animal laws, claiming God does not care for irrational brutes (p. 184).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul elsewhere uses Old Testament allegory (Gal 4:24; 2 Cor 3:13) without rejecting the literal historical meaning (p. 184).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* On transcriptional probability grounds, κημώσεις is more likely original in 1 Cor 9:9 because the rare verb would readily be corrected by scribes to the standard LXX text (φιμώσεις).
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul's question ("Is it for oxen that God cares?") is a rhetorical a fortiori argument meaning that the commandment's higher spiritual significance overshadows its minor literal application, not that God disregards animals entirely.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Deuteronomy 25:4 provides a divine principle establishing that Christian ministers have a legitimate right to material support from those they serve (p. 184).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Scripture possesses multiple levels of meaning, where the spiritual application can carry far greater weight than the literal command, without obliterating the literal sense.
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** The authors explicitly weigh the external documentary evidence (Alexandrian and Byzantine witnesses supporting φιμώσεις against Western/neutral witnesses supporting κημώσεις) and consider Philo's radical allegorical view that the literal law is untrue or abandoned.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The authors do not address whether Paul was adopting a widespread Jewish rabbinic legal tradition (*qal wahomer*) that had already extended Deuteronomy 25:4 to human laborers.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** If φιμώσεις were proven to be original, the text-critical argument for κημώσεις as an un-assimilated lectio difficilior would fall.

## 4. B001-11: 1 Corinthians 10:7 / Exodus 32:6

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** What does Exodus 32:6 (ἐκάθισεν ὁ λαὸς φαγεῖν... καὶ ἀνέστησαν παίζειν) mean in 1 Corinthians 10:7, and how do the terms τύποι / τυπικῶς function?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Exodus 32:6 LXX describes the golden calf incident, where "play" (παίζειν) included idolatrous dances (χοροί) following a sacrificial banquet (pp. 203–204).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In 1 Cor 10:6 and 11, τύποι / τυπικῶς refer to warning examples (p. 203).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Origen interprets τύπος as "examples to warn us" (p. 203).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* The quotation highlights an idolatrous banquet followed by idolatrous sport, targeting Corinthian Christians who indulged in social accessories of pagan feasting.
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Understanding τύποι as moral warning examples (patterns) fits the context better than formal redemptive-historical "types".
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Exodus 32:6 serves as an explicit scriptural warning against taking part in idolatrous banquets and associated rites (pp. 203–204).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** The historical experiences of Israel were written as moral admonitions for believers living in the final age.
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Understanding τύποι as formal redemptive-historical "types" (Israelites as types, Corinthians as antitypes). Calvin's explanation of why Paul mentions banquet/sport rather than image adoration—because the Corinthians only indulged in the accessories.
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** The authors do not address whether παίζειν had euphemistic sexual connotations in Hellenistic Jewish exegesis beyond dancing.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Interpreting τύποι as strict redemptive-historical types would imply the absurd conclusion that the Corinthians were predestined to fall just as the Israelites did.

## 5. B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13 (Comparative Cross-Epistle Analysis)

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** How does Paul's quotation of Isaiah 40:13 LXX in 1 Corinthians 2:16 relate textually and exegetically to its parallel citation in Romans 11:34?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* 1 Cor 2:16 quotes Isaiah 40:13 LXX, shortened by the omission of the middle clause: καὶ τίς αὐτοῦ σύνβουλος ἐγένετο; (p. 50).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* This omitted clause is retained in Romans 11:34, while the clause ὃς συνβιβάσει αὐτόν (retained in 1 Cor 2:16) is omitted in Romans (p. 50).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Isaiah 40:13 LXX, νοῦν translates Hebrew רוּחַ (*rūaḥ* / πνεῦμα) (p. 50).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Biblical Greek, συνβιβάζειν means "to instruct" (as frequently in the LXX), with ὅς finding an irregular grammatical antecedent in τίς (p. 50).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Paul immediately transitions from νοῦν Κυρίου (the OT citation) to νοῦν Χριστοῦ (apostolic experience), an equivalence "full of deep significance" (p. 51).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul cites Isaiah 40:13 selectively in different epistolary contexts, retaining the clause that fits his immediate argumentative focus (human inability to instruct God in 1 Cor 2:16 vs unsearchable divine counsel in Rom 11:34).
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* The Septuagint's use of νοῦς (counsel/knowledge) rather than πνεῦμα (divine creative action) provides Paul with the precise conceptual vocabulary needed to argue for the possession of the "mind of Christ" (νοῦν Χριστοῦ) by spiritual persons.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Robertson and Plummer demonstrate a deliberate, context-sensitive cross-epistle citation practice by Paul using different clauses of Isaiah 40:13 LXX, grounded in Septuagintal lexical translation choices (pp. 50–51).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** In God, νοῦς and πνεῦμα are identical in essence but distinct in aspect: νοῦς denotes Divine knowledge or counsel, while πνεῦμα denotes Divine action.
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** The variant reading νοῦν Κυρίου instead of νοῦν Χριστοῦ in 1 Cor 2:16b (weighed and rejected as a scribal harmonization to the preceding quotation).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Whether Paul's omission of the middle clause in 1 Cor 2:16 was influenced by a pre-existing liturgical or catena form of Isaiah 40:13 in the Hellenistic synagogue.
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** If 1 Cor 2:16b originally read νοῦν Κυρίου, the theological transition from Yahweh's mind to Christ's mind would be absent.

## 6. B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 (Secondary Interpretive Cross-Reference)

### Reasoning Map
- **QUESTION:** What is the origin and theological significance of the invocation formula τοῖς ἐπικαλουμένοις in 1 Corinthians 1:2, and how does it relate to Romans 10:12–13?
- **EVIDENCE:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The phrase τοῖς ἐπικαλουμένοις τὸ ὄνομα τοῦ Κυρίου ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ in 1 Cor 1:2 "goes back to Joel ii. 32" (p. 3).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* The authors explicitly cross-reference Romans 10:12, 13 (p. 3).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* In Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5), the formula is addressed to the LORD God of Israel (Yahweh) (p. 3).
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* "Here, as there, St Paul significantly brings in the worship of Christ under the O.T. formula for worship addressed to the LORD God of Israel. To be a believer is to worship Christ" (p. 3).
- **INFERENCE:**
  - *Structural Paraphrase:* Paul adopts the established Septuagint formula for the worship of Yahweh (Joel 2:32 LXX: πᾶς ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου) and directly transfers it to Jesus Christ in both 1 Cor 1:2 and Rom 10:12–13.
- **CONCLUSION:**
  - *Explicit Source Statement:* Robertson and Plummer identify a consistent Pauline hermeneutical practice across 1 Corinthians and Romans whereby the Old Testament Septuagint formula for invocation of Yahweh is christologically applied to Jesus Christ (p. 3).

### Analytical Breakdown
- **Assumptions Explicitly Acknowledged:** Calling upon the name of the Lord constitutes the definitive Old Testament formula for true divine worship.
- **Alternative Explanations Considered:** Whether the phrase might denote only passive naming ("those who are called by the name") rather than active worship/invocation (rejected in favor of active invocation).
- **Alternative Explanations Not Addressed:** Whether the christological transfer of Joel 2:32 had already taken place in early Aramaic-speaking Christian confession prior to Paul (as suggested by Acts 2:21).
- **Falsification / Weakening Evidence:** Finding that ἐπικαλεῖσθαι in this construction meant merely legal naming without connotations of worship would undermine the authors' theological conclusion.
