# S03F — Extraction

Structured extraction of Archibald Robertson and Alfred Plummer's explicit positions and claims across the substantive, extended, and comparative B001 items in their commentary, *A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the First Epistle of St Paul to the Corinthians* (ICC, 1911/1914).

---

## B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14

### S03F-E001
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E001
- **B001 ID:** B001-8
- **Claim Type:** Exegetical / Literary
- **Author's Claim:** Paul quotes Isaiah 29:14 LXX with customary freedom of citation, substituting ἀθετήσω ("set aside") for κρύψω, possibly influenced by Psalm 33:10 LXX, to apply the prophetic principle regarding the failure of worldly human statesmanship.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"substituting ἀθετήσω for κρύψω, in accordance with St Paul’s usual freedom of citation."*
- **Printed Page:** Page 19 (also Introduction § VI, p. liii)
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 1:19, p. 19 (PDF derivative p. 25); Intro p. liii (PDF derivative p. 11)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Substitution of ἀθετήσω for LXX κρύψω in Isaiah 29:14 (possibly influenced by Ps 33:10 LXX [32:10 LXX]).
- **Qualifications:** Authors note ἀθετήσω possibly comes from Ps 33:10 as a cross-scriptural possibility, rather than a proven direct quotation.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

### S03F-E002
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E002
- **B001 ID:** B001-8
- **Claim Type:** Exegetical
- **Author's Claim:** The substituted verb ἀθετήσω is post-classical and frequent in Polybius and the Septuagint, etymologically signifying 'set aside' or 'set at nought' rather than 'destroy.'
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"Its etymological sense is not 'destroy,' but 'set aside' or 'set at nought,'"
- **Printed Page:** Page 19
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 1:19, p. 19 (PDF derivative p. 25)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Lexical usage of ἀθετέω in Polybius, LXX, and New Testament generally, distinguished from ἀπόλλυμι.
- **Qualifications:** None.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

## B001-9: 1 Corinthians 6:16 / Genesis 2:24

### S03F-E003
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E003
- **B001 ID:** B001-9
- **Claim Type:** Textual-critical / Exegetical
- **Author's Claim:** Paul's citation in 1 Corinthians 6:16 is taken directly from the Septuagint of Genesis 2:24, which includes the phrase οἱ δύο ('the two') although the commentary notes it is 'not in the original' (the Hebrew text as represented in standard witnesses), introduced with φησίν to supply divine authority.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"The quotation is direct from the LXX, which has οἱ δύο, ... although it is not in the original."*
- **Printed Page:** Page 126 (also Introduction § VI, p. liii)
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 6:16, p. 126 (PDF derivative p. 37); Intro p. liii (PDF derivative p. 11)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Presence of οἱ δύο in LXX Genesis 2:24 quoted in 1 Cor 6:16 (agreeing with Matt 19:5, Mark 10:8, Eph 5:31).
- **Qualifications:** Authors explain that the subject understood with φησίν depends upon context and may supply either 'God' or ἡ γραφή. Authors note οἱ δύο is 'not in the original' Hebrew text, without asserting MT archetype dogmatism over broader Hebrew textual traditions.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

### S03F-E004
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E004
- **B001 ID:** B001-9
- **Claim Type:** Exegetical
- **Author's Claim:** The verb κολλᾶσθαι with the dative (which the authors note may come from Gen 2:24 προσκολλᾶσθαι or possibly Sirach 19:2 ὁ κολλώμενος πόρναις) denotes loyal and permanent adherence resulting in complete spiritual union, set in sharp contrast to temporary physical union with a harlot.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"to express loyal and permanent adherence, resulting in complete spiritual union. This is placed in marked contrast to the temporary physical union"*
- **Printed Page:** Page 126
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 6:16–17, p. 126 (PDF derivative p. 37)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Construction of κολλᾶσθαι with dative in LXX (2 Kings 18:6, Deut 10:20, Sirach 2:3) for adherence to Jehovah, and possible allusion to Sirach 19:2 (ὁ κολλώμενος πόρναις).
- **Qualifications:** Observes Sirach 19:2 as an alternate or contributing verbal source for Paul's phrasing.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

## B001-10: 1 Corinthians 9:9 / Deuteronomy 25:4

### S03F-E005
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E005
- **B001 ID:** B001-10
- **Claim Type:** Textual-critical
- **Author's Claim:** While noting that 'it is not easy to decide between φιμώσεις and κημώσεις' in 1 Corinthians 9:9, Robertson and Plummer conclude that κημώσεις (attested by B* D* G) is more likely original on transcriptional grounds, since the rare verb would readily be corrected by scribes to match the familiar Septuagint text of Deuteronomy 25:4.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"How could κημώσεις be so well attested, if it were not original? If it were original it would readily be corrected to the LXX"*
- **Printed Page:** Page 183 (also Introduction § VII, p. lxii)
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 9:9, p. 183 (PDF derivative p. 45); Intro p. lxii (PDF derivative p. 18)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Manuscript witnesses supporting κημώσεις (B* D* F G, Chrysostom, Theodoret) versus φιμώσεις (Sinaiticus A B3 C D3 E K L P).
- **Qualifications:** Authors explicitly open by stating 'It is not easy to decide between φιμώσεις and κημώσεις,' then weigh external evidence and transcriptional probability favoring κημώσεις as the rarer term.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

### S03F-E006
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E006
- **B001 ID:** B001-10
- **Claim Type:** Exegetical / Methodological
- **Author's Claim:** Unlike Philo and radical allegorists who rejected the literal sense of Mosaic animal commands, Paul does not reject the literal command of Deuteronomy 25:4 but reasons that it embodies a higher spiritual principle establishing the right of Christian laborers to maintenance.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"St Paul elsewhere allegorizes the O.T., ... but in neither case does he reject the literal meaning. It is not probable that he does so here"*
- **Printed Page:** Page 184
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 9:9–10, p. 184 (PDF derivative p. 46)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Comparison of Paul's exegesis in 1 Cor 9:9–10 with Philo (De Somniis i. 16, De Sacrificiis 1) and Epistle of Barnabas x. 1–2.
- **Qualifications:** Authors explicitly state that even if πάντως is translated 'altogether,' it need not be pressed to mean that oxen are not cared for at all by God (citing Ps 104:14, 145:9, Matt 6:26).
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

## B001-11: 1 Corinthians 10:7 / Exodus 32:6

### S03F-E007
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E007
- **B001 ID:** B001-11
- **Claim Type:** Exegetical
- **Author's Claim:** The quotation in 1 Corinthians 10:7 is taken verbatim from the Septuagint of Exodus 32:6, where 'play' (παίζειν) denotes idolatrous choral dances around the golden calf following a sacrificial banquet, serving as a warning against Corinthian participation in pagan temple feasts.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"The quotation, therefore, indicates an idolatrous banquet followed by idolatrous sport."*
- **Printed Page:** Pages 203–204
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 10:7, pp. 203–204 (PDF derivative pp. 53–54)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Verbatim quotation of LXX Exodus 32:6 (Ἐκάθισεν ὁ λαὸς φαγεῖν καὶ πεῖν, καὶ ἀνέστησαν παίζειν).
- **Qualifications:** Direct agreement with LXX Exodus 32:6. Connects with Calvin's query regarding accessories vs essence of idolatry.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

### S03F-E008
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E008
- **B001 ID:** B001-11
- **Claim Type:** Exegetical / Literary
- **Author's Claim:** In 1 Corinthians 10:6 and 11, the terms τύποι and τυπικῶς do not denote predictive redemptive-historical 'types' (which would imply predestined apostasy for the Corinthians), but historical patterns and warning examples for moral guidance.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"This confirms the view that τύπος does not mean 'types,' but examples for guidance, 'to the intent that we should not be.'"*
- **Printed Page:** Page 203
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 10:6, p. 203 (PDF derivative p. 53)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Occurrence of τύποι in 1 Cor 10:6, τυπικῶς in 1 Cor 10:11, and Origen's rendering of τύπος as 'example'.
- **Qualifications:** Understanding τύποι as redemptive-historical types would imply that the Corinthians were predestined to fall as the Israelites did.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

## B001-6: Romans 11:34 / Isaiah 40:13 (Comparative Cross-Epistle Evidence)

### S03F-E013
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E013
- **B001 ID:** B001-6
- **Claim Type:** Textual-critical / Exegetical
- **Author's Claim:** In 1 Corinthians 2:16, Paul cites Isaiah 40:13 from the Septuagint with an omission of the middle clause (καὶ τίς αὐτοῦ σύνβουλος ἐγένετο;) which is retained in Romans 11:34, while the clause ὃς συνβιβάσει αὐτόν retained in 1 Corinthians is omitted in Romans; furthermore, LXX νοῦν corresponds to Hebrew רוּחַ (πνεῦμα).
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"From Isa. xl. 13 (LXX), but shortened by the omission of the middle clause, καὶ τίς αὐτοῦ σύνβουλος ἐγένετο; This clause is retained in Rom. xi. 34, while ὃς συνβιβάσει αὐτόν is omitted... νοῦν Κυρίου. The νοῦν (LXX) corresponds to the Hebrew for πνεῦμα in the original."*
- **Printed Page:** Pages 50–51
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 2:16, pp. 50–51 (PDF derivative pp. 32–33; Raw scan pp. 126–127)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Cross-epistle textual comparison between 1 Cor 2:16 and Rom 11:34 on Isa 40:13 LXX; lexical analysis of LXX νοῦν translating Hebrew רוּחַ (*rūaḥ* / πνεῦμα); textual variant Χριστοῦ vs Κυρίου in 1 Cor 2:16b.
- **Qualifications:** Substantive cross-epistle comparison analyzing Pauline selective quotation and LXX lexical divergence. Visibly verified on printed pp. 50–51.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

## B001-5: Romans 10:13 / Joel 2:32 (Secondary Interpretive Cross-Reference)

### S03F-E014
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E014
- **B001 ID:** B001-5
- **Claim Type:** Exegetical
- **Author's Claim:** In 1 Corinthians 1:2, the invocation formula τοῖς ἐπικαλουμένοις τὸ ὄνομα τοῦ Κυρίου ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ goes back to Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5) and connects directly to Romans 10:12–13, significantly applying to Christ the Old Testament formula of worship addressed to Yahweh.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"τοῖς ἐπικαλουμένοις. This goes back to Joel ii. 32, and involves the thought of faith, the common bond of all. See Rom. x. 12, 13. Here, as there, St Paul significantly brings in the worship of Christ under the O.T. formula for worship addressed to the LORD God of Israel."*
- **Printed Page:** Page 3
- **Source Locator:** Commentary on 1 Cor 1:2, p. 3 (PDF derivative p. 22; Raw scan p. 79)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Invocation formula ἐπικαλεῖσθαι τὸ ὄνομα in Joel 2:32 (LXX 3:5), Rom 10:12–13, and 1 Cor 1:2; LXX transfer of divine address to Jesus Christ.
- **Qualifications:** Relevant secondary interpretive cross-reference connecting 1 Cor 1:2 and Rom 10:12–13 through shared OT invocation formula Joel 2:32. Visibly verified on printed p. 3.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

## B001-GENERAL: General Pauline Quotation Methodology

### S03F-E009
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E009
- **B001 ID:** B001-GENERAL
- **Claim Type:** Textual-critical
- **Author's Claim:** Robertson and Plummer catalog approximately thirty Old Testament quotations in 1 Corinthians across eleven different biblical books, concluding that about twenty-five are in exact or substantial agreement with the Septuagint.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"Out of these thirty quotations from the O.T., about twenty-five are in exact or substantial agreement with the LXX"*
- **Printed Page:** Page liii
- **Source Locator:** Introduction § VI: 'Quotations from the O.T.', p. liii (PDF derivative p. 11)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Comprehensive statistical catalog of 30 citations in 1 Corinthians (7 in exact agreement, 18 in substantial agreement).
- **Qualifications:** Authors note exact figures cannot always be given because some quotations resemble multiple OT passages or conflate two contexts.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

### S03F-E010
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E010
- **B001 ID:** B001-GENERAL
- **Claim Type:** Literary
- **Author's Claim:** Robertson and Plummer judge that all Old Testament quotations in 1 Corinthians are short and were probably cited from memory, in marked contrast to the long citations in the Epistle to the Hebrews which were in most cases copied from a scroll.
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"All the quotations are short, and it is probable that all of them were made from memory. There are no long citations, such as we have in Hebrews"*
- **Printed Page:** Pages lii–liii
- **Source Locator:** Introduction § VI: 'Quotations from the O.T.', pp. lii–liii (PDF derivative pp. 10–11)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Contrast in quotation length, formulaic introduction, and verbatim correspondence between Pauline epistles and Hebrews.
- **Qualifications:** Direct reference to Sanday & Headlam's Romans essay (pp. 302–307) for fuller discussion of Pauline memory citation.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

### S03F-E011
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E011
- **B001 ID:** B001-GENERAL
- **Claim Type:** Literary
- **Author's Claim:** Robertson and Plummer judge that Paul sometimes 'seems to make changes' in order to produce a wording more suitable for his immediate argument, such as substituting ἀθετήσω for κρύψω in 1 Cor 1:19 (Isa 29:14) and σοφῶν for ἀνθρώπων in 1 Cor 3:20 (Ps 94:11).
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"Sometimes he seems to make changes in order to produce a wording more suitable for his argument"*
- **Printed Page:** Page liii
- **Source Locator:** Introduction § VI: 'Quotations from the O.T.', p. liii (PDF derivative p. 11)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Textual variants introduced into citations: 1 Cor 1:19 (Isa 29:14 ἀθετήσω vs κρύψω), 1 Cor 3:20 (Ps 94:11 σοφῶν vs ἀνθρώπων).
- **Qualifications:** Framed with authorial reserve ("seems to make changes") regarding conscious argumentative adaptation vs textual tradition.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH

### S03F-E012
- **Evidence ID:** S03F-E012
- **B001 ID:** B001-GENERAL
- **Claim Type:** Textual-critical
- **Author's Claim:** Where Pauline quotations diverge from the Septuagint, variations sometimes bring the text closer to the Hebrew (e.g. 1 Cor 15:54 / Isa 25:8), or reflect preference for the Hebrew or an unknown Greek translation (1 Cor 1:20, 3:19, 14:25, 15:54), while occasionally diverging from both (1 Cor 2:9, 14:21).
- **Supporting Quotation:** *"Sometimes the variations from the LXX bring the citation closer to the Hebrew, as if the Apostle were consciously or unconsciously guided by the Hebrew"*
- **Printed Page:** Pages liii–liv
- **Source Locator:** Introduction § VI: 'Quotations from the O.T.', pp. liii–liv (PDF derivative pp. 11–12)
- **Textual Evidence Invoked:** Detailed four-fold classification of divergences from the LXX: (1) closer to Hebrew, (2) unknown Greek translation, (3) free paraphrase, (4) composite citation.
- **Qualifications:** Authors note uncertainty whether guidance by Hebrew text was conscious or unconscious, or mediated via lost Greek versions.
- **Extraction Confidence:** HIGH
