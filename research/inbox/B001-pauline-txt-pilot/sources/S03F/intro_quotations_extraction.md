# S03F — Intro Quotations Extraction

Here is the extraction from Archibald Robertson and Alfred Plummer’s A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the First Epistle of St Paul to the Corinthians (1914), taken directly from Introduction § VI ("Quotations from the O.T.", pp. lii–liv) and § VII ("Text of the First Epistle to the Corinthians", pp. lv–lxii).
1. Total Number and Distribution of Old Testament Quotations
Total Number: "The number of quotations in 1 Corinthians is about thirty, and none of the Epistles has so many, excepting Romans and Hebrews; and none quotes from so many different books, excepting Romans." [p. lii]
Distribution Across 11 Books: "In 1 Corinthians, eleven different books are quoted; Isaiah about eight times, Psalms four or five times, Deuteronomy four times, Genesis four, Exodus two or three, Numbers once or twice, Zechariah once or twice; Job, Jeremiah, Hosea, Malachi, once each." [p. lii]
Qualifications on Exact Counting: "In several cases the quotation resembles more than one passage in the O.T., and we cannot be sure which passage the Apostle has in his mind. In other cases there is a conflation of two passages, both of which are clearly in his mind. Consequently, exact numbers cannot always be given." [p. lii]
2. Proportion of Quotations Agreeing with LXX vs. Hebrew
LXX Agreement: "Out of these thirty quotations from the O.T., about twenty-five are in exact or substantial agreement with the LXX, and this is in accordance with evidence derived from the other Epistles." [p. liii]
Exact LXX Agreement: 7 instances (e.g., 1 Cor 6:16 = Gen 2:24; 9:9 = Deut 25:4; 10:7 = Exod 32:6) [p. liii].
Substantial LXX Agreement: 18 instances (e.g., 1 Cor 1:19 = Isa 29:14; 1:31 = Jer 9:24; 3:20 = Ps 94:11) [p. liii].
Divergence Guided by the Hebrew: "Sometimes the variations from the LXX bring the citation closer to the Hebrew, as if the Apostle were consciously or unconsciously guided by the Hebrew in diverging from the LXX, e.g. in xv. 54 = Isa. xxv. 8." [p. liii]
Preference for Hebrew / Unknown Versions: "In a few cases he seems to show a preference for the Hebrew, or possibly for some version not known to us," citing 1 Cor 1:20 (= Isa 19:11 f., 33:18), 3:19 (= Job 5:13), 14:25 (= Isa 45:14), and 15:54 (= Isa 25:8) [p. liv].
Divergence from Both LXX and Hebrew: "divergence from both the Hebrew and the LXX" occurs in 1 Cor 2:9 (= Isa 64:4, 65:17) and 14:21 (= Isa 28:11) [p. liv].
3. Quotation from Memory vs. Copying from a Roll (Comparison with Hebrews)
Quotation from Memory: "All the quotations are short, and it is probable that all of them were made from memory." [p. lii]
Comparison with Hebrews: "There are no long citations, such as we have in Hebrews, which no doubt were in most cases copied." [p. liii]
4. Contextual and Argumentative Modifications by Paul
Argumentative Adaptation: "Sometimes he seems to make changes in order to produce a wording more suitable for his argument..." [p. liii]
Specific Examples:
1 Cor 3:20 / Ps 94:11: "...e.g. in iii. 20 = Ps. xciv. 11, where he substitutes σοφῶν for ἀνθρώπων..." [p. liii]
1 Cor 1:19 / Isa 29:14: "...or in i. 19 = Isa. xxix. 14, where he substitutes ἀθετήσω for κρύψω (cf. Ps. xxxiii. 10)." [p. liii]
5. Manuscripts and Textual Authorities for 1 Corinthians
A. Textual Classes / Families (pp. lv–lvi)
"Syrian" (Received Text): An "eclectic text which became dominant and which is represented in the greater number of later MSS." [p. liv]
"Western": Readings that "remount to an antiquity higher than that of any extant version... common to the Greek text embodied in many early MSS., and to the early versions, especially the Old Latin." [p. lv]
"Alexandrian": Readings "due to editorial revision rather than to transcriptional licence... mainly preserved in texts of Egyptian provenance" (ℵ, A, C, P, Egyptian Versions) [pp. lv–lvi].
"Neutral": Represented pre-eminently by B (Codex Vaticanus) and ℵ (Codex Sinaiticus). "B is the most constant single representative of the 'Neutral' type of text... ℵ, on the other hand... is freer from such traces in the Pauline Epistles than elsewhere."
