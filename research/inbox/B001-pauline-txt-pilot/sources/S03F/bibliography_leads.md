Here is the breakdown of the footnoted and bibliographical sources cited, relied upon, or disputed by Archibald Robertson and Alfred Plummer across each substantive B001 passage in their commentary (A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the First Epistle of St Paul to the Corinthians, 1914).
General Introduction on OT Quotations (Intro, pp. lii–liv)
William Sanday and Arthur C. Headlam
Title: A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans (ICC)
Year: 1895 (implied)
Pages: Pages 302–307 [Intro p. lii]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Recommended as the foundational essay for understanding the principles, text-types, and methods governing Paul's Old Testament quotations.
Henry Barclay Swete
Title: An Introduction to the Old Testament in Greek
Year: Not stated (c. 1900/1902)
Pages: Pages 381–405 [Intro p. lii, liii]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Used to define criteria for direct quotations versus general scriptural allusions in Greek New Testament studies.
1. B001-8: 1 Corinthians 1:19 / Isaiah 29:14 (pp. 19–20)
Philipp Bachmann
Title: Der erste Brief des Paulus an die Korinther (cited on p. 19, fn. †)
Year: Not specified in footnote
Pages: Cited on source p. 19
Why it matters: Relied upon. Demonstrates the historical parallel between the political crisis under Assyrian threat in Isaiah 29/33 and Paul's polemic against worldly cleverness in 1 Corinthians 1.
Heinrich August Wilhelm Meyer
Title: Kritisch-exegetischer Kommentar über das Neue Testament (1 Corinthians)
Year: Not specified
Pages: Cited on source p. 19–20
Why it matters: Disputed. Meyer assigned σοφός generically, γραμματεύς to Jews, and συνζητητής to Greeks in 1 Cor 1:20. Robertson and Plummer reject this as an over-rigid classification.
Charles John Ellicott
Title: St. Paul's First Epistle to the Corinthians: with a Critical and Grammatical Commentary
Year: 1887 (implied)
Pages: Cited on source p. 20
Why it matters: Disputed. Follows Meyer's rigid tripartite division of terms. Robertson and Plummer favor Clement of Alexandria and Theodoret's broader view.
Clement of Alexandria (Stromateis) & Theodoret of Cyrrhus (Commentary on 1 Corinthians)
Year: Patristic period
Pages: Cited on source p. 19
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited against Meyer and Ellicott for a more natural, non-exclusive allotment of σοφός, γραμματεύς, and συνζητητής.
2. B001-9: 1 Corinthians 6:16 / Genesis 2:24 (pp. 126–129)
Epistle of Barnabas
Title: Epistula Barnabae (ix. 9, x. 11, xix. 2, xx. 2)
Year: Early 2nd Century AD
Pages: Cited on source p. 126
Why it matters: Relied upon. Documents parallel early Christian usage of the verb κολλᾶσθαι with the dative to denote permanent spiritual adherence.
Epictetus
Title: Discourses (Dissertationes, ii. 8)
Year: 1st/2nd Century AD
Pages: Cited on source p. 128 (and Long's translation, Vol. I, pp. 156, 157, 288)
Why it matters: Relied upon / Illustrative Parallel. Provides a Stoic parallel on carrying God within oneself and polluting the indwelling divine presence through unchaste deeds.
Edwin A. Abbott
Title: The Son of Man
Year: 1910 (implied)
Pages: Page 702 [cited on p. 129]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Supports the exegetical point on 1 Cor 6:20 (ἠγοράσθητε τιμῆς) that Paul specifies no person from whom believers are bought.
Adolf Deissmann
Title: Bible Studies
Year: 1901 (implied)
Pages: Pages 201 f. [cited on p. 128, fn.]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited for grammatical analysis of ἐάν in relative clauses.
3. B001-10: 1 Corinthians 9:9 / Deuteronomy 25:4 (pp. 183–184)
Philo of Alexandria
Title: De Humanitate; De Specialibus Legibus i.; De Somniis i. 16; De Sacrificiis Abelis et Caini 1
Year: 1st Century AD
Pages: Cited on source pp. 183–184
Why it matters: Disputed. Philo argued that Mosaic animal laws were not intended for irrational brutes, abandoning the literal sense for radical allegory. Robertson and Plummer dispute Philo's rejection of the literal meaning, arguing that Paul retains the literal principle while applying its higher spiritual import.
Samuel Rolles Driver
Title: A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on Deuteronomy (ICC)
Year: 1895 (implied)
Pages: On Deut. xxv. 4 [cited on p. 183]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited regarding the humanitarian character of Deuteronomy and modern Eastern threshing practices (referencing Conder, Robinson, etc.).
Epistle of Barnabas
Title: Epistula Barnabae (x. 1, 2, xi. 1)
Year: Early 2nd Century AD
Pages: Cited on source p. 184
Why it matters: Disputed. Cited alongside Philo as an example of abandoning the literal commandment.
Saint John Chrysostom & Theodoret of Cyrrhus
Year: Patristic period
Pages: Cited on source p. 183
Why it matters: Relied upon. Both patristic commentators support the unassimilated manuscript reading κημώσεις in 1 Cor 9:9 against φιμώσεις.
H. St. John Thackeray
Title: A Grammar of the Old Testament in Greek
Year: 1909 (implied)
Pages: Pages 193 f. [cited on p. 184]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited for lexical/grammatical analysis of οὐ πάντως vs. πάντως.
Heinrich Weinel
Title: St. Paul: The Man and His Work
Year: 1906 (implied)
Pages: Pages 53, 59 [cited on p. 184]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited on Paul's interpretation of Deuteronomy 25:4 and πάντως.
Gotthold Resch
Title: Agrapha: Aussercanonische Evangelienfragmente
Year: 1889/1906
Pages: Pages 30, 152, 336 [cited on p. 184]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited regarding the introductory formula δι' ἡμᾶς γὰρ ἐγράφη.
4. B001-11: 1 Corinthians 10:7 / Exodus 32:6 (pp. 203–204)
Origen
Title: Commentary on 1 Corinthians / Homilies
Year: 3rd Century AD
Pages: Cited on source p. 203
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited for understanding τύποι as warning examples rather than formal redemptive-historical types.
George Milligan
Title: St. Paul's Epistles to the Thessalonians
Year: 1908 (implied)
Pages: Note on 1 Thess. i. 7 [cited on p. 203]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited for the semantic development of τύπος from "stamp/mark" to "pattern/example."
John Calvin
Title: Commentary on the First Epistle to the Corinthians
Year: 1546 (implied)
Pages: Cited on source pp. 203–204
Why it matters: Discussed / Relied upon. Calvin explains why Paul mentions the banquet and sport rather than direct image adoration—because Corinthian believers indulged in the social accessories of pagan feasting without thinking they were committing idolatry.
Benjamin Jowett
Title: The Epistles of St. Paul to the Thessalonians, Galatians, Romans ("On the Connexion of Immorality and Idolatry")
Year: 1855/1859
Pages: Volume II, page 70 [cited on p. 204]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited for the close historical connection between pagan idolatry and sexual immorality in ancient Corinth.
G. Buchanan Gray
Title: A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on Numbers (ICC)
Year: 1903 (implied)
Pages: Pages 380 f. [cited on p. 204, fn. †]
Why it matters: Relied upon. Cited for source-critical analysis of the Baal-Peor narrative in Numbers 25.
(Note: B001 items 1 through 7 and item 12 pertain to Romans and Galatians and are NOT DISCUSSED in this volume).
