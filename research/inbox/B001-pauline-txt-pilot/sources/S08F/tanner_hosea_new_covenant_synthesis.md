# S08F — Tanner Hosea New Covenant Synthesis

**Source:** J. Paul Tanner, *The New Covenant and Paul’s Quotations from Hosea in Romans 9:25–26*, *Bibliotheca Sacra*, Vol. 162, No. 645 (January–March 2005), pp. 95–110.

---

## 1. Romans 9:25–26: Composite Citation & Relation to Isaiah Remnant Quotations (Rom 9:27–29)

### Composite Quotation Structure
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed p. 98 [PDF p. 4]
- **Analysis:** In Romans 9:25–26, Paul combines Hosea 2:23 and Hosea 1:10b into a single continuous composite quotation: *"I will call those who were not My people, 'My people,' and her who was not beloved, 'Beloved' . . . And it shall be that in the place where it was said to them, 'you are not My people,' there they shall be called sons of the living God"* (Tanner 2005:98).

### Relation to Isaiah 10:22–23 and 1:9 in Romans 9:27–29
- **Printed Page Numbers:** Printed pp. 97–98 [PDF pp. 3–4]
- **Analysis:** Tanner points out that Paul uses two pairs of Old Testament quotations in tandem. The Isaiah citations (Isa 10:22–23 and 1:9) are introduced by *"Isaiah cries out concerning Israel,"* supporting the inclusion of a believing Jewish remnant. Conversely, the Hosea citations serve to establish that God's "vessels of mercy" include believing Gentiles (Tanner 2005:98).
- **Exact Quotation:** *"Thus Paul used this string of quotations to prove that God's mercy has been extended to a believing remnant within Israel and to prove that it has been extended to Gentiles"* (Tanner 2005:98).

### Structural Sandwiching in Romans 9
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed p. 97 [PDF p. 3]
- **Analysis:** The four OT quotations in Romans 9:25–29 are structurally bracketed between verses 22–24 and verses 30–31 to demonstrate that divine election extends to both Jew and Gentile on the basis of mercy (Tanner 2005:97–98).

## 2. Textual Relationship: Paul’s Quotation vs. Septuagint (LXX) & Masoretic Text (MT)

### Dependence on a Greek Vorlage
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed p. 99 [PDF p. 5]
- **Analysis:** Tanner notes that Paul cited from a Greek rendering of Hosea rather than directly translating the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Tanner 2005:99).

### Reversal of Clause Order in Hosea 2:23
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed p. 99 [PDF p. 5]
- **Analysis:** Paul inverted the two clauses of Hosea 2:23 in Romans 9:25. In the original Hebrew MT and Septuagint, the compassion clause precedes the people clause; Paul flips them to put "My people" first (Tanner 2005:99).
- **Exact Quotation:** *"...this is only an illusion created by the fact that Paul quoted from a Greek rendering of Hosea and the fact that he reversed the order of the clauses (perhaps to stress the 'My people' aspect of the promise and God's 'call' to them)"* (Tanner 2005:99).

### Modification of the Main Verb (*καλέσω* vs. *ἐρῶ* / *'āmar*)
- **Printed Page Numbers:** Printed p. 99 [PDF p. 5], fn. 4
- **Analysis:** Relying on Douglas Moo's textual analysis, Tanner notes that Paul intentionally substituted Hosea’s original verb "I will say" (MT *וְאָמַרְתִּי* / LXX *ἐρῶ*) with "I will call" (*καλέσω*) to match his argument regarding divine election in Romans 9:24 ("whom He also called") (Tanner 2005:99, fn. 4).
- **Exact Quotation:** *"By reversing the order of the clauses in his quotation of Hos. 2:23, Paul is able to put this verb at the beginning of his composite quotation from Hosea"* (Tanner 2005:99, fn. 4, quoting Moo).

### Selective Omission of Hosea 1:10a
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed p. 99 [PDF p. 5]
- **Analysis:** In quoting Hosea 1:10 in Romans 9:26, Paul selectively omitted verse 10a (the Abrahamic "sand of the sea" promise made to Israel) and quoted only verse 10b ("sons of the living God") (Tanner 2005:99–100).

## 3. The Hermeneutical Problem: Applying Israel's Promises to Gentiles

### Original Historical Setting of Hosea 1–2
- **Printed Page Numbers:** Printed pp. 99–100 [PDF pp. 5–6]
- **Analysis:** In their 8th-century B.C. context, *Lo-ruhamah* ("no compassion") and *Lo-ammi* ("not My people") represented the northern kingdom of Israel being disowned and exiled by God under Assyrian judgment. Hosea predicted their future restoration, with no direct mention of Gentiles (Tanner 2005:99–100).
- **Exact Quotation:** *"Nothing in the context of these passages makes reference to Gentiles, nor did Hosea imply that the fulfillment of these promises would be with Gentiles"* (Tanner 2005:100).

### The Core Hermeneutical Tension
- **Printed Page Number:** Printed p. 101 [PDF p. 7]
- **Analysis:** The problem is explaining how statements originally directed to unfaithful ethnic Israel can legitimately be cited by Paul as proof for Gentile salvation in the church (Tanner 2005:101).

### Tanner’s Solution: Direct, Partial Phase of Fulfillment
- **Printed Page Numbers:** Printed pp. 101–102 [PDF pp. 7–8]
- **Analysis:** Tanner refutes the idea that Paul misused, spiritualized, or altered Hosea's meaning. Nor did Paul cite Hosea as a mere general analogy. Instead, Paul recognized that Gentile inclusion represents a direct, partial phase of fulfillment of Hosea's prophecy, which does not cancel the future fulfillment reserved for ethnic Israel (Tanner 2005:101–103).
- **Exact Quotation:** *"His point in Romans 9 was not to deny a fulfillment with Israel but only to affirm a fulfillment also with Gentiles"* (Tanner 2005:101).

## 4. Tanner's New Covenant Framework

### The Mechanism of Status Change
- **Printed Page Numbers:** Printed pp. 106–108 [PDF pp. 12–14], p. 110 [PDF p. 16]
- **Analysis:** Tanner asserts that what makes the status change from "not My people" to "My people" possible is full participation in the New Covenant (Jer 31:31–34; Ezek 36:24–28; Hos 2:18) (Tanner 2005:106–110).

### Progressive Revelation & Historic Realization
- **Printed Page Numbers:** Printed pp. 103–105 [PDF pp. 9–11]
- **Analysis:** Through historic events (Pentecost, Cornelius' household in Acts 10–11) and direct revelation of the "mystery" (Eph 3:3), the early apostles came to realize that believing Gentiles receive the Holy Spirit equally with believing Jews, establishing them as full participants in New Covenant spiritual blessings (Tanner 2005:103–105).

### Rejection of Replacement Theology (Supersessionism)
- **Printed Page Numbers:** Printed pp. 95–96 [PDF pp. 1–2], p. 101 [PDF p. 7], pp. 109–110 [PDF pp. 15–16]
- **Analysis:** Tanner explicitly rejects replacement theology (the view that the church replaces Israel or becomes "spiritual Israel") (Tanner 2005:95–96, 101, 103, 110).
- **Exact Quotation:** *"In this esteemed status, however, believing Gentiles are not Israel or some kind of spiritual Israel. Yet as New Covenant participants, they fully are 'the people of God'..."* (Tanner 2005:110).

### Distinction Between Shared Spiritual Blessings & National Promises
- **Printed Page Numbers:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12], fn. 16; p. 110 [PDF p. 16]
- **Analysis:** Believing Gentiles share equal access to the spiritual blessings of the New Covenant (forgiveness of sins, indwelling Spirit). However, specific physical and land promises (e.g., regathering to the land of Palestine, national deliverance in Jer 30:7 and Ezek 37:25) remain reserved for the believing remnant of ethnic Israel at Christ's return (Tanner 2005:106, 110).
