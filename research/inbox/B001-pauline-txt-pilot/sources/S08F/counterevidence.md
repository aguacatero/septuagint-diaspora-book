## 2. B001-2 (Romans 9:25 / Hosea 2:23) & B001-3 (Romans 9:26 / Hosea 1:10)

### Expresses Uncertainty
1. **Chronology of the "Mystery" Revelation:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 103 [PDF p. 9], fn. 11
   - **Details:** Tanner expresses uncertainty regarding the precise date when Paul received the revelation of the mystery of the church: "Probably Paul had received this revelation before then, but how much earlier is difficult to say."
2. **Early Church Awareness Before Acts 15:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 104 [PDF p. 10]
   - **Details:** Tanner notes the historical uncertainty regarding whether early believers fully grasped Gentile inclusion prior to the Jerusalem Council: "An interesting question is whether this truth was even understood in the church before the Jerusalem Council of A.D. 49 (Acts 15)... Yet Acts 15 does not reflect this..."
3. **Exact Genesis of Paul's Hosea Insight:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 104 [PDF p. 10]
   - **Details:** Tanner acknowledges that the exact timing of Paul's insight into Hosea 1–2 cannot be determined with complete certainty: "It may be that this understanding was contemporaneous with the revelation of the mystery mentioned in Ephesians 3. On the other hand several key developments would have at least paved the way..."

### Competing Textual Forms & Pauline Textual Adjustments
1. **Reversal of Clause Order in Hosea 2:23:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 99 [PDF p. 5]
   - **Details:** Tanner observes that Paul inverted the clause order of Hosea 2:23 (placing "My people" before "Beloved") "perhaps to stress the 'My people' aspect of the promise and God's 'call' to them."
2. **Verb Substitution (*καλέσω* vs. MT/LXX):**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 99 [PDF p. 5], fn. 4
   - **Details:** Tanner (citing Douglas Moo) notes that Paul altered Hosea's verb "I will say" (MT *וְאָמַרְתִּי* / LXX *ἐρῶ*) to "I will call" (*καλέσω*), aligning with his preceding emphasis on divine election in Romans 9:24 (*οὓς καὶ ἐκάλεσεν*).
3. **Selective Truncation (Omission of Hosea 1:10a):**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 99 [PDF p. 5]
   - **Details:** In Romans 9:26, Paul quotes only Hosea 1:10b ("there they shall be called sons of the living God"), omitting Hosea 1:10a (the Abrahamic sand-of-the-sea promise of national restoration).
4. **Reliance on Greek Vorlage:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 99 [PDF p. 5]
   - **Details:** Tanner affirms that Paul cited from a Greek rendering of Hosea rather than producing an ad hoc translation from the Hebrew MT.

### Interpretive Nuances & Contextual Tensions
1. **Original Historical Target Exclusively Northern Israel:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed pp. 99–100 [PDF pp. 5–6]
   - **Details:** Tanner highlights that Hosea's 8th-century B.C. prophecy referred strictly to the northern kingdom of Israel: "Nothing in the context of these passages makes reference to Gentiles, nor did Hosea imply that the fulfillment of these promises would be with Gentiles."
2. **Hermeneutical Tension of Application:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 101 [PDF p. 7]
   - **Details:** Tanner frames the exegetical challenge: "His point in Romans 9 was not to deny a fulfillment with Israel but only to affirm a fulfillment also with Gentiles. However, this raises the question, How can the Hosea verses also be true of Gentile believers in the church?"
3. **Absence of Overt Gentile Reference in Old Testament New Covenant Passages:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 105 [PDF p. 11]
   - **Details:** Tanner notes that "a first reading of the passages from the Old Testament about the New Covenant would not have given the impression that Gentiles were to be included."

### Disagreements with Other Scholars
1. **Disputes Alva J. McClain (1973), John A. Battle, Jr. (1981), & David L. Turner (1985):**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 98 [PDF p. 4], fn. 2, 3
   - **Details:** Tanner rejects the position that Romans 9:25–26 refers exclusively to national Israel: "Any attempt to argue that the Hosea quotations were used by Paul in Romans to argue for the inclusion of all ethnic Israelites must certainly be rejected" (p. 98, fn. 2 for McClain and Battle; fn. 3 for Turner).
2. **Disputes George Eldon Ladd (1977) & Douglas J. Moo (1996) on Replacement Theology:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed pp. 100–101 [PDF pp. 6–7], fn. 6, 8; p. 103 [PDF p. 9]; p. 109 [PDF p. 15], fn. 22; p. 110 [PDF p. 16]
   - **Details:** Tanner rejects Ladd's claim that the NT identifies the church as "spiritual Israel" (p. 109, fn. 22) and Moo's premise that OT predictions of renewed Israel are fulfilled replacement-style in the church (p. 101, fn. 6), emphasizing that Paul maintains a consistent distinction between ethnic Israel and Gentiles throughout Romans 9–11.
3. **Disputes Robert B. Chisholm Jr. (1985), John A. Witmer (1983), & S. Lewis Johnson (1992) on Mere Analogy:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed pp. 101–102 [PDF pp. 7–8], fn. 8
   - **Details:** Tanner rejects treating Paul's citation as a mere analogy or general principle (Chisholm and Witmer on pp. 101–102, fn. 8; Johnson on p. 102, fn. 8), agreeing with Moo (p. 102, fn. 8) that Paul required direct scriptural proof.
4. **Disputes Richard Daniels (1999) on Non-Participating Mediation:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 104 [PDF p. 10], fn. 13
   - **Details:** Tanner disputes Daniels's thesis in *Faith and Mission* that the church is merely a "nonparticipating mediator" of the New Covenant, arguing instead that Gentiles are full participants in its spiritual blessings.
5. **Disputes Leon J. Wood (1985) & Francis I. Andersen & David Noel Freedman (1980):**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 107 [PDF p. 13], fn. 18
   - **Details:** Tanner criticizes Wood for ignoring the mention of "covenant" in Hosea 2:18 and Andersen/Freedman for failing to identify it, following Hans Walter Wolff (also in fn. 18) in recognizing Hosea 2:18 as an end-time New Covenant reference.

### Weakening Evidence Acknowledged by Author
1. **Silence of the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15):**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 104 [PDF p. 10]
   - **Details:** Tanner acknowledges that during the Jerusalem Council debate over Gentile salvation, the leaders did not cite Hosea 1–2 or articulate the New Covenant hermeneutic.
2. **Unfulfilled National & Land Promises:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 101 [PDF p. 7], fn. 7; p. 106 [PDF p. 12], fn. 16; p. 110 [PDF p. 16]
   - **Details:** Tanner acknowledges that major Old Testament New Covenant passages contain explicit physical and land promises (e.g., Jer 30:7; Zech 12:3; Ezek 37:25) that cannot legitimately be fulfilled by the church, requiring a future climactic fulfillment with ethnic Israel.
3. **Indefinite Terminology in Hosea 2:18:**
   - **Source Locator:** Printed p. 106 [PDF p. 12]
   - **Details:** Tanner acknowledges that Hosea 2:18 uses the indefinite expression "a covenant" rather than explicitly stating "new covenant."
