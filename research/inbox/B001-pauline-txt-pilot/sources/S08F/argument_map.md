# S08F — Argument Map

**Source:** J. Paul Tanner, *The New Covenant and Paul’s Quotations from Hosea in Romans 9:25–26*, *Bibliotheca Sacra*, Vol. 162, No. 645 (January–March 2005), pp. 95–110.

---

## 1. Core Reasoning Chain: Romans 9:25–26 / Hosea 2:23 & 1:10 (B001-2 & B001-3)

### QUESTION
- **Explicit Source Statement:** How does Paul's use of Hosea 1:10 and 2:23 in Romans 9:25–26 contribute to understanding Israel and what it means to be "the people of God"? More pointedly, was Paul using these verses with the same meaning as the prophet, or was he violating their original historical sense by offering them as proof that Gentiles become "My people"? (Tanner 2005:95, 98).

### EVIDENCE
1. **Original Hosea Context:** In Hosea 1:10b and 2:23, the original 8th-century B.C. historical context concerned the northern kingdom of Israel being disowned ("not My people") and subsequently restored to divine mercy, with no explicit reference to Gentiles (Tanner 2005:99–100).
2. **Textual Adaptation:** In Romans 9:25–26, Paul quotes Hosea 2:23 and 1:10b from a Greek rendering, reversing the clause order in Hosea 2:23 and changing "I will say" to "I will call" (*καλέσω*) to match his preceding emphasis on divine election in Romans 9:24 (Tanner 2005:99, fn. 4).
3. **Contextual Framing in Romans:** In Romans 9:24–29, the Old Testament quotations are structurally bracketed by verses 22–24 and 30–31, where verse 24 explicitly states that God called "vessels of mercy" not from among Jews only, but also from among Gentiles (Tanner 2005:97–98).
4. **Tanner's Appeal to Early Church Narrative (Acts 10–11):** In Tanner's theological framework, the narrative of Acts 10–11 is invoked to argue that the early church witnessed Gentiles receiving the Holy Spirit without becoming Jewish proselytes, which Tanner uses as an explanatory historical-theological premise (Tanner 2005:104–105).
5. **New Covenant Prophetic Baseline:** Old Testament New Covenant prophecies (Jeremiah 31:31–34; Ezekiel 36:24–28; Hosea 2:18) define "My people" as those who participate in the New Covenant through the indwelling Spirit and forgiveness of sins (Tanner 2005:106–108).

### INFERENCE
1. **Participation via New Covenant (Tanner's Theological Construction):** Under Tanner's reading, participation in the New Covenant is what conveys spiritual status as "the people of God." Tanner infers that early church events and the Pauline revelation of the "mystery" (Eph 3:3) led the apostles to realize that believing Gentiles share fully in New Covenant blessings, making the Hosea status change ("not My people" to "My people") legitimately applicable to believing Gentiles as well as the believing Jewish remnant (Tanner 2005:106–110).
2. **Progressive Breadth of Fulfillment:** Paul does not deny or cancel the fulfillment of Hosea's promises for ethnic Israel; rather, he recognizes that the divinely intended fulfillment is broader than was apparent in 8th-century B.C. prophecy and encompasses Gentiles as an added, legitimate phase of fulfillment (Tanner 2005:102–103).

### CONCLUSION
- **Explicit Source Statement:** Paul's application of Hosea 2:23 and 1:10 to Gentile believers in Romans 9:25–26 is a direct, partial phase of fulfillment of Hosea's prophecy through the New Covenant, establishing believing Gentiles as full "people of God" alongside the believing remnant of Israel without identifying the church as "spiritual Israel" or negating future national promises reserved for ethnic Israel (Tanner 2005:102, 110).

## 2. Analytical Breakdown

### Assumptions the Author Explicitly Acknowledges
1. **"True Israel" as Believing Jewish Remnant:** Tanner explicitly assumes that "true Israel" in Romans 9:6 refers strictly to the believing remnant within ethnic Israel, not a composite entity of Jews and Gentiles (Tanner 2005:95–96).
2. **Covenant Promises to Ethnic Israel Stand:** He explicitly assumes that God's covenant promises to ethnic Israel have not failed and will be fulfilled with the believing remnant of Israel at Christ's return (Tanner 2005:96, 105).
3. **Narrative and Revelatory Baseline as Theological Premises:** Tanner assumes the historical reliability and chronological utility of the Acts 10–11 narrative and the early revelatory date of Ephesians (Eph 3:3) as foundational premises of his theological reconstruction. In keeping with the project constitution, these are cataloged as Tanner's secondary interpretive premises, not as independently audited historical certainties (Tanner 2005:104–105).
4. **New Covenant Reference in Hosea 2:18:** He explicitly assumes that Hosea 2:18 ("I will also make a covenant for them") refers directly to the New Covenant promised in Jeremiah 31 and Ezekiel 36 (Tanner 2005:106–107).

### Alternative Explanations the Author Considers and Resolves
1. **Replacement Theology / Supersessionism (Church = "Spiritual Israel"):** Considered and rejected (citing Ladd, Moo). Tanner argues this position fails because Paul consistently distinguishes Israel from the church throughout Romans 9–11 and because specific Old Testament prophecies (e.g., Jer 30:7; Zech 12:3) cannot legitimately be fulfilled by the church (Tanner 2005:95–96, 101, 103, 110).
2. **Mere Analogy or General Principle:** Considered and rejected (citing Chisholm, Witmer, S. Lewis Johnson). Tanner agrees with Douglas Moo that an analogy is hermeneutically inadequate because Paul requires direct scriptural proof for God's calling of Gentiles (Tanner 2005:101–102).
3. **Reference Restricted to National Israel Only:** Considered and rejected (citing McClain, Battle, Turner). Tanner argues this view fails because the bracketed context of Romans 9:22–24 and 9:30–31 demands that the Hosea citations serve as proof for Gentile inclusion (Tanner 2005:98).
4. **Sensus Plenior / Heightening:** Discussed via Roy B. Zuck. Tanner notes that NT writers extended OT passages to fill them with more meaning (*references plenior*), but Tanner prefers framing Gentile inclusion as an added, divinely intended phase of New Covenant fulfillment rather than an arbitrary new meaning (Tanner 2005:103).

### Unaddressed Alternative Hypotheses
1. **Pre-Pauline Textual Variant:** Tanner does not address whether Paul's reversal of clauses in Hosea 2:23 and substitution of *καλέσω* ("I will call") was derived from an existing, unrecorded Hellenistic Jewish Greek manuscript variant rather than being Paul's own deliberate theological modification.
2. **Typological Lo-Ammi Symmetry:** Tanner does not address whether Hosea 1:10b was intended in its 8th-century context as an implicit universalistic typology where Israel's temporary "Gentile-like" pagan status (*Lo-ammi*) made their restoration structurally identical to Gentile conversion.

### Potential Falsification Criteria
1. **Total Exclusion of Gentiles from Hosea:** Tanner notes that any attempt to argue that the Hosea quotations were used by Paul to argue for the inclusion of all ethnic Israelites "must certainly be rejected" because it violates the structural context of Romans 9:24 and 9:30–31 (Tanner 2005:98).
2. **Gentile Non-Participation in New Covenant:** If it could be demonstrated from Scripture that Gentiles do not participate in the New Covenant (as argued by Richard Daniels, whom Tanner explicitly disputes in fn. 13), Tanner's core mechanism for Gentile status change ("not My people" to "My people") would be falsified (Tanner 2005:104, 106–110).
