# Narrative Causal Ladder

Use the least aggressive classification that the evidence supports.

1. **Scriptural coloring** — biblical wording or imagery overlays an otherwise independently intelligible narrative.
2. **Scriptural shaping** — Scripture materially determines how an inherited event or tradition is narrated.
3. **Co-construction** — some tradition may predate the evangelist, while Scripture generates important narrative particulars.
4. **Probable narrative generation** — the episode or its defining particulars are better explained as arising from the scriptural text than from an independently evidenced event.

## Moving up the ladder

Stronger classifications should require convergence among several features:

- a distinctive textual hook rather than generic scriptural language;
- multiple specific correspondences;
- meaningful order or structural correspondence;
- demonstrable use of the relevant scriptural text by the author;
- weak or absent independent attestation for the generated detail;
- explanatory superiority over the model of an event merely being described biblically;
- source-dependence controls so copied details are not counted as independent confirmation.

## Limitation

Even **probable narrative generation** does not automatically establish that no underlying person, event, or tradition existed. Historical consequences are assessed separately in HIS.
