# Cultural Comparison Controls

Every proposed Hellenistic or Greco-Roman comparison must answer all of the following.

1. **Chronology** — Is the proposed comparandum securely earlier than or plausibly available during composition?
2. **Accessibility** — Is there a plausible cultural or literary route by which the author or community could encounter the motif/form?
3. **Specificity** — Are the correspondences distinctive rather than generic features such as miraculous births, healings, divine favor, or death?
4. **Density** — Are multiple meaningful features shared?
5. **Order / structure** — Do corresponding elements occur in a meaningful sequence or literary structure?
6. **Jewish-scriptural control** — Does Jewish Scripture explain the same feature more directly?
7. **Genre control** — Is the feature simply common to ancient biography, historiography, encomium, wonder tales, or another broad genre?
8. **Differences** — What major features do not match, and do those differences weaken a dependence claim?
9. **Transmission claim** — What mechanism is actually being proposed: direct literary dependence, probable cultural influence, shared convention, or only resemblance?

## Classification rule

- Use **Direct dependence** only when a specific source/form plus plausible transmission and distinctive structural evidence make direct use the best explanation.
- Use **Probable cultural influence** when a source-specific claim cannot be established but a distinctive cluster plus historical accessibility makes influence likely.
- Use **Shared Mediterranean convention** when the Gospel and comparanda plausibly participate in the same cultural repertoire without evidence of direct borrowing.
- Use **Weak parallel** when resemblance is too generic, late, inaccessible, sparse, or structurally weak to carry explanatory weight.

Weak and rejected parallels must not be used cumulatively as if many weak parallels become one strong argument.
