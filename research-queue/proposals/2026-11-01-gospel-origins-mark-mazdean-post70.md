## Investigation B — John 1:1–18 and Mazdean comparanda (MAZ-JOHN-01)

**Barnea's actual thesis (source-derived, pending independent scholarly review):** The Johannine Prologue might assimilate a network of Mazdean concepts through a shared late-first/early-second-century eastern Mediterranean religious environment and a process termed *interpretatio Iohannica*, rather than directly copying Avestan scripture or developing from fully formed Roman Mithraism. Proposed comparanda include Miϑra, mąϑra/divine utterance, tanu.mąϑra (“incarnate utterance”), aša/truth, the eschatological final saošiiaṇt, renewed creation and resurrection. The article discusses Judea/Anatolia and an early Mithraeum at Caesarea (approximately 80 CE) and treats John's final form roughly 100–125 CE; its reasoning is explicitly **cumulative rather than demonstrative**.

**Questions:**
1. Collate John 1:1–18 (including key variant witnesses) with Gen 1 LXX, Jewish Wisdom traditions, Philo, Jewish Memra/Aramaic traditions, and Johannine new-creation themes. Distinguish lexical resemblance from structural/functional parallel.
2. Establish independent dating and transmission histories of the relevant Avestan materials, later Iranian interpretations, Roman Miϑra/Mithras cult evidence, and Jewish/Greco-Roman intermediary traditions. Do not project late manuscript attestations automatically into the first century or conflate Iranian Miϑra with Roman Mithraism.
3. Test contact routes and actual accessibility in Judea/Anatolia: archaeology, languages, communities, geography, earliest securely dated witnesses, and potential mediators.
4. Create a full comparative passage table for Logos, light/darkness, truth, incarnation, creation/restoration, savior and resurrection motifs; record substantial differences, not just matches.
5. Test at least four rival accounts: (i) Jewish scriptural/Wisdom/Philo derivation; (ii) independent Jewish-Christian theological innovation; (iii) diffuse/shared eastern Mediterranean convention; (iv) specific Mazdean conceptual influence. Consider composite accounts.
6. Explain whether this proposed cultural layer clarifies the **literary portrayal** of Jesus without assuming that dependence proves Jesus nonhistorical.

**Deliverable:** critical source bibliography; date-and-provenance table; primary-text concordance; motif/structure matrix; transmission plausibility chart; adversarial comparative review; qualified CUL and NAR candidate entries. Do **not** label direct borrowing absent positive evidence.

## Investigation C — Reopen the Pauline authenticity labels

Current project uses the conventional seven “undisputed Pauline letters” as an operational corpus. That phrase describes a **scholarly consensus category**, not mathematical certainty. The owner requests an adversarial re-examination of all seven, without assuming they are forged or late.

**Hypotheses to compare:**
- H1 (conventional baseline): authentic Pauline letters composed in the mid-first century, prior to 70 CE, with later manuscript transmission and possible interpolation.
- H2: individually mixed authentic core, editorial/interpolation, composite, and later redaction with distinguishable layers.
- H3: some or all surviving letters are substantially post-70 compositions or pseudepigrapha.
- H4: a post-70 edited letter collection preserves some earlier Pauline material.

**Tests:** chronology of manuscript evidence versus composition dates; external attestations (including 1 Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Marcion, and the reception chronology, critically dated); language, style and stylometry with corpus-size and genre controls; coherence of Paul's biography and social networks; temple/cult references and post-70 implications (e.g., Romans 9–11, 1 Corinthians, Galatians); independently dated political and historical allusions; theological trajectories; text-critical interpolation candidates; source-dependence and circularity traps. Distinguish “letter composed,” “tradition originated,” “edited into extant form,” and “earliest surviving manuscript.”

**Falsification:** Strong pre-70 interlocking evidence or demonstrated earlier independent quotation weighs against comprehensive post-70 composition; late manuscript survival **does not by itself date composition**; silence about 70 CE is not automatically evidence for either chronology. Treat stylistic anomaly cautiously in tiny ancient corpora.

**Deliverable:** seven-letter-by-seven-letter authenticity/dating dossier with explicit pre-70, post-70, layered, and unresolved verdicts; independent methodological review **before** any proposal to rename the canonical primary corpus.

## Investigation D — Corpus-wide post-70 composition hypothesis

**Owner hypothesis, not current finding:** All extant canonical Gospels and Pauline letters took shape after the Temple's fall in 70 CE. Use a tiered model separating earlier traditions from first complete text, redaction, and final canonical form.

**Comparative timeline hypotheses:** (A) conventional Paul pre-70, Mark circa 65–75, Matthew/Luke later, John late-first/early-second century; (B) universal original post-70 compositions; (C) mixed pre-70 source cores and post-70 definitive editions. Do not make Mark 13 a circular proof of post-event composition; test predictive prophecy, retrospective allusion, and later redaction separately.

**Evidence:** Mark 13 and parallels; temple destruction/sanctuary cues and cult references; Roman-Judean War narratives; James, Peter, Paul, and Jerusalem-community traditions; internal literary dependence; Luke–Acts dating and Pauline reception; Josephus and other external witnesses; earliest securely dated papyri, codices, patristic citations, and securely dated inscriptions. Distinguish termini post/ante quem from conventional estimates. Explicitly model the probability of surviving evidence and catalog genuine counterexamples.

**Deliverable:** reproducible chronology table with independent evidence IDs, source-specific date ranges, alternatives, and uncertainty; argument-dependency graph (avoid using Mark's date to prove Paul's date and vice versa); specialist adversarial assessment.
