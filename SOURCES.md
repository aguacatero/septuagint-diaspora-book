# Supporting Books & Sources for "Shaped by the Septuagint"

This repo is a preliminary work-in-progress for a peer-review-grade book arguing that the New Testament (especially Mark, Matthew, Luke, and John) relies primarily on the Greek Septuagint (LXX) rather than direct Hebrew textual knowledge — within the context of a Greek-speaking Jewish diaspora with limited Hebrew literacy, with speculative components (mythic parallels) clearly marked.

---

## I. Septuagint (LXX) & Its Role in Early Christianity

1. **The Septuagint: Greek Scriptures for Greek-Speaking Jews and Christians (Oxford)** — General reference; LXX produced by Hellenistic Jews, became authoritative for NT authors. ([Bible Interp summary](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/articles/2005/03/din298001))
2. **The Oxford Handbook of the Septuagint** (Oxford University Press, edited volume; ISBN 978-0-19-872432-6) — Surveys current LXX research; states LXX was "version of Scripture most familiar to writers of New Testament." ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34470))
3. **Edmon Gallagher, *Paul and the Septuagint Canon*** (and related essays) — Proof that the OG/LXX text was cited in the NT in contrast to Hebrew Scriptures; argues Greek Jewish Scriptures were deemed Scripture for the Early Church. ([textandcanon.org](https://textandcanon.org/paul-and-the-septuagint-canon/))
4. **Albert Pietersma & Benjamin G. Wright, *A New English Translation of the Septuagint*** (Oxford University Press) — Critical translation; demonstrates LXX reading traditions underlying NT citations.
5. **Salvesen & Law, eds., *The Oxford Handbook of the Septuagint*** (Oxford University Press, 2021) — 776-page comprehensive survey; includes LXX in Early Judaism and Christianity, Paul as witness to LXX text.

## II. Paul’s Use of the Septuagint (Specific Passages in the Book)

6. **Florian Wilk / Paul and Septuagint research** — Letters of Paul as witnesses to LXX text (cited in SBL Cart reference, 2024–2025).
7. **Paul’s Epistles (1 Cor 15:55, Gal 3:13, Rom 9:33, Rom 10:15, Rom 11:9)** — The book’s Chapter 1 and 3 build on Paul’s quotations of Deut 21:23, Isa 28:16, Hosea 2:23, Psalm 69:22–23, Isa 52:7, which all align with LXX rather than MT.
8. **Gallagher / Edmon Gallagher (2024)** — Confirms Paul treats LXX form as authoritative Scripture; not a secondary gloss but the biblical text he builds theology on.

## IV. Jewish Diaspora & Language / Literacy Context

12. **Tessa Rajak, *Translation and Survival: The Greek Bible of the Ancient Jewish Diaspora*** (Oxford University Press, 2009) — Direct source cited in Septuagint Wikipedia entry; describes LXX as Bible of diaspora Jews.
13. **Sage Journals — "A Split Jewish Diaspora: Its Dram� Consequences"** (Journal of Intercultural Studies / SAGE) — Documents language divide between western Jewish diaspora and Hebrew-speaking Judea; supports thesis that diaspora communities operated in Greek.
14. **Academia.edu — "Ancient Jewish Greek Practices of Reading and Their Material Aspects"** — Evidence for continuous Jewish tradition of using Greek translations in synagogal contexts; supports LXX as primary scriptural vehicle.
15. **The Making of Diaspora / Jewish Book Council** — Historical context of diaspora concept; links to Septuagint as first use of term.

---

## V. Early Christian Evidence & Non-Christian References (Chapter 4 / Chapter 6 of Book)

16. **Pliny the Younger — Letter to Trajan (c. 112 CE)** — Primary source: describes Christian worship of Christus, hymn singing in Greek, refusal of Roman gods; shows Greek-speaking Christian communities without Hebrew liturgical forms.
17. **Ignatius of Antioch — Epistles (c. 110 CE)** — Early 2nd-century Christian leader; letters contain LXX language (e.g., "the Word was made flesh"); addresses Greek-speaking communities across Asia Minor.
18. **Tacitus — Annals 15.44 (c. 115 CE)** — References Christus executed by Pilate; reflects Greek-speaking Roman awareness of Christian LXX-based movement.
19. **Josephus — *Antiquities of the Jews* / Testimonium Flavianum** — Discussed extensively with arguments for interpolation; the book argues Josephus reflects Christian LXX-shaped memory rather than independent Hebrew-based testimony.
20. **Acts of the Apostles** — Written in Greek; depicts diaspora Jewish communities (Hellenists) reading Scripture in Greek synagogues; supports LXX framework.
21. **Didache / Shepherd of Hermas (c. late 1st–2nd cent)** — LXX-based Eucharist prayers and vision language.
22. **Papyrus 46 / Chester Beatty (Pauline letters)** — Earliest complete Pauline manuscript; contains LXX citations supporting LXX dependence.

---

## VI. Mythic / Hellenistic Parallel Literature (Speculative Chapter — Marked in Book)

23. **Greco-Egyptian myth studies** — Osiris / Adonis / Attis (dying-rising gods); Asclepius / Perseus (miracle births); Heracles; Ceres / Demeter (feeding miracles). The book uses these as comparative templates — explicitly marked as speculative / to-test.
24. **Philo of Alexandria writings** — Jewish-Hellenistic philosopher; relies almost exclusively on LXX; provides model for how diaspora Jews used Greek Scripture rather than Hebrew.
25. **Mystery cult literature (Eleusinian, Mithraic)** — Ritual parallels to early Christian liturgy; cited to show Hellenistic cultural embedding.

---

## VII. Primary Source Collections / Manuscript Evidence

26. **Göttingen Septuagint (critical edition)** — Used for methodological comparison with Masoretic Text; supports systematic LXX-vs-MT analysis proposed in book’s Chapter 8.
27. **BHS (Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia)** — Standard Hebrew text for comparison with LXX.
28. **Dead Sea Scrolls (4QDeut-q, 4QSam)** — Include LXX-affinity fragments; show LXX used alongside Hebrew texts in some contexts.
29. **Dura-Europos synagogue inscriptions** — Predominantly Greek; minimal Hebrew; archaeological corroboration of diaspora language practice.
30. **Papyrus 2 / Nag Hammadi library** — Early Christian texts with LXX vocabulary; supports LXX-only manuscript predictions.
